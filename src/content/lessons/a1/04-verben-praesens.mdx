In chapter 2 you used four verbs with four persons. Now you get the complete system. Once you know the six endings, you can use hundreds of German verbs, because the great majority are regular.

## The idea

A German verb has two parts: a **stem**, which carries the meaning, and an **ending**, which shows who is doing it.

Take *lernen* (to learn). Remove **-en** and you have the stem **lern-**. Each person then adds its own ending: ich lern**e**, du lern**st**, er lern**t**.

Because the ending already tells you the person, the endings are not decoration. If you get them wrong, a German listener has to stop and work out who you mean.

## Step by step

<Step n="1" title="All the persons">

| Singular | | Plural | |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **ich** | I | **wir** | we |
| **du** | you (one person, informal) | **ihr** | you (several people, informal) |
| **er** | he | **sie** | they |
| **sie** | she | **Sie** | you (formal, one or several) |
| **es** | it | | |

German has three words for English *you*: **du** for one friend, **ihr** for several friends, **Sie** for anyone you address formally.

The word **sie** has three jobs: *she*, *they*, and with a capital letter, formal *you*. The verb ending tells you which one is meant.

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="The six endings">

| Person | Ending | lernen (to learn) | machen (to do, make) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ich | **-e** | lern**e** | mach**e** |
| du | **-st** | lern**st** | mach**st** |
| er / sie / es | **-t** | lern**t** | mach**t** |
| wir | **-en** | lern**en** | mach**en** |
| ihr | **-t** | lern**t** | mach**t** |
| sie / Sie | **-en** | lern**en** | mach**en** |

Only four different endings do all the work: **-e, -st, -t, -en**. The *wir* form and the *sie / Sie* form are always identical to the infinitive.

<Example de="Ich lerne Deutsch." en="I am learning German." />
<Example de="Du lernst schnell." en="You learn quickly." />
<Example de="Sie lernt Englisch." en="She is learning English." />
<Example de="Wir lernen zusammen." en="We learn together." />
<Example de="Ihr lernt viel." en="You (all) learn a lot." />
<Example de="Sie lernen Spanisch." en="They are learning Spanish." />

</Step>

<Step n="3" title="Telling sie from sie from Sie">

<Example de="Sie **lernt** Deutsch." en="She is learning German. (ending -t: one person)" />
<Example de="Sie **lernen** Deutsch." en="They are learning German. Or: You are learning German." />

With the ending **-t**, *sie* can only be *she*. With **-en** it is either *they* or the formal *you*; in writing the capital S decides, in speech the situation does.

</Step>

<Step n="4" title="Stems ending in -t or -d">

Try to say *du arbeitst*. The consonants pile up. German solves this by slipping in an **e** before *-st* and *-t*.

| | arbeiten (to work) | finden (to find) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ich | arbeite | finde |
| du | arbeit**e**st | find**e**st |
| er / sie / es | arbeit**e**t | find**e**t |
| wir | arbeiten | finden |
| ihr | arbeit**e**t | find**e**t |
| sie / Sie | arbeiten | finden |

<Example de="Er arbeitet in Berlin." en="He works in Berlin." />
<Example de="Wo arbeitest du?" en="Where do you work?" />

Other common verbs of this kind: *warten* (to wait), *kosten* (to cost), *antworten* (to answer), *reden* (to talk).

</Step>

<Step n="5" title="Stems ending in an s-sound">

If the stem ends in **-s, -ß, -z or -x**, the *du* form adds only **-t**, because the s is already there. You saw this with *heißen*.

| Infinitive | du | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| heißen | du heiß**t** | you are called |
| tanzen | du tanz**t** | you dance |
| reisen | du reis**t** | you travel |
| sitzen | du sitz**t** | you sit |

<Example de="Du tanzt sehr gut." en="You dance very well." />
<Example de="Reist du gern?" en="Do you like travelling?" />

</Step>

<Step n="6" title="du, ihr or Sie?">

| Use | With |
| --- | --- |
| **du** | one friend, a family member, a child, a fellow student |
| **ihr** | two or more of those |
| **Sie** | adults you do not know, shop staff, officials, your doctor, your boss until they offer *du* |

*Sie* is the same whether you speak to one person or to several. The older person, or the one of higher rank, is the one who offers *du*. In the exam you say **Sie** to the examiners and **du** or **Sie** to the other candidates; both are accepted, but be consistent.

<Example de="Woher kommst du, Anna?" en="Where are you from, Anna?" />
<Example de="Woher kommt ihr, Anna und Tom?" en="Where are you two from, Anna and Tom?" />
<Example de="Woher kommen Sie, Frau Weber?" en="Where are you from, Mrs Weber?" />

</Step>

<Step n="7" title="One tense, three meanings">

The German present tense covers what English needs three forms for.

<Example de="Ich lerne Deutsch." en="I learn German. / I am learning German. / I do learn German." />

It also covers the near future, as long as a time word makes that clear:

<Example de="Morgen arbeite ich nicht." en="Tomorrow I am not working." />

</Step>

## Compared with English

<Compare en="I learn, you learn, we learn, they learn" de="ich lern**e**, du lern**st**, wir lern**en**, sie lern**en**" note="English changes the verb only for he, she and it. German changes it for every person." />

<Compare en="you (one word)" de="**du**, **ihr**, **Sie**" note="Before you can say 'you' in German, you have to decide: one person or several, friend or stranger." />

<Compare en="Are you working? Do you work?" de="Arbeitest du?" note="German has no 'do' and no '-ing' form. The one verb form does both jobs." />

<Compare en="She works. They work." de="Sie arbeit**et**. Sie arbeit**en**." note="English tells she from they by the pronoun. German uses the same pronoun and lets the verb ending decide." />

## Typical mistakes

<Mistake wrong="Er **lerne** Deutsch." right="Er **lernt** Deutsch." why="-e belongs to ich. He, she and it take -t." />

<Mistake wrong="Du **arbeitst** viel." right="Du **arbeitest** viel." why="A stem ending in -t or -d needs an extra e before -st and -t." />

<Mistake wrong="Ich **bin lernen** Deutsch." right="Ich **lerne** Deutsch." why="German has no '-ing' form with 'to be'. The simple present says it all." />

<Mistake wrong="Wo **wohnt** ihr? Wir **wohnt** in Köln." right="Wo wohnt ihr? Wir **wohnen** in Köln." why="ihr takes -t, but wir takes -en, the same as the infinitive." />

<Mistake wrong="Du **tanzst** gut." right="Du **tanzt** gut." why="After an s-sound in the stem, du adds only -t." />

## Summary

<Summary>

| Person | Ending | Example | Stem in -t / -d |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ich | -e | ich mache | ich arbeite |
| du | -st | du machst | du arbeit**e**st |
| er / sie / es | -t | er macht | er arbeit**e**t |
| wir | -en | wir machen | wir arbeiten |
| ihr | -t | ihr macht | ihr arbeit**e**t |
| sie / Sie | -en | sie machen | sie arbeiten |

**du** = one friend · **ihr** = several friends · **Sie** = formal, one or many.

**Stem ends in s, ß, z, x:** du adds only -t (*du heißt, du tanzt*).

</Summary>
