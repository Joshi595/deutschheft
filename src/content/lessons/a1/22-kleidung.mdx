Shopping for clothes brings together several things you already know (numbers, prices, the accusative, modal verbs) and adds two new ones: a verb that works backwards compared with English, and two small words for pointing at things.

## The idea

English says *I like the jacket*: the person is the subject. German has a very common verb, **gefallen**, that turns this around. The **thing** is the subject, and the person is added in the dative: *Die Jacke gefällt **mir***, literally "the jacket pleases me".

Because the thing is the subject, the verb agrees with the thing. One jacket **gefällt**; two shoes **gefallen**.

The person needs a dative pronoun: **mir** (to me), **dir** (to you), **ihm** (to him), **ihr** (to her). You have used two of them since the first chapter: *Wie geht es **dir**? Wie geht es **Ihnen**?* Now you get the whole set.

## Step by step

<Step n="1" title="Clothes">

| der | die | das |
| --- | --- | --- |
| der Pullover (jumper) | die Hose (trousers) | das Hemd (shirt) |
| der Mantel (coat) | die Jacke (jacket) | das Kleid (dress) |
| der Rock (skirt) | die Bluse (blouse) | das T-Shirt |
| der Schuh (shoe) | die Jeans (jeans) | |
| der Hut (hat) | die Mütze (cap) | |
| der Schal (scarf) | die Socke (sock) | |

*die Hose* and *die Jeans* are singular in German: *Die Hose **ist** neu.* Shoes and socks usually come in the plural: *die Schuhe, die Socken*.

<Example de="der Pullover, der Mantel, der Rock." en="jumper, coat, skirt" />
<Example de="die Hose, die Jacke, die Bluse." en="trousers, jacket, blouse" />
<Example de="das Hemd, das Kleid, die Schuhe." en="shirt, dress, shoes" />

To say what someone has on, use **tragen** (er trägt) or **anhaben**:

<Example de="Sie trägt heute ein Kleid." en="She is wearing a dress today." />
<Example de="Er hat einen Mantel an." en="He has a coat on." />

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="Colours">

| | | | |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| rot (red) | blau (blue) | grün (green) | gelb (yellow) |
| schwarz (black) | weiß (white) | grau (grey) | braun (brown) |
| orange | rosa (pink) | lila (purple) | bunt (colourful) |

Add **hell-** for light and **dunkel-** for dark: *hellblau, dunkelgrün*.

After *sein*, a colour takes no ending:

<Example de="Die Jacke ist **blau**." en="The jacket is blue." />
<Example de="Die Schuhe sind **schwarz**." en="The shoes are black." />
<Example de="Welche Farbe hat das Hemd? Es ist **weiß**." en="What colour is the shirt? It is white." />

In front of a noun, colours and other adjectives take endings (*eine blau**e** Jacke*). That is a topic for A2. Until then you can always say it with *sein*, or with **in**: eine Jacke **in Blau**.

</Step>

<Step n="3" title="gefallen">

| Thing (subject) | Verb | Person (dative) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Die Jacke | **gefällt** | mir. |
| Das Kleid | **gefällt** | ihr. |
| Die Schuhe | **gefallen** | mir nicht. |

<Example de="Die Jacke **gefällt** mir." en="I like the jacket." />
<Example de="Die Schuhe **gefallen** mir nicht." en="I do not like the shoes." />
<Example de="**Gefällt** dir der Pullover?" en="Do you like the jumper?" />
<Example de="Wie **gefällt** Ihnen das Hemd?" en="How do you like the shirt?" />

*gefallen* is a vowel-changing verb: *es gefällt*. Only two forms matter in practice: **gefällt** for one thing, **gefallen** for several.

</Step>

<Step n="4" title="The dative pronouns">

| Nominative | Dative | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ich | **mir** | to me |
| du | **dir** | to you |
| er / es | **ihm** | to him, to it |
| sie | **ihr** | to her |
| wir | **uns** | to us |
| ihr | **euch** | to you |
| sie | **ihnen** | to them |
| Sie | **Ihnen** | to you (formal) |

<Example de="Das Kleid gefällt **ihr**." en="She likes the dress." />
<Example de="Der Mantel gefällt **ihm** nicht." en="He does not like the coat." />
<Example de="Gefällt **euch** die Wohnung?" en="Do you like the flat?" />

Two more verbs work the same way and are useful in a clothes shop:

| Verb | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **passen** | to fit | Die Hose passt **mir** nicht. |
| **stehen** | to suit | Das Kleid steht **dir** gut. |

And of course **helfen**: *Können Sie **mir** helfen?*

</Step>

<Step n="5" title="welcher? and dieser">

**welch-** asks *which?* and **dies-** answers *this*. Both take the same endings as *der, die, das*, so the last letter tells you the gender.

| | der | die | das | plural |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| which? | welch**er** | welch**e** | welch**es** | welch**e** |
| this | dies**er** | dies**e** | dies**es** | dies**e** |

<Example de="**Welcher** Pullover gefällt dir? **Dieser** hier." en="Which jumper do you like? This one here." />
<Example de="**Welche** Jacke ist neu? **Diese** hier." en="Which jacket is new? This one." />
<Example de="**Welches** Hemd ist billig? **Dieses** hier." en="Which shirt is cheap? This one." />

As the object of a verb, the masculine forms become **welchen** and **diesen**, just as *der* becomes *den*:

<Example de="**Welchen** Mantel nehmen Sie? Ich nehme **diesen**." en="Which coat are you taking? I will take this one." />

</Step>

<Step n="6" title="In the clothes shop">

<Example de="Kann ich Ihnen helfen?" en="Can I help you?" />
<Example de="Ich suche eine Jacke." en="I am looking for a jacket." />
<Example de="Welche Größe haben Sie?" en="What size are you?" />
<Example de="Größe vierzig." en="Size 40." />
<Example de="Kann ich die Jacke anprobieren?" en="Can I try the jacket on?" />
<Example de="Die Jacke ist zu klein. Haben Sie sie in Größe zweiundvierzig?" en="The jacket is too small. Do you have it in size 42?" />
<Example de="Die passt gut. Ich nehme sie." en="That fits well. I will take it." />

| German | English |
| --- | --- |
| die Größe | size |
| anprobieren | to try on |
| die Umkleidekabine | fitting room |
| zu groß / zu klein / zu eng | too big / too small / too tight |
| im Angebot | on offer |
| umtauschen | to exchange |

</Step>

<Step n="7" title="Three ways to say you like something">

| For | Use | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| how something looks | **gefallen** + dative | Die Jacke gefällt mir. |
| food, people, things in general | **mögen** + accusative | Ich mag Kaffee. |
| activities | verb + **gern** | Ich tanze gern. |
| how food tastes | **schmecken** + dative | Die Suppe schmeckt mir. |

</Step>

## Compared with English

<Compare en="**I** like **the jacket**." de="**Die Jacke** gefällt **mir**." note="Subject and object swap places. In German the jacket is the one doing the pleasing." />

<Compare en="I like the shoe**s**." de="Die Schuhe gefall**en** mir." note="Because the thing is the subject, a plural thing makes the verb plural." />

<Compare en="The trousers **are** new." de="Die Hose **ist** neu." note="'Trousers' and 'jeans' are plural in English and singular in German." />

<Compare en="**Which** jacket? **This** one." de="**Welche** Jacke? **Diese**." note="English 'which' and 'this' never change. The German words carry the gender ending, and there is no word for 'one'." />

## Typical mistakes

<Mistake wrong="**Ich** gefalle die Jacke." right="Die Jacke gefällt **mir**." why="With gefallen the thing is the subject and the person is in the dative. 'Ich gefalle' would mean that you are the one who is liked." />

<Mistake wrong="Die Schuhe **gefällt** mir." right="Die Schuhe **gefallen** mir." why="The verb agrees with the thing. Schuhe is plural." />

<Mistake wrong="Die Jacke gefällt **mich**." right="Die Jacke gefällt **mir**." why="gefallen takes the dative, so it is mir, not the accusative mich." />

<Mistake wrong="Die Hose **sind** zu klein." right="Die Hose **ist** zu klein." why="Hose is singular in German." />

<Mistake wrong="**Welcher** Jacke gefällt dir?" right="**Welche** Jacke gefällt dir?" why="welch- takes the ending of the article. Jacke is feminine, so welche." />

## Summary

| | ich | du | er / es | sie | wir | ihr | sie | Sie |

**gefallen:** thing + gefällt / gefallen + person in the dative. *Die Jacke gefällt mir. Die Schuhe gefallen mir.*

Also with the dative: **passen** (fit), **stehen** (suit), **schmecken** (taste), **helfen** (help).
