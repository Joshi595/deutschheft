Part three of the A1 speaking exam is called *Bitten formulieren und darauf reagieren*: making requests and responding to them. You draw a card with a picture or a word, such as a pen or a window, and ask someone to do something with it. The other person answers. This chapter gives you both sides of that exchange.

## The idea

The imperative is the form of the verb for telling someone to do something: *Come! Wait! Sit down!*

Because German has three kinds of *you*, it has **three imperatives**: one for **Sie**, one for **du**, one for **ihr**. The *Sie* form is the easiest and the safest, and it is the one you use with the examiners.

An imperative on its own can sound like an order. Add **bitte** and it becomes a request. Germans use the imperative with *bitte* far more freely than English speakers do; it is not rude.

## Step by step

<Step n="1" title="The Sie form">

Take the infinitive and put **Sie** after it. That is all.

| Infinitive | Imperative | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| kommen | **Kommen Sie!** | Come! |
| warten | **Warten Sie!** | Wait! |
| nehmen | **Nehmen Sie!** | Take! |
| sprechen | **Sprechen Sie!** | Speak! |

<Example de="Kommen Sie bitte herein!" en="Please come in!" />
<Example de="Warten Sie bitte einen Moment." en="Please wait a moment." />
<Example de="Sprechen Sie bitte langsam." en="Please speak slowly." />
<Example de="Nehmen Sie bitte Platz." en="Please take a seat." />

It looks like a yes/no question (*Kommen Sie?*). The difference is in the voice: a question rises, a request falls.

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="The du form">

Take the **du form** of the verb, then remove both the **-st** ending and the word *du*.

| du form | Imperative | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| du kommst | **Komm!** | Come! |
| du machst | **Mach!** | Do! |
| du gehst | **Geh!** | Go! |
| du schreibst | **Schreib!** | Write! |

<Example de="Komm bitte her!" en="Please come here!" />
<Example de="Mach bitte die Tür zu." en="Please close the door." />
<Example de="Schreib mir bald!" en="Write to me soon!" />

Stems ending in **-t** or **-d** keep an **-e** so that they can be pronounced: Wart**e**! Arbeit**e**! Antwort**e**!

</Step>

<Step n="3" title="du forms of vowel-changing verbs">

The rule from step 2 handles these by itself, with one twist.

Verbs with **e → i** or **e → ie** keep the changed vowel:

| du form | Imperative | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| du nimmst | **Nimm!** | Take! |
| du gibst | **Gib!** | Give! |
| du sprichst | **Sprich!** | Speak! |
| du liest | **Lies!** | Read! |
| du isst | **Iss!** | Eat! |
| du hilfst | **Hilf!** | Help! |

Verbs with **a → ä** drop the umlaut again:

| du form | Imperative | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| du fährst | **Fahr!** | Drive! |
| du schläfst | **Schlaf!** | Sleep! |

<Example de="Gib mir bitte das Buch." en="Please give me the book." />
<Example de="Nimm den Bus!" en="Take the bus!" />
<Example de="Schlaf gut!" en="Sleep well!" />

</Step>

<Step n="4" title="The ihr form">

The same as the normal *ihr* form, just without the word *ihr*.

<Example de="Kommt bitte!" en="Please come! (to several people)" />
<Example de="Wartet einen Moment." en="Wait a moment." />
<Example de="Nehmt die Bücher." en="Take the books." />

</Step>

<Step n="5" title="sein and haben">

| | sein | haben |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sie | **Seien Sie** … | Haben Sie … |
| du | **Sei** … | Hab … |
| ihr | **Seid** … | Habt … |

<Example de="Seien Sie bitte pünktlich." en="Please be on time." />
<Example de="Sei bitte ruhig!" en="Please be quiet!" />
<Example de="Hab keine Angst." en="Do not be afraid." />

</Step>

<Step n="6" title="Separable verbs">

The prefix goes to the end, exactly as in a normal sentence.

<Example de="**Machen** Sie bitte das Fenster **auf**." en="Please open the window." />
<Example de="**Steh** bitte **auf**!" en="Please get up!" />
<Example de="**Ruf** mich morgen **an**." en="Call me tomorrow." />
<Example de="**Füllen** Sie bitte das Formular **aus**." en="Please fill in the form." />

</Step>

<Step n="7" title="Making it polite, and saying no">

**bitte** can stand at the start, after the verb group, or at the end. **mal** softens a request between friends.

<Example de="Bitte kommen Sie herein." en="Please come in." />
<Example de="Kommen Sie bitte herein." en="Please come in." />
<Example de="Komm mal her." en="Come here a moment." />

A negative instruction puts **nicht** where it would go in a statement, or uses **kein**:

<Example de="Rauchen Sie hier bitte nicht." en="Please do not smoke here." />
<Example de="Vergiss das Buch nicht!" en="Do not forget the book!" />
<Example de="Mach keinen Lärm!" en="Do not make any noise!" />

On signs and in written instructions, German often uses the bare infinitive instead:

<Example de="Bitte nicht rauchen." en="No smoking, please." />
<Example de="Bitte hier warten." en="Please wait here." />

</Step>

<Step n="8" title="Requests in the exam">

There are two good ways to ask. Both are accepted; the question with *können* is a little softer.

<Example de="Geben Sie mir bitte den Stift." en="Please give me the pen." />
<Example de="Können Sie mir bitte den Stift geben?" en="Can you give me the pen, please?" />

And the answers:

<Example de="Ja, gern. Hier, bitte." en="Yes, of course. Here you are." />
<Example de="Ja, natürlich." en="Yes, of course." />
<Example de="Tut mir leid, ich habe keinen Stift." en="I am sorry, I do not have a pen." />

</Step>

## Compared with English

<Compare en="Come! (to anyone)" de="**Komm!** / **Kommt!** / **Kommen Sie!**" note="English has one imperative. German has three, matching du, ihr and Sie." />

<Compare en="**Could you** open the window, please?" de="**Machen Sie** bitte das Fenster auf." note="English softens requests with 'could you'. German is happy with a plain imperative plus bitte." />

<Compare en="**Don't** forget!" de="Vergiss **nicht**!" note="No 'do' in German. nicht simply follows." />

<Compare en="**Be** quiet!" de="**Sei** ruhig!" note="The imperative of sein is irregular: sei, seid, seien Sie." />

## Typical mistakes

<Mistake wrong="**Du komm** bitte!" right="**Komm** bitte!" why="The du imperative has no pronoun." />

<Mistake wrong="**Kommst** bitte!" right="**Komm** bitte!" why="Remove the -st ending as well as the du." />

<Mistake wrong="**Nehm** den Bus!" right="**Nimm** den Bus!" why="Verbs with e → i keep the changed vowel in the du imperative." />

<Mistake wrong="**Fähr** langsam!" right="**Fahr** langsam!" why="Verbs with a → ä lose the umlaut in the imperative." />

<Mistake wrong="**Aufmachen** Sie das Fenster!" right="**Machen** Sie das Fenster **auf**!" why="A separable verb splits in the imperative too." />
