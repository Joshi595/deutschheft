English has one word for *the*. German has three in the singular: **der**, **die** and **das**. Which one you need depends on the noun, and getting it right matters, because almost everything later in German grammar (cases, adjective endings, pronouns) builds on it.

## The idea

Every German noun belongs to one of three groups, called genders: **masculine** (der), **feminine** (die) and **neuter** (das).

For people, the gender usually matches: *der Mann* (the man), *die Frau* (the woman). For things, it is simply a property of the word and has nothing to do with meaning. A table is masculine (*der Tisch*), a lamp is feminine (*die Lampe*), a book is neuter (*das Buch*). There is no logic to discover here, so do not look for one.

The practical consequence is a habit you should start today: **never learn a noun alone**. Learn *der Tisch*, not *Tisch*. In this course every noun in the vocabulary lists comes with its article and its plural.

## Step by step

<Step n="1" title="Three articles, one for each gender">

| Gender | Article | Example | |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| masculine | **der** | der Tisch | the table |
| feminine | **die** | die Lampe | the lamp |
| neuter | **das** | das Buch | the book |

<Example de="**Der** Tisch ist groß." en="The table is big." />
<Example de="**Die** Lampe ist neu." en="The lamp is new." />
<Example de="**Das** Buch ist gut." en="The book is good." />

And remember from chapter 1: every noun is written with a **capital letter**.

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="People: gender follows the person">

| Masculine | Feminine |
| --- | --- |
| der Mann (man) | die Frau (woman) |
| der Vater (father) | die Mutter (mother) |
| der Lehrer (male teacher) | die Lehrerin (female teacher) |
| der Student | die Studentin |

Many words for people have a feminine form made with **-in**: *Lehrer → Lehrerin*, *Arzt → Ärztin*.

Two famous exceptions: **das Mädchen** (the girl) and **das Kind** (the child) are neuter. The next step explains why *Mädchen* is.

</Step>

<Step n="3" title="Endings that give the gender away">

You cannot guess the gender of *Tisch*. But many nouns have an ending that settles it, and these are worth knowing because they cover thousands of words.

| Ending | Gender | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **-ung** | die | die Zeitung, die Wohnung |
| **-heit, -keit** | die | die Freiheit, die Möglichkeit |
| **-ion** | die | die Information, die Station |
| **-e** (most) | die | die Lampe, die Tasche, die Straße |
| **-chen, -lein** | das | das Mädchen, das Brötchen |
| **-um** | das | das Zentrum, das Museum |
| **-er** (a person or a machine) | der | der Lehrer, der Computer |
| days, months, seasons | der | der Montag, der Mai, der Sommer |

*-chen* makes things small, and anything small becomes neuter. That is why *das Mädchen* is neuter although a girl is female.

<Example de="die Zeitung, die Wohnung, die Information." en="the newspaper, the flat, the information" />
<Example de="das Mädchen, das Brötchen, das Museum." en="the girl, the bread roll, the museum" />
<Example de="der Lehrer, der Computer, der Montag." en="the teacher, the computer, Monday" />

</Step>

<Step n="4" title="Compound nouns: the last part decides">

German builds long nouns by joining short ones. The gender, and the plural, come from the **last** part.

| Parts | Compound | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| das Haus + die Tür | **die** Haustür | front door |
| die Kinder + das Zimmer | **das** Kinderzimmer | children's room |
| das Telefon + die Nummer | **die** Telefonnummer | phone number |

<Example de="die Haustür, das Kinderzimmer, die Telefonnummer." />

</Step>

<Step n="5" title="The plural: always die">

In the plural the three genders disappear. Every plural noun takes **die**.

<Example de="der Tisch, **die** Tische." en="the table, the tables" />
<Example de="das Buch, **die** Bücher." en="the book, the books" />
<Example de="die Lampe, **die** Lampen." en="the lamp, the lamps" />

</Step>

<Step n="6" title="How the noun itself changes">

English adds *-s*. German has five patterns, and some add an umlaut as well.

| Pattern | Singular | Plural | Typical for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **-e** | der Tisch | die Tisch**e** | many masculine nouns |
| **-e** + umlaut | der Stuhl | die St**ü**hl**e** | many short masculine nouns |
| **-er** (+ umlaut) | das Kind, das Buch | die Kind**er**, die B**ü**ch**er** | many short neuter nouns |
| **-n / -en** | die Lampe, die Frau | die Lampe**n**, die Frau**en** | almost all feminine nouns |
| **-s** | das Auto, das Handy | die Auto**s**, die Handy**s** | foreign words, words ending in a vowel |
| **no ending** (sometimes umlaut) | der Lehrer, der Bruder | die Lehrer, die Br**ü**der | nouns in -er, -en, -el, -chen |

The safest rule of all: a feminine noun ending in **-e** adds **-n**. For the rest, learn the plural together with the noun, which is why the word lists show it.

<Example de="ein Stuhl, zwei Stühle." en="one chair, two chairs" />
<Example de="ein Kind, drei Kinder." en="one child, three children" />
<Example de="eine Frau, zwei Frauen." en="one woman, two women" />
<Example de="ein Auto, vier Autos." en="one car, four cars" />

</Step>

<Step n="7" title="er, sie, es for things too">

When you refer back to a noun, the pronoun follows its **gender**, not whether it is a person or a thing. A table is *he*.

| Noun | Pronoun | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| der Tisch | **er** | Der Tisch ist neu. **Er** ist groß. |
| die Lampe | **sie** | Die Lampe ist alt. **Sie** ist klein. |
| das Buch | **es** | Das Buch ist gut. **Es** ist interessant. |
| die Bücher | **sie** | Die Bücher sind neu. **Sie** sind teuer. |

<Example de="Wo ist der Stift? **Er** ist hier." en="Where is the pen? It is here." />
<Example de="Wie ist die Wohnung? **Sie** ist schön." en="What is the flat like? It is nice." />

</Step>

## Compared with English

<Compare en="**the** table, **the** lamp, **the** book" de="**der** Tisch, **die** Lampe, **das** Buch" note="One English word, three German ones. The article is part of the noun and has to be learned with it." />

<Compare en="The table is new. **It** is big." de="Der Tisch ist neu. **Er** ist groß." note="English uses 'it' for all things. German picks er, sie or es according to the noun's gender." />

<Compare en="book, book**s**" de="Buch, B**ü**ch**er**" note="English plurals are nearly always -s. German has several patterns, often with an umlaut." />

<Compare en="the girl … **she**" de="das Mädchen … **es** (or **sie**)" note="Grammatically das Mädchen is neuter. In everyday speech Germans often say 'sie' when they mean the girl herself." />

## Typical mistakes

<Mistake wrong="Tisch ist groß." right="**Der** Tisch ist groß." why="A singular noun nearly always needs an article in German, just as 'table is big' is wrong in English." />

<Mistake wrong="Die Lampe ist neu. **Es** ist klein." right="Die Lampe ist neu. **Sie** ist klein." why="The pronoun follows the gender of the noun. Lampe is feminine, so it is 'sie'." />

<Mistake wrong="zwei **Kinds**" right="zwei **Kinder**" why="-s is only one of the plural patterns, and not the most common. Kind takes -er." />

<Mistake wrong="**das** Zeitung" right="**die** Zeitung" why="Nouns ending in -ung are always feminine." />

<Mistake wrong="der mann, die frau" right="der **M**ann, die **F**rau" why="All nouns are written with a capital letter." />

## Summary

<Summary>

| | Singular | Plural |
| --- | --- | --- |
| masculine | **der** Tisch → er | **die** Tische → sie |
| feminine | **die** Lampe → sie | **die** Lampen → sie |
| neuter | **das** Buch → es | **die** Bücher → sie |

**Always die:** -ung, -heit, -keit, -ion, most nouns in -e.
**Always das:** -chen, -lein, -um.
**Usually der:** -er for people and machines; days, months, seasons.

**Plural:** feminine nouns add -n or -en; the rest you learn with the word.

</Summary>
