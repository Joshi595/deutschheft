Being able to say that you are ill, and to understand what you should do about it, is one of the most practical things A1 gives you. In the exam it appears as phone messages from a doctor's surgery, as a written excuse for missing a course, and as a role-play at the doctor's.

## The idea

German has two main ways to say that something hurts, and they follow different patterns.

- **Ich habe …schmerzen.** You *have* pains in a part of the body: *Kopfschmerzen, Bauchschmerzen, Zahnschmerzen*. One compound noun does the job.
- **Mein … tut weh.** The body part *does hurt*: *Mein Kopf tut weh.* The body part is the subject, so the verb agrees with it: one thing **tut** weh, several things **tun** weh.

Around these two patterns you need a small vocabulary of symptoms and a few fixed phrases for the surgery. The chapter also adds the accusative pronouns (*mich, dich, ihn …*), because health talk is full of them: *Der Arzt untersucht **mich**. Ich rufe **Sie** an.*

<Example de="der Kopf, der Hals, der Bauch, der Rücken." en="head, throat, stomach, back" />
<Example de="das Auge, das Ohr, die Nase, der Mund." en="eye, ear, nose, mouth" />
<Example de="der Arm, die Hand, das Bein, der Fuß." en="arm, hand, leg, foot" />

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="Ich habe …schmerzen">

Join the body part to **-schmerzen** (pains) and use *haben*.

| German | English |
| --- | --- |
| Kopfschmerzen | a headache |
| Bauchschmerzen | stomach ache |
| Halsschmerzen | a sore throat |
| Zahnschmerzen | toothache |
| Rückenschmerzen | backache |
| Ohrenschmerzen | earache |

<Example de="Ich habe Kopfschmerzen." en="I have a headache." />
<Example de="Mein Sohn hat Bauchschmerzen." en="My son has stomach ache." />
<Example de="Haben Sie Schmerzen?" en="Are you in pain?" />

*Schmerzen* is plural, and there is no article.

</Step>

<Step n="3" title="Mein … tut weh">

**wehtun** is a separable verb: *tut* in position 2, *weh* at the end. It agrees with the body part.

<Example de="Mein Kopf **tut** weh." en="My head hurts." />
<Example de="Mein Bein **tut** weh." en="My leg hurts." />
<Example de="Meine Augen **tun** weh." en="My eyes hurt." />
<Example de="Wo **tut** es weh?" en="Where does it hurt?" />
<Example de="Hier **tut** es weh." en="It hurts here." />

</Step>

<Step n="4" title="What is wrong">

| German | English |
| --- | --- |
| Ich bin krank. | I am ill. |
| Ich bin erkältet. | I have a cold. |
| Ich habe Fieber. | I have a temperature. |
| Ich habe Husten. | I have a cough. |
| Ich habe Schnupfen. | I have a runny nose. |
| Ich habe Grippe. | I have flu. |
| Mir ist schlecht. | I feel sick. |
| Ich bin müde. | I am tired. |
| Ich fühle mich nicht gut. | I do not feel well. |

<Example de="Was fehlt Ihnen?" en="What is the matter?" />
<Example de="Ich habe seit drei Tagen Fieber und Husten." en="I have had a temperature and a cough for three days." />
<Example de="Gute Besserung!" en="Get well soon!" />

**seit** (since, for) is followed by the present tense in German, because the illness is still going on.

</Step>

<Step n="5" title="Making an appointment">

<Example de="Guten Tag, hier ist Maria Rossi. Ich brauche einen Termin." en="Hello, this is Maria Rossi. I need an appointment." />
<Example de="Wann haben Sie Zeit?" en="When are you free?" />
<Example de="Können Sie morgen um zehn Uhr kommen?" en="Can you come tomorrow at ten o'clock?" />
<Example de="Ja, das geht. Vielen Dank." en="Yes, that works. Thank you very much." />
<Example de="Bitte bringen Sie Ihre Versichertenkarte mit." en="Please bring your health insurance card." />

| German | English |
| --- | --- |
| der Termin | appointment |
| die Praxis | surgery, practice |
| die Sprechstunde | consulting hours |
| die Versichertenkarte | health insurance card |
| der Notfall | emergency |
| das Rezept | prescription |
| das Medikament / die Tablette | medicine / tablet |

</Step>

<Step n="6" title="What the doctor says">

The doctor uses the imperative with *Sie* from chapter 18, and you report it with **sollen** from chapter 13.

<Example de="Bleiben Sie zwei Tage im Bett." en="Stay in bed for two days." />
<Example de="Trinken Sie viel Tee." en="Drink a lot of tea." />
<Example de="Nehmen Sie die Tabletten dreimal am Tag." en="Take the tablets three times a day." />
<Example de="Der Arzt sagt, ich **soll** im Bett bleiben." en="The doctor says I should stay in bed." />
<Example de="Ich **darf** nicht arbeiten." en="I am not allowed to work." />

</Step>

<Step n="7" title="mich, dich, ihn: the accusative pronouns">

In chapter 9 the articles changed in the accusative. Pronouns change too.

| Nominative | Accusative | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ich | **mich** | me |
| du | **dich** | you |
| er | **ihn** | him, it |
| sie | **sie** | her, it |
| es | **es** | it |
| wir | **uns** | us |
| ihr | **euch** | you |
| sie / Sie | **sie / Sie** | them / you |

As with the articles, only the masculine form of the third person changes clearly (*er → ihn*). *sie* and *es* stay as they are.

<Example de="Der Arzt untersucht **mich**." en="The doctor examines me." />
<Example de="Ich rufe **dich** morgen an." en="I will call you tomorrow." />
<Example de="Kennst du Herrn Weber? Ja, ich kenne **ihn**." en="Do you know Mr Weber? Yes, I know him." />
<Example de="Wo ist die Tablette? Ich habe **sie** hier." en="Where is the tablet? I have it here." />
<Example de="Besuchst du **uns** am Wochenende?" en="Are you visiting us at the weekend?" />

Remember that things have gender: a masculine thing is **ihn**. *Wo ist der Schlüssel? Ich habe **ihn**.*

</Step>

## Compared with English

<Compare en="I have **a** headache." de="Ich habe Kopfschmerzen." note="German uses the plural 'pains' with no article, and joins it to the body part in one word." />

<Compare en="My leg hurts. My legs hurt." de="Mein Bein tut weh. Meine Beine tun weh." note="The same structure as English: the body part is the subject and the verb agrees with it." />

<Compare en="I **have had** a cough **for** three days." de="Ich **habe seit** drei Tagen Husten." note="For something that started in the past and is still going on, German uses the present tense with seit." />

<Compare en="I see **him**. I see **it** (the key)." de="Ich sehe **ihn**. Ich sehe **ihn** (den Schlüssel)." note="German uses ihn for any masculine noun, whether a person or a thing." />

## Typical mistakes

<Mistake wrong="Ich habe **einen Kopfschmerz**." right="Ich habe **Kopfschmerzen**." why="Pains are plural and take no article." />

<Mistake wrong="Meine Augen **tut** weh." right="Meine Augen **tun** weh." why="The verb agrees with the body part. Augen is plural." />

<Mistake wrong="Ich **bin** Fieber." right="Ich **habe** Fieber." why="Symptoms you have take haben. sein goes with adjectives: Ich bin krank." />

<Mistake wrong="Ich kenne **er**." right="Ich kenne **ihn**." why="After kennen the pronoun is the object, so it needs the accusative form." />

<Mistake wrong="Ich **habe** seit drei Tagen krank **gewesen**." right="Ich **bin** seit drei Tagen krank." why="With seit, German uses the present tense, because you are still ill." />
