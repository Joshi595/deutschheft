*Was machen Sie in Ihrer Freizeit?* is one of the questions you can count on in the exam, and "hobby" is a standard cue card. Making an arrangement is just as central: the A2 speaking exam has a whole task on it, and the A1 writing task is very often a short message agreeing or cancelling a meeting.

## The idea

For hobbies, German gives you one small word that does most of the work: **gern**. Put it after the verb and the sentence means you like doing that thing. Its two partners, **lieber** and **am liebsten**, let you say what you prefer and what you like best.

An arrangement is a small negotiation with four moves: **suggest**, **accept or decline**, **agree a time**, **agree a place**. Each move has a few ready-made sentences. Learn those and you can arrange anything.

## Step by step

<Step n="1" title="Free-time activities">

| German | English |
| --- | --- |
| Fußball spielen | to play football |
| Gitarre spielen | to play the guitar |
| Musik hören | to listen to music |
| ins Kino gehen | to go to the cinema |
| Freunde treffen | to meet friends |
| spazieren gehen | to go for a walk |
| Sport machen | to do sport |
| Rad fahren | to cycle |
| schwimmen | to swim |
| lesen | to read |
| kochen | to cook |
| tanzen | to dance |
| wandern | to hike |
| fotografieren | to take photos |
| reisen | to travel |

Many activities are a noun plus a verb. In a sentence the verb goes to position 2 as always, and the noun waits at the end.

<Example de="Ich **spiele** am Wochenende **Fußball**." en="I play football at the weekend." />
<Example de="Wir **gehen** heute Abend **ins Kino**." en="We are going to the cinema this evening." />
<Example de="Sie **fährt** gern **Rad**." en="She likes cycling." />

*spielen* is used for games and for musical instruments, with no article: *Fußball spielen, Tennis spielen, Klavier spielen*.

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="gern: liking to do something">

**gern** comes straight after the verb (or after the subject, when the subject follows the verb).

<Example de="Ich schwimme **gern**." en="I like swimming." />
<Example de="Ich höre **gern** Musik." en="I like listening to music." />
<Example de="Kochst du **gern**?" en="Do you like cooking?" />
<Example de="Am Wochenende gehe ich **gern** spazieren." en="At the weekend I like going for a walk." />

The negative is **nicht gern**:

<Example de="Ich tanze **nicht gern**." en="I do not like dancing." />

</Step>

<Step n="3" title="lieber and am liebsten">

| Word | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **gern** | like | Ich lese gern. |
| **lieber** | prefer | Ich höre lieber Musik. |
| **am liebsten** | like best | Am liebsten gehe ich ins Kino. |

<Example de="Ich spiele gern Tennis, aber ich spiele **lieber** Fußball." en="I like playing tennis, but I prefer playing football." />
<Example de="**Am liebsten** treffe ich meine Freunde." en="Best of all I like meeting my friends." />
<Example de="Was machst du **am liebsten**?" en="What do you like doing best?" />

To compare two things, add **als** (than): *Ich trinke lieber Tee **als** Kaffee.*

</Step>

<Step n="4" title="Talking about your hobbies">

<Example de="Was machen Sie in Ihrer Freizeit?" en="What do you do in your free time?" />
<Example de="Was sind deine Hobbys?" en="What are your hobbies?" />
<Example de="Meine Hobbys sind Lesen und Schwimmen." en="My hobbies are reading and swimming." />
<Example de="In meiner Freizeit koche ich gern." en="In my free time I like cooking." />

When a verb is used as a noun, as in *Lesen* and *Schwimmen*, it gets a capital letter.

How often you do it:

| German | English |
| --- | --- |
| immer | always |
| oft | often |
| manchmal | sometimes |
| selten | rarely |
| nie | never |
| jeden Tag | every day |
| einmal pro Woche | once a week |

<Example de="Ich gehe **oft** ins Kino." en="I often go to the cinema." />
<Example de="Ich mache **zweimal pro Woche** Sport." en="I do sport twice a week." />

</Step>

<Step n="5" title="Suggesting something">

<Example de="Hast du am Samstag Zeit?" en="Do you have time on Saturday?" />
<Example de="Wollen wir ins Kino gehen?" en="Shall we go to the cinema?" />
<Example de="Möchtest du mitkommen?" en="Would you like to come along?" />
<Example de="Gehen wir heute Abend essen?" en="Shall we go out to eat this evening?" />

*Wollen wir …?* is the standard way to make a suggestion. It corresponds to English *Shall we …?*

</Step>

<Step n="6" title="Accepting and declining">

Saying yes:

<Example de="Ja, gern!" en="Yes, I would love to!" />
<Example de="Gute Idee!" en="Good idea!" />
<Example de="Ja, das geht." en="Yes, that works." />

Saying no politely. A German refusal normally comes with a reason or another suggestion:

<Example de="Tut mir leid, ich kann nicht." en="I am sorry, I cannot." />
<Example de="Am Samstag habe ich leider keine Zeit." en="Unfortunately I have no time on Saturday." />
<Example de="Ich muss arbeiten. Geht es am Sonntag?" en="I have to work. Would Sunday work?" />

</Step>

<Step n="7" title="Fixing the time and place">

<Example de="Wann treffen wir uns?" en="When shall we meet?" />
<Example de="Um wie viel Uhr?" en="At what time?" />
<Example de="Um sieben Uhr vor dem Kino." en="At seven o'clock in front of the cinema." />
<Example de="Wo treffen wir uns?" en="Where shall we meet?" />
<Example de="Bis Samstag!" en="See you on Saturday!" />

A complete arrangement:

<Example de="Hast du am Freitag Zeit? Wollen wir essen gehen?" en="Are you free on Friday? Shall we go out to eat?" />
<Example de="Am Freitag kann ich leider nicht. Geht es am Samstag?" en="I cannot on Friday, unfortunately. Would Saturday work?" />
<Example de="Ja, Samstag ist gut. Um wie viel Uhr?" en="Yes, Saturday is fine. At what time?" />
<Example de="Um acht Uhr im Restaurant Roma." en="At eight o'clock at the Restaurant Roma." />
<Example de="Gut, bis Samstag!" en="Good, see you on Saturday!" />

</Step>

## Compared with English

<Compare en="I **like** swimm**ing**." de="Ich schwimme **gern**." note="English puts a verb in front (like + -ing). German keeps the main verb and adds the adverb gern after it." />

<Compare en="I **prefer** tea." de="Ich trinke **lieber** Tee." note="German has no everyday verb for 'prefer'. It uses lieber with the verb for what you are doing." />

<Compare en="**Shall** we go to the cinema?" de="**Wollen** wir ins Kino gehen?" note="A suggestion is made with wollen, literally 'Do we want to …?'" />

<Compare en="I play **the** guitar." de="Ich spiele Gitarre." note="No article with instruments and games in German." />

## Typical mistakes

<Mistake wrong="Ich **mag** schwimmen." right="Ich **schwimme gern**." why="For activities German uses verb + gern. mögen is for things and people: Ich mag Musik." />

<Mistake wrong="Ich **gern** spiele Fußball." right="Ich spiele **gern** Fußball." why="gern comes after the verb, never in front of it." />

<Mistake wrong="Ich spiele **das** Klavier." right="Ich spiele Klavier." why="Instruments and games take no article after spielen." />

<Mistake wrong="Ich trinke **mehr gern** Tee." right="Ich trinke **lieber** Tee." why="The comparative of gern is the irregular form lieber." />

<Mistake wrong="Nein." right="Tut mir leid, am Samstag kann ich leider nicht." why="A bare 'nein' to an invitation sounds rude. Give a reason, or at least say you are sorry." />
