Shopping gives you a complete, repeatable conversation, and its pieces come up all through the A1 exam: prices in the listening part, notices and adverts in the reading part, and "asking for something" in the speaking part. You already have the numbers, the accusative and *möchten*. This chapter puts them to work.

## The idea

A shop conversation in German runs along the same rails every time:

1. A greeting.
2. You say what you want and how much.
3. The assistant asks whether you want anything else.
4. You say yes and name it, or say that is all.
5. The assistant tells you the total.
6. You pay and say goodbye.

Learn one sentence for each station and you can shop anywhere. The only new grammar is how German states a quantity, and it is simpler than English: there is no word for *of*.

## Step by step

<Step n="1" title="Where to buy what">

| Shop | You buy |
| --- | --- |
| der Supermarkt | almost everything |
| die Bäckerei | Brot, Brötchen, Kuchen |
| die Metzgerei | Fleisch, Wurst |
| der Markt | Obst, Gemüse |
| die Apotheke | Medikamente (medicine) |
| der Kiosk | Zeitungen, Getränke |
| das Kaufhaus | Kleidung, Möbel (clothes, furniture) |

To say where you are going to shop, use **in** with a shop and **auf** with the market. For now learn them as fixed phrases.

<Example de="Ich kaufe Brot **in der Bäckerei**." en="I buy bread at the bakery." />
<Example de="Wir kaufen Obst **auf dem Markt**." en="We buy fruit at the market." />
<Example de="Ich gehe **in den Supermarkt**." en="I am going to the supermarket." />

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="Saying what you want">

Three ways, from plain to very polite. All take the accusative.

<Example de="Ich brauche einen Liter Milch." en="I need a litre of milk." />
<Example de="Ich möchte ein Brot, bitte." en="I would like a loaf of bread, please." />
<Example de="Ich hätte gern zwei Brötchen." en="I would like two bread rolls." />

*Ich hätte gern* is the most common phrase in German shops. Learn it as one block; its grammar belongs to a much later level.

To ask whether the shop has something:

<Example de="Haben Sie Tomaten?" en="Do you have tomatoes?" />
<Example de="Wo finde ich den Käse?" en="Where do I find the cheese?" />

</Step>

<Step n="3" title="Quantities: no 'of'">

German puts the quantity and the noun side by side, with nothing in between.

| German | English |
| --- | --- |
| ein Kilo Äpfel | a kilo **of** apples |
| ein Pfund Tomaten | a pound (500 g) **of** tomatoes |
| 200 Gramm Käse | 200 grams **of** cheese |
| ein Liter Milch | a litre **of** milk |
| eine Flasche Wasser | a bottle **of** water |
| eine Packung Reis | a packet **of** rice |
| eine Dose Tomaten | a tin **of** tomatoes |
| ein Glas Marmelade | a jar **of** jam |
| ein Stück Kuchen | a piece **of** cake |
| eine Tasse Kaffee | a cup **of** coffee |

<Example de="Ich hätte gern ein Kilo Äpfel." en="I would like a kilo of apples." />
<Example de="Zweihundert Gramm Käse, bitte." en="200 grams of cheese, please." />
<Example de="Ich nehme eine Flasche Wasser." en="I will take a bottle of water." />

*Kilo*, *Gramm*, *Liter* and *Pfund* stay singular after a number: *zwei Kilo*, not *zwei Kilos*. Containers do form a plural: zwei Flasche**n**, drei Packung**en**, zwei Dose**n**.

</Step>

<Step n="4" title="Asking the price">

The verb is **kosten**, and it agrees with the thing: one item *kostet*, several items *kosten*.

<Example de="Was **kostet** das Brot?" en="What does the bread cost?" />
<Example de="Was **kosten** die Äpfel?" en="What do the apples cost?" />
<Example de="Ein Kilo kostet zwei Euro neunzig." en="A kilo costs €2.90." />
<Example de="Das ist aber teuer!" en="That is expensive!" />
<Example de="Das ist billig." en="That is cheap." />

| German | English |
| --- | --- |
| teuer | expensive |
| billig, günstig | cheap, good value |
| das Angebot | special offer |
| im Angebot | on offer |

</Step>

<Step n="5" title="The rest of the conversation">

What the assistant says:

<Example de="Bitte schön? Was darf es sein?" en="Yes, please? What can I get you?" />
<Example de="Sonst noch etwas?" en="Anything else?" />
<Example de="Das macht zusammen acht Euro zwanzig." en="That comes to €8.20 altogether." />

What you say:

<Example de="Ja, ich brauche noch Eier." en="Yes, I also need eggs." />
<Example de="Nein danke, das ist alles." en="No thank you, that is all." />
<Example de="Kann ich mit Karte zahlen?" en="Can I pay by card?" />
<Example de="Hier, bitte. Auf Wiedersehen!" en="Here you are. Goodbye!" />

</Step>

<Step n="6" title="A whole conversation">

<Example de="Guten Morgen! Was darf es sein?" en="Good morning! What can I get you?" />
<Example de="Guten Morgen. Ich hätte gern vier Brötchen und ein Brot." en="Good morning. I would like four rolls and a loaf of bread." />
<Example de="Gern. Sonst noch etwas?" en="Certainly. Anything else?" />
<Example de="Ja, ein Stück Apfelkuchen, bitte." en="Yes, a piece of apple cake, please." />
<Example de="Das macht sechs Euro achtzig." en="That comes to €6.80." />
<Example de="Bitte schön. Tschüs!" en="Here you are. Bye!" />

</Step>

<Step n="7" title="Reading signs">

The reading exam shows you short notices. The key words:

| German | English |
| --- | --- |
| geöffnet / offen | open |
| geschlossen | closed |
| Öffnungszeiten | opening hours |
| Montag bis Freitag | Monday to Friday |
| von 8 bis 20 Uhr | from 8 am to 8 pm |
| Sonderangebot | special offer |
| nur heute | today only |
| Eingang / Ausgang | entrance / exit |
| Kasse | checkout, till |

<Example de="Öffnungszeiten: Montag bis Freitag von acht bis zwanzig Uhr." en="Opening hours: Monday to Friday from 8 to 20." />
<Example de="Am Sonntag geschlossen." en="Closed on Sundays." />

Most shops in Germany are closed on Sundays, so this sign is one you will really see.

</Step>

## Compared with English

<Compare en="a kilo **of** apples" de="ein Kilo Äpfel" note="No 'of'. Quantity and noun stand next to each other." />

<Compare en="two kilo**s**, three litre**s**" de="zwei Kilo, drei Liter" note="Units of measurement stay singular after a number in German." />

<Compare en="How much **is** it? / How much **are** they?" de="Was **kostet** das? / Was **kosten** die Äpfel?" note="German uses kosten rather than sein, and the verb changes for the plural just as English 'is' and 'are' do." />

<Compare en="That'll be eight twenty." de="Das **macht** acht Euro zwanzig." note="German says the total 'makes' so much." />

## Typical mistakes

<Mistake wrong="ein Kilo **von** Äpfeln" right="ein Kilo Äpfel" why="Quantities take no preposition. Just put the noun after the amount." />

<Mistake wrong="Was **kostet** die Tomaten?" right="Was **kosten** die Tomaten?" why="Tomaten is plural, so the verb has to be plural too." />

<Mistake wrong="Ich möchte zwei **Kilos** Kartoffeln." right="Ich möchte zwei **Kilo** Kartoffeln." why="Kilo, Gramm, Liter and Pfund have no plural after a number." />

<Mistake wrong="Ich **will** ein Brot." right="Ich **möchte** ein Brot. / Ich **hätte gern** ein Brot." why="'Ich will' is grammatically correct but sounds demanding. In a shop use möchte or hätte gern." />

<Mistake wrong="Ich hätte gern **ein** Liter Milch." right="Ich hätte gern **einen** Liter Milch." why="der Liter is masculine and it is the object, so it needs einen." />
