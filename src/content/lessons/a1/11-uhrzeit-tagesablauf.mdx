Times and days run through the whole A1 exam. Announcements in the listening part give departure times; notices in the reading part give opening hours; and in the writing part you often have to say when you are coming. A wrong time means a wrong answer, so this chapter is worth doing slowly.

## The idea

German has **two ways of telling the time**, and you need both.

The **official** way uses the 24-hour clock and simply reads the numbers: 14:30 is *vierzehn Uhr dreißig*. You hear it at stations, on the radio and in any announcement. It is easy.

The **everyday** way uses the 12-hour clock with *nach* (past), *vor* (to) and *halb* (half). This is what people say to each other. It has one famous trap: *halb drei* is not half past three. It is half past **two**, because Germans think of it as "halfway to three".

The second idea of the chapter is the **separable verb**: a verb whose first part breaks off and travels to the end of the sentence. Daily routines are full of them.

## Step by step

<Step n="1" title="Asking the time">

<Example de="Wie spät ist es?" en="What time is it? (literally: how late is it?)" />
<Example de="Wie viel Uhr ist es?" en="What time is it?" />
<Example de="Es ist drei Uhr." en="It is three o'clock." />

**die Uhr** means both *clock* and *o'clock*. The word for *hour* as a length of time is different: **die Stunde**.

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="The official way">

Hour + **Uhr** + minutes. Use the 24-hour clock.

| Written | Spoken |
| --- | --- |
| 7:00 | sieben Uhr |
| 9:15 | neun Uhr fünfzehn |
| 14:30 | vierzehn Uhr dreißig |
| 18:45 | achtzehn Uhr fünfundvierzig |
| 21:05 | einundzwanzig Uhr fünf |

<Example de="Der Zug fährt um vierzehn Uhr dreißig." en="The train leaves at 14:30." />
<Example de="Es ist einundzwanzig Uhr fünf." en="It is 21:05." />

</Step>

<Step n="3" title="The everyday way">

Use the 12-hour clock, and leave out the word *Uhr* except on the full hour.

| Time | Spoken | Literally |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3:00 | drei Uhr | three o'clock |
| 3:05 | fünf **nach** drei | five past three |
| 3:15 | Viertel **nach** drei | quarter past three |
| 3:30 | **halb vier** | half (to) four |
| 3:45 | Viertel **vor** vier | quarter to four |
| 3:55 | fünf **vor** vier | five to four |

<Example de="Es ist Viertel nach drei." en="It is quarter past three." />
<Example de="Es ist halb vier." en="It is half past three." />
<Example de="Es ist Viertel vor vier." en="It is quarter to four." />

**halb** always names the hour that is coming. *halb vier* = 3:30. *halb acht* = 7:30. *halb eins* = 12:30.

For "one o'clock", say **ein Uhr** with the word *Uhr*, but **eins** without it: *Es ist ein Uhr. Es ist halb eins.*

</Step>

<Step n="4" title="The days of the week">

| | |
| --- | --- |
| der Montag | Monday |
| der Dienstag | Tuesday |
| der Mittwoch | Wednesday |
| der Donnerstag | Thursday |
| der Freitag | Friday |
| der Samstag | Saturday |
| der Sonntag | Sunday |

All days are masculine. **das Wochenende** is the weekend.

<Example de="Montag, Dienstag, Mittwoch, Donnerstag, Freitag, Samstag, Sonntag." />

And the parts of the day:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| der Morgen | morning |
| der Vormittag | late morning |
| der Mittag | midday |
| der Nachmittag | afternoon |
| der Abend | evening |
| die Nacht | night |

</Step>

<Step n="5" title="um, am, von … bis">

Each kind of time has its own little word.

| Word | Used with | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **um** | clock times | **um** acht Uhr, **um** halb neun |
| **am** | days and parts of the day | **am** Montag, **am** Abend, **am** Wochenende |
| **in der** | night only | **in der** Nacht |
| **von … bis** | a span | **von** neun **bis** siebzehn Uhr |

<Example de="Der Kurs beginnt **um** neun Uhr." en="The course starts at nine o'clock." />
<Example de="**Am** Samstag arbeite ich nicht." en="On Saturday I do not work." />
<Example de="Ich arbeite **von** Montag **bis** Freitag." en="I work from Monday to Friday." />
<Example de="**Wann** kommst du? **Um** halb acht." en="When are you coming? At half past seven." />

Adding **-s** to a day or part of the day means "every": *montags* (on Mondays), *abends* (in the evenings). These are written small.

</Step>

<Step n="6" title="Separable verbs">

Many German verbs have a small prefix: **auf**stehen (to get up), **ein**kaufen (to shop), **an**rufen (to phone), **fern**sehen (to watch TV), **an**fangen (to begin), **auf**räumen (to tidy up), **mit**kommen (to come along).

In a sentence the verb is conjugated in position 2 as usual, and **the prefix goes to the very end**.

| Infinitive | Sentence |
| --- | --- |
| **auf**stehen | Ich **stehe** um sieben Uhr **auf**. |
| **ein**kaufen | Wir **kaufen** am Samstag **ein**. |
| **an**rufen | Sie **ruft** ihre Mutter **an**. |
| **an**fangen | Der Film **fängt** um acht Uhr **an**. |
| **mit**kommen | **Kommst** du **mit**? |

<Example de="Ich **stehe** um sieben Uhr **auf**." en="I get up at seven o'clock." />
<Example de="Wir **kaufen** am Samstag **ein**." en="We go shopping on Saturday." />
<Example de="Wann **fängt** der Kurs **an**?" en="When does the course begin?" />

The two halves hold the rest of the sentence between them like a bracket. When you listen, the meaning is not complete until the last word: *Ich rufe dich …* could still end in *an* (I phone you) or something else.

In the dictionary, and in the word lists here, separable verbs are written as one word. You recognise them because the stress falls on the prefix: **AUF**stehen.

</Step>

<Step n="7" title="A day">

<Example de="Ich stehe um halb sieben auf." en="I get up at half past six." />
<Example de="Um sieben Uhr frühstücke ich." en="At seven o'clock I have breakfast." />
<Example de="Ich arbeite von acht bis sechzehn Uhr." en="I work from eight until four." />
<Example de="Am Nachmittag kaufe ich ein." en="In the afternoon I go shopping." />
<Example de="Am Abend sehe ich fern oder rufe meine Eltern an." en="In the evening I watch TV or phone my parents." />
<Example de="Um elf Uhr gehe ich ins Bett." en="At eleven o'clock I go to bed." />

Notice the word order from chapter 8: when the time comes first, the verb is still second and *ich* follows it.

</Step>

## Compared with English

<Compare en="half past **three** (3:30)" de="halb **vier**" note="English looks back to the hour that has passed. German looks forward to the hour that is coming." />

<Compare en="**at** eight, **on** Monday, **in** the evening" de="**um** acht, **am** Montag, **am** Abend" note="Three English prepositions, two German ones. um for clock times, am for days and parts of the day." />

<Compare en="I **get up** at seven." de="Ich **stehe** um sieben **auf**." note="English has verbs in two parts too, but keeps them together. German sends the second part to the end." />

<Compare en="3 pm" de="15 Uhr" note="Timetables, appointments and announcements in German use the 24-hour clock. There is no am or pm." />

## Typical mistakes

<Mistake wrong="Es ist halb drei. (meaning 3:30)" right="Es ist halb **vier**." why="halb names the coming hour. halb drei is 2:30." />

<Mistake wrong="Ich **aufstehe** um sieben Uhr." right="Ich **stehe** um sieben Uhr **auf**." why="The prefix of a separable verb goes to the end of the sentence." />

<Mistake wrong="**Am** acht Uhr beginnt der Kurs." right="**Um** acht Uhr beginnt der Kurs." why="Clock times take um. am is for days and parts of the day." />

<Mistake wrong="**In** Montag arbeite ich." right="**Am** Montag arbeite ich." why="Days of the week take am." />

<Mistake wrong="Es ist drei **Uhren**." right="Es ist drei **Uhr**." why="In telling the time, Uhr never takes a plural. 'drei Uhren' would mean three clocks." />
