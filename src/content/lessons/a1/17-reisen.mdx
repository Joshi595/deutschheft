The last chapter answered **Wo?**: where something is. This one answers **Wohin?**: where someone is going. Travel is also the richest source of listening tasks in the A1 exam: platform numbers, departure times and delays, all in station announcements.

## The idea

German has three little words for English *to*, and which one you use depends on the destination.

- **nach** for cities, countries and *home*.
- **zu** for people, and for places you go *up to* without stressing that you go inside.
- **in** for places you go *into*.

The third one hides a neat piece of grammar. The preposition **in** can take two cases. With **Wo?** it takes the dative, as you learned last time: *Ich bin **im** Kino.* With **Wohin?** it takes the accusative: *Ich gehe **ins** Kino.* Same preposition, different case, and the case alone tells the listener whether you are there already or on your way.

## Step by step

<Step n="1" title="nach: cities, countries, home">

Use **nach** with place names that have no article, and in the fixed phrase *nach Hause*.

<Example de="Ich fahre **nach** Berlin." en="I am going to Berlin." />
<Example de="Wir fliegen **nach** Italien." en="We are flying to Italy." />
<Example de="Ich gehe **nach** Hause." en="I am going home." />

Also for directions: *nach links* (to the left), *nach rechts*.

The few countries that have an article take **in** instead: *in die Schweiz, in die Türkei, in die USA*.

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="zu: people and places">

**zu** always takes the dative, and you know its short forms: **zum** (masculine, neuter) and **zur** (feminine).

<Example de="Ich gehe **zum** Arzt." en="I am going to the doctor." />
<Example de="Sie fährt **zur** Arbeit." en="She is going to work." />
<Example de="Wir gehen **zu** Anna." en="We are going to Anna's." />
<Example de="Wie komme ich **zum** Flughafen?" en="How do I get to the airport?" />

With a person, *zu* is the only choice.

</Step>

<Step n="3" title="in + accusative: going into a place">

| | Wo? dative | Wohin? accusative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| der Park | **im** Park | **in den** Park |
| das Kino | **im** Kino | **ins** Kino |
| die Stadt | **in der** Stadt | **in die** Stadt |

**ins** is short for *in das*.

<Example de="Ich bin **im** Kino. Ich gehe **ins** Kino." en="I am at the cinema. I am going to the cinema." />
<Example de="Wir sind **in der** Stadt. Wir fahren **in die** Stadt." en="We are in town. We are going into town." />
<Example de="Die Kinder sind **im** Park. Die Kinder gehen **in den** Park." en="The children are in the park. The children are going to the park." />

The verb gives you the clue. Verbs of **movement** (*gehen, fahren, fliegen, kommen*) go with *Wohin?* and the accusative. Verbs of **staying** (*sein, wohnen, arbeiten, bleiben*) go with *Wo?* and the dative.

</Step>

<Step n="4" title="Where from: aus and von">

| Word | Use | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **aus** | out of a place, country or city | Ich komme **aus** Indien. |
| **von** | from a person or a point | Ich komme **vom** Arzt. |

Both take the dative. **vom** is short for *von dem*.

<Example de="Der Zug kommt **aus** Hamburg." en="The train comes from Hamburg." />
<Example de="Ich komme gerade **von der** Arbeit." en="I have just come from work." />

</Step>

<Step n="5" title="How you travel: mit + dative">

| German | English |
| --- | --- |
| mit **dem** Bus | by bus |
| mit **dem** Zug | by train |
| mit **dem** Auto | by car |
| mit **dem** Fahrrad | by bicycle |
| mit **dem** Flugzeug | by plane |
| mit **der** U-Bahn | by underground |
| mit **der** Straßenbahn | by tram |
| **zu Fuß** | on foot |

*mit* always takes the dative: *dem* for masculine and neuter, *der* for feminine. *zu Fuß* is the one exception to the pattern.

<Example de="Ich fahre **mit dem** Bus zur Arbeit." en="I go to work by bus." />
<Example de="Wir fliegen **mit dem** Flugzeug nach Spanien." en="We are flying to Spain." />
<Example de="Sie geht **zu Fuß**." en="She walks." />

Remember: *gehen* is on foot, *fahren* is with any vehicle, *fliegen* is by plane.

</Step>

<Step n="6" title="At the station">

| German | English |
| --- | --- |
| die Fahrkarte / das Ticket | ticket |
| einfach | one way |
| hin und zurück | return |
| das Gleis | platform, track |
| die Abfahrt | departure |
| die Ankunft | arrival |
| abfahren | to leave |
| ankommen | to arrive |
| umsteigen | to change (trains) |
| einsteigen / aussteigen | to get on / to get off |
| die Verspätung | delay |
| pünktlich | on time |

*abfahren, ankommen, umsteigen, einsteigen* and *aussteigen* are all separable.

<Example de="Eine Fahrkarte nach München, bitte." en="A ticket to Munich, please." />
<Example de="Einfach oder hin und zurück?" en="One way or return?" />
<Example de="Wann fährt der nächste Zug nach Köln?" en="When does the next train to Cologne leave?" />
<Example de="Der Zug fährt um zehn Uhr fünfzehn von Gleis drei ab." en="The train leaves at 10:15 from platform three." />
<Example de="Muss ich umsteigen?" en="Do I have to change?" />
<Example de="Wann kommt der Zug in Berlin an?" en="When does the train arrive in Berlin?" />

</Step>

<Step n="7" title="Announcements">

Station announcements follow a fixed pattern. Listen for three things: the destination, the platform, the time.

<Example de="Der Intercity nach Hamburg fährt heute von Gleis sieben." en="The Intercity to Hamburg leaves today from platform seven." />
<Example de="Der Zug aus München hat zwanzig Minuten Verspätung." en="The train from Munich is twenty minutes late." />
<Example de="Bitte einsteigen! Die Türen schließen." en="Please board! The doors are closing." />

*nach* tells you where a train is going; *aus* tells you where it is coming from. Mixing them up in the exam gives you the wrong train.

</Step>

## Compared with English

<Compare en="I am going **to** Berlin, **to** the doctor, **to** the cinema." de="Ich fahre **nach** Berlin, **zum** Arzt, **ins** Kino." note="One English 'to', three German words, chosen by the kind of destination." />

<Compare en="I am **in** the cinema. / I am going **into** the cinema." de="Ich bin **im** Kino. / Ich gehe **ins** Kino." note="English changes the preposition (in, into). German keeps in and changes the case." />

<Compare en="**by** bus, **by** train" de="**mit dem** Bus, **mit dem** Zug" note="German says 'with the bus' and needs the article in the dative." />

<Compare en="I **go** to work by car." de="Ich **fahre** mit dem Auto zur Arbeit." note="English 'go' covers everything. German says fahren whenever a vehicle is involved." />

## Typical mistakes

<Mistake wrong="Ich fahre **zu** Berlin." right="Ich fahre **nach** Berlin." why="Cities and countries without an article take nach." />

<Mistake wrong="Ich gehe **nach** Kino." right="Ich gehe **ins** Kino." why="nach is only for place names and 'nach Hause'. A building you go into takes in + accusative." />

<Mistake wrong="Ich gehe **im** Park." right="Ich gehe **in den** Park." why="gehen is movement towards a place, so in takes the accusative. 'im Park' would mean you are walking around inside it." />

<Mistake wrong="Ich fahre **mit den** Bus." right="Ich fahre **mit dem** Bus." why="mit takes the dative. Masculine der becomes dem, not den." />

<Mistake wrong="Ich **gehe** mit dem Zug nach Köln." right="Ich **fahre** mit dem Zug nach Köln." why="gehen means on foot. With a train you fahren." />

## Summary

<Summary>

| Destination | Word | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| city, country, home | **nach** | nach Berlin, nach Hause |
| person; a place you go up to | **zu** + dative | zu Anna, zum Arzt, zur Arbeit |
| a place you go into | **in** + accusative | ins Kino, in die Stadt, in den Park |

| | Wo? (dative) | Wohin? (accusative) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| der | im Park | in den Park |
| das | im Kino | ins Kino |
| die | in der Stadt | in die Stadt |

**Transport:** mit dem Bus / Zug / Auto / Fahrrad · mit der U-Bahn · zu Fuß.

**From:** aus (a place) · von (a person or point).

</Summary>
