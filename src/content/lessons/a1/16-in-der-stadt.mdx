You have been using small pieces of the dative for a while without being told: *in **der** Bäckerei*, *auf **dem** Markt*, *im Wohnzimmer*, *vor **dem** Kino*. This chapter explains them. It is your second case, and at A1 you need it for one job above all: saying **where** something is.

## The idea

German asks two different questions about place.

- **Wo?** Where is something? It is **at rest** in a location.
- **Wohin?** Where is something going? It is **moving** to a destination.

The answer to **Wo?** uses the **dative** case. (The answer to *Wohin?* is the subject of the next chapter.)

In the dative the articles change like this: **der → dem**, **das → dem**, **die → der**. Masculine and neuter share one form, and the feminine *die* turns into *der*. That last one looks odd, because *der* is normally the masculine article, but after a preposition of place it can only be the feminine dative.

## Step by step

<Step n="1" title="Places in town">

| der | die | das |
| --- | --- | --- |
| der Bahnhof (station) | die Bank (bank) | das Rathaus (town hall) |
| der Supermarkt | die Post (post office) | das Kino (cinema) |
| der Park | die Apotheke (pharmacy) | das Café |
| der Marktplatz (market square) | die Schule (school) | das Hotel |
| der Flughafen (airport) | die Kirche (church) | das Krankenhaus (hospital) |
| der Parkplatz (car park) | die Haltestelle (stop) | das Restaurant |
| | die Bibliothek (library) | das Museum |

<Example de="der Bahnhof, die Post, das Rathaus." en="the station, the post office, the town hall" />
<Example de="die Apotheke, die Haltestelle, das Krankenhaus." en="the pharmacy, the stop, the hospital" />

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="The dative articles">

| | Nominative | Dative | with ein |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| masculine | der Park | **dem** Park | ein**em** Park |
| neuter | das Kino | **dem** Kino | ein**em** Kino |
| feminine | die Post | **der** Post | ein**er** Post |
| plural | die Geschäfte | **den** Geschäfte**n** | |

Notice the letters: **m** for masculine and neuter, **r** for feminine, **n** for plural (where the noun itself adds an *-n* as well).

<Example de="Ich bin in **dem** Park." en="I am in the park." />
<Example de="Sie arbeitet in **der** Bank." en="She works at the bank." />
<Example de="Wir sind in **dem** Kino." en="We are in the cinema." />

</Step>

<Step n="3" title="The prepositions of place">

| Preposition | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **in** | in, inside | in der Bank |
| **an** | at, by, on (a vertical surface) | an der Haltestelle |
| **auf** | on (a horizontal surface); at (an open place) | auf dem Marktplatz |
| **neben** | next to | neben der Post |
| **vor** | in front of | vor dem Kino |
| **hinter** | behind | hinter dem Bahnhof |
| **zwischen** | between | zwischen der Bank und dem Café |
| **über** | above | über der Tür |
| **unter** | under | unter dem Tisch |

<Example de="Die Apotheke ist **neben der** Bank." en="The pharmacy is next to the bank." />
<Example de="Wir treffen uns **vor dem** Kino." en="We are meeting in front of the cinema." />
<Example de="Der Parkplatz ist **hinter dem** Supermarkt." en="The car park is behind the supermarket." />
<Example de="Das Café ist **zwischen der** Post **und dem** Hotel." en="The café is between the post office and the hotel." />

</Step>

<Step n="4" title="Short forms: im and am">

Two combinations are almost always shortened.

| Long | Short |
| --- | --- |
| in dem | **im** |
| an dem | **am** |

<Example de="Ich bin **im** Supermarkt." en="I am in the supermarket." />
<Example de="Die Kinder sind **im** Kino." en="The children are at the cinema." />
<Example de="Das Hotel ist **am** Bahnhof." en="The hotel is by the station." />

The feminine does not shorten: *in der Schule*, *an der Haltestelle*.

Now you can see where the time expressions from chapter 11 come from: **am** Montag is short for *an dem Montag*.

</Step>

<Step n="5" title="Asking where something is">

<Example de="Entschuldigung, wo ist die Post?" en="Excuse me, where is the post office?" />
<Example de="Gibt es hier eine Apotheke?" en="Is there a pharmacy here?" />
<Example de="Wo kann ich hier Brot kaufen?" en="Where can I buy bread here?" />
<Example de="Ist es weit?" en="Is it far?" />

Possible answers:

<Example de="Die Post ist gleich hier, neben dem Rathaus." en="The post office is right here, next to the town hall." />
<Example de="Nein, es ist nicht weit. Nur fünf Minuten zu Fuß." en="No, it is not far. Only five minutes on foot." />
<Example de="Tut mir leid, ich bin nicht von hier." en="I am sorry, I am not from here." />

</Step>

<Step n="6" title="Asking the way">

For the way *to* a place, German uses **zu** with the dative. It too has short forms.

| Long | Short | Used with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| zu dem | **zum** | masculine and neuter: zum Bahnhof, zum Kino |
| zu der | **zur** | feminine: zur Post, zur Schule |

<Example de="Wie komme ich **zum** Bahnhof?" en="How do I get to the station?" />
<Example de="Wie komme ich **zur** Post?" en="How do I get to the post office?" />

</Step>

<Step n="7" title="Understanding directions">

| German | English |
| --- | --- |
| geradeaus | straight on |
| links / rechts | left / right |
| die erste Straße links | the first street on the left |
| die zweite Straße rechts | the second street on the right |
| an der Ampel | at the traffic lights |
| an der Kreuzung | at the crossroads |
| bis zur Kirche | as far as the church |
| dann | then |

<Example de="Gehen Sie geradeaus bis zur Ampel." en="Go straight on as far as the traffic lights." />
<Example de="Dann gehen Sie links." en="Then go left." />
<Example de="Nehmen Sie die zweite Straße rechts." en="Take the second street on the right." />
<Example de="Der Bahnhof ist auf der linken Seite." en="The station is on the left-hand side." />

*Gehen Sie …* is a polite command. Chapter 18 looks at these forms properly; for now you only need to understand them.

</Step>

## Compared with English

<Compare en="in **the** park, in **the** bank" de="in **dem** Park, in **der** Bank" note="English 'the' never changes. German shows the dative on the article: dem for masculine and neuter, der for feminine." />

<Compare en="**at** the bus stop, **at** the market" de="**an** der Haltestelle, **auf** dem Markt" note="English 'at' has no single German equivalent. German picks an, auf or in according to the shape of the place." />

<Compare en="**to** the station, **to** the post office" de="**zum** Bahnhof, **zur** Post" note="German fuses the preposition and the article, and the short form shows the gender." />

<Compare en="next **to** the bank, in front **of** the cinema" de="neben der Bank, vor dem Kino" note="English often needs two words. German uses one preposition." />

## Typical mistakes

<Mistake wrong="Ich bin in **die** Bank." right="Ich bin in **der** Bank." why="Being somewhere answers Wo? and takes the dative. Feminine die becomes der." />

<Mistake wrong="Die Post ist neben **das** Rathaus." right="Die Post ist neben **dem** Rathaus." why="Neuter das becomes dem in the dative." />

<Mistake wrong="Wie komme ich **zu** Bahnhof?" right="Wie komme ich **zum** Bahnhof?" why="zu needs the article, and zu dem is shortened to zum." />

<Mistake wrong="Wie komme ich **zum** Post?" right="Wie komme ich **zur** Post?" why="Post is feminine, so zu der becomes zur." />

<Mistake wrong="Ich bin **in dem** Kino. (sounds stiff)" right="Ich bin **im** Kino." why="in dem and an dem are normally shortened to im and am." />

## Summary

<Summary>

| | Nominative | Dative | in + | an + | zu + |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| masculine | der | **dem** | im Park | am Bahnhof | zum Bahnhof |
| neuter | das | **dem** | im Kino | am Rathaus | zum Kino |
| feminine | die | **der** | in der Bank | an der Haltestelle | zur Post |

**Wo?** → dative. *Wo ist die Apotheke? Neben **der** Bank.*

**Prepositions of place:** in · an · auf · neben · vor · hinter · zwischen · über · unter.

**The way:** Wie komme ich zum / zur …? · geradeaus · links · rechts · die erste Straße links.

</Summary>
