The listening part of *Start Deutsch 1* comes first in the exam and lasts about **20 minutes**. There are **15 items** in three parts. The speakers talk clearly and fairly slowly, about everyday things, but you cannot stop the recording or ask anyone to repeat. Preparation here means training your ear for a small number of things: numbers, times, days and places.

## What this part tests

**Teil 1: six short conversations.** For each one you have a question and three possible answers, **a**, **b** or **c**, often shown as pictures in the real exam. You hear each conversation **twice**.

**Teil 2: four announcements**, the kind you hear at a station, an airport, in a shop or on the radio. For each there is one statement, and you decide **richtig** or **falsch**. You hear each announcement **once only**.

**Teil 3: five phone messages or short conversations.** Again a question with three possible answers. You hear each one **twice**.

In this course the recordings are spoken by your device's German voice, not by actors, and the app lets you play them as often as the exam allows. The real exam has several different human voices and some background noise. Before the exam, listen to at least one official practice paper to get used to that.

## How to approach it

<Step n="1" title="Use the pause: read the question first">

Before each recording there is a short pause. Use every second of it to read the question and the answers. Then you know **what kind of information** you are listening for.

| The question starts with | Listen for |
| --- | --- |
| Wann …? | a time, a day or a date |
| Wie viel kostet …? | a price |
| Wo …? | a place |
| Wohin …? | a destination |
| Was …? | a thing or an activity |
| Wer …? | a person |
| Welche Nummer …? | a number |

<Example de="Wann fährt der Zug?" en="When does the train leave?" />
<Example de="Wie viel kostet die Fahrkarte?" en="How much is the ticket?" />
<Example de="Wo treffen sich die Freunde?" en="Where are the friends meeting?" />

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="Expect more than one candidate">

The speakers nearly always mention **two or three** of the possible answers. One is right; the others are there to test you. Typical patterns:

- A first suggestion is **rejected**: *Am Samstag? Nein, da kann ich nicht. Am Sonntag.*
- Something is **corrected**: *Nicht Gleis fünf, sondern Gleis acht.*
- Something **changes**: *Der Kurs ist heute nicht um neun, sondern um zehn Uhr.*

So do not mark the first answer you hear. Wait for the end, and listen for the words that cancel or confirm.

| Cancels what came before | Confirms |
| --- | --- |
| nein, nicht, leider | ja, gut, in Ordnung |
| aber, sondern | genau, stimmt |
| das geht nicht | das geht, das passt |
| lieber | einverstanden |

<Example de="Am Samstag kann ich leider nicht. Geht es am Sonntag?" en="I cannot on Saturday, unfortunately. Would Sunday work?" />
<Example de="Nicht um neun, sondern um zehn Uhr." en="Not at nine, but at ten o'clock." />

</Step>

<Step n="3" title="Numbers: write them down">

Numbers are the hardest thing to hold in your head. Write down every number you hear in the margin, as figures. Then compare with the answers.

Remember the two traps from chapter 3 and chapter 11:

- **Units before tens.** *fünfundvierzig* is 45, not 54. Write the 5 on the right first.
- **halb.** *halb acht* is 7:30, not 8:30.

<Example de="Der Zug fährt um siebzehn Uhr fünfundvierzig." en="The train leaves at 17:45." />
<Example de="Wir treffen uns um halb acht." en="We are meeting at half past seven." />
<Example de="Das macht dreiundzwanzig Euro fünfzig." en="That comes to €23.50." />

Pairs that sound alike and are worth practising: *dreizehn* and *dreißig*, *vierzehn* and *vierzig*, *sechzehn* and *sechzig*. The teens are stressed on the end, the tens on the beginning.

</Step>

<Step n="4" title="Teil 2: once only">

Because you hear each announcement only once, read the statement very carefully beforehand. Decide what would make it true and what would make it false. Then listen for exactly that.

Announcements follow fixed patterns:

<Example de="Achtung, eine Durchsage: Der Zug nach Köln fährt heute von Gleis vier." en="Attention, an announcement: the train to Cologne leaves today from platform four." />
<Example de="Liebe Kunden, heute im Angebot: Äpfel, das Kilo nur ein Euro neunundneunzig." en="Dear customers, on offer today: apples, only €1.99 a kilo." />
<Example de="Der Flug nach Wien hat dreißig Minuten Verspätung." en="The flight to Vienna is thirty minutes late." />

</Step>

<Step n="5" title="Teil 3: messages">

A phone message has a standard shape: who is calling, what has changed or what they want, and what you should do.

<Example de="Hallo, hier ist Anna." en="Hello, this is Anna." />
<Example de="Ich kann heute leider nicht kommen." en="Unfortunately I cannot come today." />
<Example de="Ruf mich bitte zurück." en="Please call me back." />
<Example de="Meine Nummer ist …" en="My number is …" />

The question usually asks about the middle part: what has changed, or what the caller wants.

</Step>

<Step n="6" title="When you miss something">

You will miss words. Everyone does. Three rules:

1. **Do not stop to think** about a word you did not catch. The recording goes on, and the answer may be in the next sentence.
2. In Teil 1 and Teil 3, use the **first hearing** to get the general idea and the **second** to check the detail.
3. If you still do not know, **guess**. Never leave an item blank.

</Step>

## Summary

<Summary>

| Part | What you hear | Task | Times | Items |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Teil 1 | short conversations | a, b or c | twice | 6 |
| Teil 2 | announcements | richtig or falsch | **once** | 4 |
| Teil 3 | phone messages | a, b or c | twice | 5 |

**Method:** read the question in the pause · know what kind of fact you need · write numbers down · wait for the end before you choose.

**Listen for the turn:** nein · nicht · leider · aber · sondern · lieber.

**Traps:** fünfundvierzig = 45 · halb acht = 7:30 · nach Köln (going to) and aus Köln (coming from).

</Summary>
