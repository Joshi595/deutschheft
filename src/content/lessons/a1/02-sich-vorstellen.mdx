In the A1 speaking exam the first task is always the same: *Stellen Sie sich bitte vor.* Please introduce yourself. You get a card with cue words (name, age, country, where you live, languages, job, hobby) and say a sentence for each. This chapter gives you the first four.

## The idea

An introduction is built from four short sentences, and each uses a different verb.

<Example de="Ich **heiße** Maria Rossi." en="My name is Maria Rossi." />
<Example de="Ich **komme** aus Italien." en="I come from Italy." />
<Example de="Ich **wohne** in München." en="I live in Munich." />
<Example de="Ich **bin** Studentin." en="I am a student." />

Notice that the verb ends in **-e** every time the subject is *ich*. That is the heart of German verbs: **the ending of the verb changes with the person**. English does this only once (*I come, he comes*). German does it for every person. In this chapter you meet three endings; chapter 4 completes the set.

## Step by step

<Step n="1" title="The people: ich, du, Sie, er, sie">

| German | English | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ich** | I | written small, unlike English *I* |
| **du** | you | one person, informal |
| **Sie** | you | formal, always with a capital S |
| **er** | he | |
| **sie** | she | with a small s |

*Sie* and *sie* sound the same. In writing the capital letter tells them apart; in speech the verb ending does, as you will see below.

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="Three regular verbs: heißen, kommen, wohnen">

Take the infinitive, remove **-en**, and you have the stem: *komm-*, *wohn-*, *heiß-*. Then add the ending for the person.

| | kommen (to come) | wohnen (to live) | heißen (to be called) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ich | komm**e** | wohn**e** | heiß**e** |
| du | komm**st** | wohn**st** | heiß**t** |
| er / sie | komm**t** | wohn**t** | heiß**t** |
| Sie | komm**en** | wohn**en** | heiß**en** |

The *Sie* form is always the same as the infinitive, which makes formal German the easy option.

*heißen* has one small irregularity: the stem already ends in an s-sound, so *du* adds only **-t** (*du heißt*, not *du heißst*).

<Example de="Ich komme aus Polen." en="I come from Poland." />
<Example de="Du kommst aus Spanien." en="You come from Spain." />
<Example de="Er kommt aus der Türkei." en="He comes from Turkey." />
<Example de="Sie wohnt in Berlin." en="She lives in Berlin." />

</Step>

<Step n="3" title="The verb sein (to be)">

*sein* is irregular in every language, and German is no exception. There is no pattern here; these forms simply have to be learned.

| | sein | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ich | **bin** | I am |
| du | **bist** | you are |
| er / sie | **ist** | he / she is |
| Sie | **sind** | you are (formal) |

<Example de="Ich bin Maria." en="I am Maria." />
<Example de="Bist du Tom?" en="Are you Tom?" />
<Example de="Das ist Herr Schmidt." en="That is Mr Schmidt." />
<Example de="Sind Sie Frau Weber?" en="Are you Mrs Weber?" />

**Das ist …** is how you introduce someone else, whether a man or a woman.

</Step>

<Step n="4" title="Questions with a question word">

The question word comes first and the **verb comes second**, straight after it. The subject follows the verb.

| Question word | Meaning | Formal | Informal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Wie** | how | Wie heißen Sie? | Wie heißt du? |
| **Woher** | where from | Woher kommen Sie? | Woher kommst du? |
| **Wo** | where | Wo wohnen Sie? | Wo wohnst du? |
| **Wer** | who | Wer sind Sie? | Wer bist du? |
| **Was** | what | Was sind Sie von Beruf? | Was bist du von Beruf? |

<Example de="**Wie** heißen Sie?" en="What is your name?" />
<Example de="**Woher** kommst du?" en="Where do you come from?" />
<Example de="**Wo** wohnen Sie?" en="Where do you live?" />
<Example de="**Wer** ist das?" en="Who is that?" />

*Wo* asks for a place where something is. *Woher* asks for an origin. Do not mix them up: *Wo kommst du?* is not German.

</Step>

<Step n="5" title="aus and in">

Where you are from takes **aus**. Where you live takes **in**.

<Example de="Ich komme **aus** Indien." en="I come from India." />
<Example de="Ich wohne **in** Hamburg." en="I live in Hamburg." />

Most countries have no article: *aus Italien, aus Indien, aus Deutschland*. A few do, and then the article appears after *aus*:

| Country | from … |
| --- | --- |
| die Schweiz | aus **der** Schweiz |
| die Türkei | aus **der** Türkei |
| die USA (plural) | aus **den** USA |
| der Iran | aus **dem** Iran |

You do not need to understand yet why the article changes. Learn these four as fixed expressions.

</Step>

<Step n="6" title="Languages">

For languages you need one more verb, **sprechen** (to speak). For now learn two forms: *ich spreche* and *Sie sprechen*.

<Example de="Ich spreche Englisch und ein bisschen Deutsch." en="I speak English and a little German." />
<Example de="Welche Sprachen sprechen Sie?" en="Which languages do you speak?" />

The names of languages are written with a capital letter and mostly end in **-isch**: Englisch, Spanisch, Französisch, Italienisch, Türkisch, Russisch, Arabisch, Polnisch. *Deutsch* is the exception.

</Step>

<Step n="7" title="Putting it together">

A complete introduction, as you would give it in the exam:

<Example de="Guten Tag. Ich heiße Maria Rossi." en="Hello. My name is Maria Rossi." />
<Example de="Ich komme aus Italien, aus Rom." en="I come from Italy, from Rome." />
<Example de="Jetzt wohne ich in München." en="Now I live in Munich." />
<Example de="Ich spreche Italienisch, Englisch und ein bisschen Deutsch." en="I speak Italian, English and a little German." />

In the third sentence the subject *ich* stands after the verb. The sentence begins with *Jetzt* (now), and since the verb has to stay in second place, *ich* moves behind it. Chapter 8 explains this rule fully.

</Step>

## Compared with English

<Compare en="My name **is** Maria." de="Ich **heiße** Maria." note="German has a verb of its own for this: heißen, 'to be called'. 'Mein Name ist Maria' is also correct, and more formal." />

<Compare en="Where **do** you live?" de="Wo **wohnst** du?" note="German has no helper verb like 'do' in questions. The main verb itself moves to second place." />

<Compare en="I **am living** in Berlin. / I **live** in Berlin." de="Ich **wohne** in Berlin." note="German has only one present tense. 'Ich wohne' covers both 'I live' and 'I am living'." />

<Compare en="Where are you **from**?" de="**Woher** kommst du?" note="English puts 'from' at the end. German packs it into the question word: wo + her = woher." />

## Typical mistakes

<Mistake wrong="Ich **bin** Maria Rossi heißen." right="Ich **heiße** Maria Rossi." why="Use either 'Ich heiße Maria' or 'Ich bin Maria'. Never both verbs in one sentence." />

<Mistake wrong="Ich **komme von** Italien." right="Ich **komme aus** Italien." why="Your country or city of origin always takes aus, not von." />

<Mistake wrong="Wie heißt Sie?" right="Wie heißen Sie?" why="The formal Sie always takes the -en form of the verb, the same as the infinitive." />

<Mistake wrong="Wo kommst du?" right="Woher kommst du?" why="Wo asks where something is. Asking for someone's origin needs woher." />

<Mistake wrong="Ich wohne in Berlin **jetzt**." right="**Jetzt** wohne ich in Berlin." why="'Ich wohne jetzt in Berlin' is also fine. What German does not do is put the time word at the very end, as English often does." />

## Summary

<Summary>

| | heißen | kommen | wohnen | sein |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ich | heiße | komme | wohne | bin |
| du | heißt | kommst | wohnst | bist |
| er / sie | heißt | kommt | wohnt | ist |
| Sie | heißen | kommen | wohnen | sind |

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Wie heißen Sie? / Wie heißt du? | Ich heiße … |
| Woher kommen Sie? / Woher kommst du? | Ich komme aus … |
| Wo wohnen Sie? / Wo wohnst du? | Ich wohne in … |
| Welche Sprachen sprechen Sie? | Ich spreche … |

**Remember:** question word first, verb second. *aus* for origin, *in* for where you live.

</Summary>
