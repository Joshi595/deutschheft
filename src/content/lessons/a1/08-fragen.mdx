In part two of the A1 speaking exam you draw a card with a word on it, such as *Wohnort* or *Sonntag*, and you have to **ask** another candidate a question about it. So you need to produce questions on demand. That depends on one rule, the most important word-order rule in German.

## The idea

**In a statement, the conjugated verb is always the second element.**

Not the second *word*: the second *element*. The first element can be the subject, a time, a place, or a whole phrase of several words. Whatever it is, the verb comes next, and if the subject was not first, it goes straight after the verb.

Questions simply move the verb. In a question with a question word, the question word is the first element and the verb is still second. In a yes/no question the first position is left empty, so the verb comes first.

That is the entire system. The rest of this chapter is practice and vocabulary.

## Step by step

<Step n="1" title="Statements: verb second">

| 1 | 2 (verb) | rest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ich | **lerne** | heute Deutsch. |
| Heute | **lerne** | ich Deutsch. |
| Deutsch | **lerne** | ich heute. |

All three sentences are correct and mean the same; the first position carries the emphasis. Look at what happens to *ich*: as soon as something else takes position 1, *ich* moves behind the verb. This swap is called **inversion**.

<Example de="Ich **wohne** jetzt in Köln." en="I live in Cologne now." />
<Example de="Jetzt **wohne** ich in Köln." en="Now I live in Cologne." />
<Example de="In Köln **wohne** ich seit einem Jahr." en="I have lived in Cologne for a year." />

*In Köln* is two words but one element, so the verb is still second.

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="und, aber, oder do not count">

The joining words **und** (and), **aber** (but), **oder** (or) and **denn** (because) stand in position zero. The sentence after them starts counting from one again.

<Example de="Ich komme aus Indien, **aber** ich **wohne** in Köln." en="I come from India, but I live in Cologne." />
<Example de="Er lernt Deutsch **und** sie **lernt** Englisch." en="He is learning German and she is learning English." />

</Step>

<Step n="3" title="Questions with a question word">

Question word first, verb second, subject third.

| 1 | 2 (verb) | 3 | rest |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wo | **wohnen** | Sie? | |
| Was | **machst** | du | heute? |
| Wann | **kommt** | der Bus? | |

<Example de="**Wo** wohnen Sie?" en="Where do you live?" />
<Example de="**Was** machst du heute?" en="What are you doing today?" />
<Example de="**Wann** kommt der Bus?" en="When does the bus come?" />

</Step>

<Step n="4" title="The question words">

All German question words start with **w** (pronounced *v*), which is why these are called *W-Fragen*.

| German | English | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **wer** | who | Wer ist das? |
| **was** | what | Was ist das? |
| **wo** | where (location) | Wo wohnst du? |
| **woher** | where from | Woher kommst du? |
| **wohin** | where to | Wohin gehst du? |
| **wann** | when | Wann kommst du? |
| **wie** | how | Wie heißt du? |
| **warum** | why | Warum lernst du Deutsch? |
| **wie viel** | how much | Wie viel kostet das? |
| **wie viele** | how many | Wie viele Kinder haben Sie? |
| **wie lange** | how long | Wie lange lernst du schon Deutsch? |
| **welcher / welche / welches** | which | Welche Sprachen sprechen Sie? |

Two false friends for English speakers: **wer** means *who*, not *where*, and **wo** means *where*, not *who*.

<Example de="**Wer** ist das? Das ist mein Bruder." en="Who is that? That is my brother." />
<Example de="**Wohin** gehst du? Nach Hause." en="Where are you going? Home." />
<Example de="**Wie viele** Geschwister hast du?" en="How many siblings do you have?" />

</Step>

<Step n="5" title="Yes/no questions: verb first">

Take a statement and move the verb to the front. Nothing else changes, and nothing is added.

| Statement | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Du **kommst** aus Polen. | **Kommst** du aus Polen? |
| Sie **haben** Kinder. | **Haben** Sie Kinder? |
| Er **ist** verheiratet. | **Ist** er verheiratet? |

<Example de="**Sprechen** Sie Englisch?" en="Do you speak English?" />
<Example de="**Hast** du heute Zeit?" en="Do you have time today?" />
<Example de="**Wohnt** ihr in Berlin?" en="Do you live in Berlin?" />

Your voice goes **up** at the end of a yes/no question and **down** at the end of a question with a question word.

</Step>

<Step n="6" title="ja, nein and doch">

To a positive question you answer **ja** or **nein**.

<Example de="Kommst du aus Polen? **Ja**, ich komme aus Polen." en="Are you from Poland? Yes, I am." />
<Example de="Kommst du aus Polen? **Nein**, ich komme aus Ungarn." en="Are you from Poland? No, I am from Hungary." />

When the question is **negative** (it contains *nicht* or *kein*) and you want to contradict it, the answer is neither *ja* nor *nein* but **doch**.

<Example de="Kommst du **nicht** aus Polen? **Doch**, ich komme aus Polen." en="Aren't you from Poland? Yes, I am." />
<Example de="Hast du **keine** Zeit? **Doch**, ich habe Zeit." en="Don't you have time? Yes, I do." />
<Example de="Hast du **keine** Zeit? **Nein**, ich habe keine Zeit." en="Don't you have time? No, I don't." />

| Question | Agree | Contradict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| positive: *Hast du Zeit?* | Ja. | Nein. |
| negative: *Hast du keine Zeit?* | Nein. | **Doch.** |

</Step>

<Step n="7" title="Short answers">

German does not use short answers like English *Yes, I do*. You answer with *ja*, *nein* or *doch* alone, or you repeat the whole sentence.

<Example de="Sprechen Sie Deutsch? Ja, ein bisschen." en="Do you speak German? Yes, a little." />

</Step>

## Compared with English

<Compare en="Today I **learn** German. (subject, then verb)" de="Heute **lerne** ich Deutsch. (verb, then subject)" note="English keeps subject before verb even when a time comes first. German keeps the verb second, so the subject has to move." />

<Compare en="**Do** you speak English?" de="**Sprechen** Sie Englisch?" note="English needs the helper 'do'. German just puts the main verb first." />

<Compare en="Where **do** you live?" de="Wo **wohnen** Sie?" note="Again no 'do'. Question word, then the verb itself." />

<Compare en="Don't you have time? **Yes**, I do." de="Hast du keine Zeit? **Doch**, ich habe Zeit." note="English uses 'yes' for both. German has a special word for 'yes' after a negative question." />

## Typical mistakes

<Mistake wrong="Heute **ich lerne** Deutsch." right="Heute **lerne ich** Deutsch." why="The verb must be second. When Heute is first, ich moves behind the verb." />

<Mistake wrong="Wo **du wohnst**?" right="Wo **wohnst du**?" why="After the question word comes the verb, then the subject." />

<Mistake wrong="**Tust** du sprechen Deutsch?" right="**Sprichst** du Deutsch?" why="German has no helper verb for questions. The main verb moves to the front." />

<Mistake wrong="Hast du keinen Hunger? **Ja**, ich habe Hunger." right="Hast du keinen Hunger? **Doch**, ich habe Hunger." why="To contradict a negative question you need doch." />

<Mistake wrong="**Wer** wohnst du?" right="**Wo** wohnst du?" why="wo = where, wer = who. They are the opposite of what an English speaker expects." />

## Summary

<Summary>

| Sentence type | Position 1 | Position 2 | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| statement | anything | **verb** | Heute **lerne** ich Deutsch. |
| W-question | question word | **verb** | Wo **wohnst** du? |
| yes/no question | **verb** | subject | **Wohnst** du in Köln? |

**Question words:** wer (who) · was (what) · wo (where) · woher (where from) · wohin (where to) · wann (when) · wie (how) · warum (why) · wie viel (how much) · wie viele (how many) · welche (which).

**Answers:** positive question → ja / nein. Negative question → nein (agree) / **doch** (contradict).

</Summary>
