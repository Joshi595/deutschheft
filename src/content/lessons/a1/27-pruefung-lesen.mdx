The reading part of *Start Deutsch 1* lasts **25 minutes** and has **15 items** in three parts. Every item is worth the same. You will meet words you do not know, and that is intended: the exam tests whether you can find a piece of information, not whether you understand every word.

## What this part tests

**Teil 1: two short personal texts** (a note, an email, a postcard) with five statements. For each statement you decide **richtig** or **falsch**.

**Teil 2: five situations**, each with two short texts side by side: adverts, website extracts or notices. You decide which of the two, **a** or **b**, fits the situation.

**Teil 3: five signs or notices**, such as you see on a shop door or a notice board. For each there is one statement, and again you decide **richtig** or **falsch**.

All the texts are short, and all are the kind of thing you really meet in daily life: opening hours, a message from a friend, an advert for a flat, a sign at a station.

## How to approach it

<Step n="1" title="Read the question before the text">

Always read the statement or the situation **first**. Then you read the text with a purpose: you are looking for one fact. Reading the text first and the question second costs time and attention.

Underline the **key words** in the question: usually a time, a day, a place, a price, or a negative.

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="Look for the same idea in other words">

The text almost never uses the same words as the question. It says the same thing differently. Learn to spot these pairs.

| In the question | In the text |
| --- | --- |
| am Wochenende | am Samstag und Sonntag |
| jeden Tag | täglich, Montag bis Sonntag |
| geschlossen | nicht geöffnet, Ruhetag |
| billig | günstig, im Angebot, nur 5 Euro |
| kostenlos | gratis, Sie zahlen nichts |
| Man braucht einen Termin. | nur nach Anmeldung |
| nicht erlaubt | verboten, bitte nicht … |
| am Vormittag | von 9 bis 12 Uhr |
| später kommen | Verspätung haben |

<Example de="Montag Ruhetag." en="Closed on Mondays." />
<Example de="Eintritt frei." en="Admission free." />
<Example de="Nur nach Anmeldung." en="By appointment only." />
<Example de="Täglich geöffnet." en="Open daily." />

</Step>

<Step n="3" title="Watch the small words">

A single small word can turn a statement around. These are the ones the exam uses to make a statement false:

| Word | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| nicht, kein | not, no |
| nur | only |
| auch | also |
| außer | except |
| ab | from (a time or date onwards) |
| bis | until |
| vor / nach | before / after |
| noch | still |
| schon | already |

<Example de="Geöffnet täglich **außer** Montag." en="Open every day except Monday." />
<Example de="**Ab** 18 Uhr geschlossen." en="Closed from 6 pm." />
<Example de="**Nur** für Kunden." en="For customers only." />

If the sign says *außer Montag* and the statement says *Am Montag ist das Geschäft geöffnet*, the answer is **falsch**.

</Step>

<Step n="4" title="Teil 1: one statement, one place in the text">

The statements follow the order of the text. The answer to statement 1 comes before the answer to statement 2. Find the sentence in the text that deals with the statement, read it carefully, and compare.

A statement is **falsch** as soon as one detail is wrong: the right person on the wrong day is false.

</Step>

<Step n="5" title="Teil 2: compare a and b on one point">

Each situation contains one deciding detail: you want to learn German **in the evening**, or you are looking for a flat **with a garden**. Find that detail, then check each text for it. Usually one text clearly matches and the other clearly fails on exactly that point. Both texts will be about the right general topic, so the topic alone tells you nothing.

</Step>

<Step n="6" title="Teil 3: signs are short, so every word counts">

A sign has perhaps ten words. Read all of them. Look especially at days, times and the small words from step 3. Then read the statement again and ask one precise question: *is this allowed, open, possible, on that day, at that time?*

</Step>

<Step n="7" title="Time and guessing">

You have 25 minutes for 15 items, which is plenty. A sensible plan is 8 minutes for each part, leaving a minute to check that every item has an answer.

**Never leave an item blank.** There is no penalty for a wrong answer, and every item has only two possibilities, so a guess is right half the time.

At the end of the real exam you copy your answers onto a separate answer sheet. Leave time for that, and copy carefully.

</Step>

## Summary

<Summary>

| Part | Texts | Task | Items |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Teil 1 | two short messages | richtig or falsch | 5 |
| Teil 2 | two adverts for each situation | a or b | 5 |
| Teil 3 | signs and notices | richtig or falsch | 5 |

**Method:** question first · underline the key words · find the same idea in other words · check the small words (nicht, nur, außer, ab, bis).

**Rules:** one wrong detail makes a statement falsch · never leave a blank · 25 minutes, about 8 for each part.
