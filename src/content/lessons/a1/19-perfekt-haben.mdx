So far everything has been in the present. Now you learn to talk about what has already happened: what you did yesterday, where you worked before, what you bought. In spoken German, and in the short messages of the writing exam, the tense for this is the **Perfekt**.

## The idea

The Perfekt is built from two parts, and by now the shape will be familiar:

**a form of haben in position 2 + a past participle at the very end.**

*Ich **habe** gestern Pizza **gegessen**.*

It is the sentence bracket again, the same one modal verbs and separable verbs use. The helper verb is conjugated and sits second; the word that carries the meaning waits at the end.

English has the same construction (*I **have eaten***), but uses it differently. In German the Perfekt is simply the normal spoken past. *Ich habe gegessen* covers both *I ate* and *I have eaten*. That makes your life easier: for conversation, this one tense is enough.

Most verbs form the Perfekt with *haben*. A smaller group uses *sein* instead; that is the next chapter.

## Step by step

<Step n="1" title="The shape of the sentence">

| 1 | 2: haben | middle | end: participle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ich | **habe** | gestern Deutsch | **gelernt**. |
| Wir | **haben** | Pizza | **gegessen**. |
| Gestern | **hat** | er lange | **gearbeitet**. |

<Example de="Ich **habe** gestern Deutsch **gelernt**." en="I learned German yesterday." />
<Example de="Wir **haben** Pizza **gegessen**." en="We ate pizza." />
<Example de="Was **hast** du am Wochenende **gemacht**?" en="What did you do at the weekend?" />

Only *haben* changes with the person. The participle is the same for everyone.

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="Regular verbs: ge- + stem + -t">

Take the stem, put **ge-** in front and **-t** behind.

| Infinitive | Stem | Participle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| machen | mach | **ge**mach**t** |
| lernen | lern | **ge**lern**t** |
| kaufen | kauf | **ge**kauf**t** |
| spielen | spiel | **ge**spiel**t** |
| wohnen | wohn | **ge**wohn**t** |
| kochen | koch | **ge**koch**t** |
| hören | hör | **ge**hör**t** |
| fragen | frag | **ge**frag**t** |

Stems ending in **-t** or **-d** add **-et**, as they do in the present: arbeiten → gearbeit**et**, warten → gewart**et**, kosten → gekost**et**.

<Example de="Ich habe ein Buch gekauft." en="I bought a book." />
<Example de="Sie hat in Berlin gewohnt." en="She lived in Berlin." />
<Example de="Wir haben Musik gehört." en="We listened to music." />
<Example de="Er hat den ganzen Tag gearbeitet." en="He worked all day." />

</Step>

<Step n="3" title="Irregular verbs: ge- + stem + -en">

The common old verbs end in **-en**, and often change their vowel. There is no way to work these out; they have to be learned, like English *eat, ate, eaten*.

| Infinitive | Participle | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| essen | **gegessen** | eaten |
| trinken | **getrunken** | drunk |
| lesen | **gelesen** | read |
| sehen | **gesehen** | seen |
| schreiben | **geschrieben** | written |
| sprechen | **gesprochen** | spoken |
| nehmen | **genommen** | taken |
| treffen | **getroffen** | met |
| finden | **gefunden** | found |
| schlafen | **geschlafen** | slept |
| helfen | **geholfen** | helped |
| geben | **gegeben** | given |

<Example de="Ich habe einen Kaffee getrunken." en="I drank a coffee." />
<Example de="Hast du den Film gesehen?" en="Have you seen the film?" />
<Example de="Sie hat mir eine E-Mail geschrieben." en="She wrote me an email." />
<Example de="Wir haben lange geschlafen." en="We slept late." />

A few verbs mix the two patterns: an irregular stem with a **-t** ending. The ones to know now are bringen → **gebracht**, denken → **gedacht**, wissen → **gewusst**, kennen → **gekannt**.

</Step>

<Step n="4" title="Verbs without ge-">

Two groups form the participle without **ge-**.

**Verbs ending in -ieren.** They are regular and end in **-t**.

| Infinitive | Participle |
| --- | --- |
| studieren | studiert |
| telefonieren | telefoniert |
| fotografieren | fotografiert |
| reparieren | repariert |

**Verbs with a prefix that never separates:** *be-, ver-, er-, ent-, ge-, zer-*.

| Infinitive | Participle |
| --- | --- |
| besuchen | besucht |
| bezahlen | bezahlt |
| verkaufen | verkauft |
| erzählen | erzählt |
| verstehen | verstanden |
| vergessen | vergessen |
| bekommen | bekommen |
| beginnen | begonnen |

<Example de="Ich habe meine Eltern besucht." en="I visited my parents." />
<Example de="Er hat mit seiner Mutter telefoniert." en="He talked to his mother on the phone." />
<Example de="Hast du das verstanden?" en="Did you understand that?" />
<Example de="Ich habe den Schlüssel vergessen." en="I have forgotten the key." />

</Step>

<Step n="5" title="Separable verbs: ge- in the middle">

The prefix stays at the front, and **ge-** slips in between the prefix and the verb. The result is one word.

| Infinitive | Participle |
| --- | --- |
| einkaufen | ein**ge**kauft |
| aufmachen | auf**ge**macht |
| anrufen | an**ge**rufen |
| fernsehen | fern**ge**sehen |
| anfangen | an**ge**fangen |
| mitbringen | mit**ge**bracht |

<Example de="Ich habe im Supermarkt eingekauft." en="I did the shopping at the supermarket." />
<Example de="Sie hat ihre Mutter angerufen." en="She phoned her mother." />
<Example de="Wir haben am Abend ferngesehen." en="We watched TV in the evening." />

</Step>

<Step n="6" title="Questions and negatives">

Nothing new: the verb in position 2 (or 1) is *haben*, and *nicht* stands before the participle.

<Example de="**Hast** du schon **gegessen**?" en="Have you eaten yet?" />
<Example de="Was **haben** Sie gestern **gemacht**?" en="What did you do yesterday?" />
<Example de="Ich habe das **nicht** verstanden." en="I did not understand that." />
<Example de="Er hat **noch nicht** bezahlt." en="He has not paid yet." />

</Step>

<Step n="7" title="Words for the past">

| German | English |
| --- | --- |
| gestern | yesterday |
| vorgestern | the day before yesterday |
| heute Morgen | this morning |
| letzte Woche | last week |
| letztes Jahr | last year |
| am Wochenende | at the weekend |
| vor zwei Tagen | two days ago |
| schon | already |
| noch nicht | not yet |
| früher | in the past, earlier |

<Example de="**Gestern** habe ich lange gearbeitet." en="Yesterday I worked late." />
<Example de="**Vor zwei Jahren** habe ich in Wien gewohnt." en="Two years ago I lived in Vienna." />

When the time word comes first, *haben* is still second and the subject follows it.

</Step>

## Compared with English

<Compare en="I **ate**. / I **have eaten**." de="Ich **habe gegessen**." note="English has two past tenses and strict rules for choosing between them. Spoken German uses the Perfekt for both." />

<Compare en="I have **eaten** a pizza." de="Ich habe eine Pizza **gegessen**." note="English keeps 'have' and 'eaten' together. German puts the participle at the very end." />

<Compare en="**Did** you see the film?" de="**Hast** du den Film gesehen?" note="English uses 'did'. German uses haben, in first place." />

<Compare en="work**ed**, play**ed**; eat**en**, writt**en**" de="gearbeit**et**, gespiel**t**; gegess**en**, geschrieb**en**" note="Both languages have a regular pattern and an irregular one, and the irregular verbs are largely the same old everyday verbs." />

## Typical mistakes

<Mistake wrong="Ich habe **gegessen** eine Pizza." right="Ich habe eine Pizza **gegessen**." why="The participle goes to the end of the sentence." />

<Mistake wrong="Ich habe ein Buch **gekaufen**." right="Ich habe ein Buch **gekauft**." why="kaufen is regular, so its participle ends in -t." />

<Mistake wrong="Ich habe meine Mutter **gebesucht**." right="Ich habe meine Mutter **besucht**." why="Verbs with the prefix be- take no ge-." />

<Mistake wrong="Ich habe **geeinkauft**." right="Ich habe **eingekauft**." why="In a separable verb, ge- goes between the prefix and the stem." />

<Mistake wrong="Er **habe** gestern gearbeitet." right="Er **hat** gestern gearbeitet." why="haben must agree with the subject: er hat." />

## Summary

<Summary>

**Perfekt = haben (position 2) + participle (end).**

| Type | Pattern | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| regular | ge + stem + t | gemacht, gekauft, gearbeitet |
| irregular | ge + stem + en | gegessen, gesehen, geschrieben |
| -ieren | stem + t, no ge- | studiert, telefoniert |
| be-, ver-, er- … | no ge- | besucht, verstanden, vergessen |
| separable | prefix + ge + … | eingekauft, angerufen |

**Learn by heart:** gegessen · getrunken · gelesen · gesehen · geschrieben · gesprochen · genommen · getroffen · gefunden · geschlafen · geholfen · gegeben.

**Time words:** gestern · letzte Woche · am Wochenende · vor zwei Tagen · schon · noch nicht.

</Summary>
