Numbers are everywhere in the A1 exam: phone numbers and prices in the listening part, your age and house number on the form you fill in, and times and dates in almost every text. They are worth learning until they come without thinking.

## The idea

German numbers follow a tidy system with one twist. Up to twelve, each number has its own word. From thirteen to nineteen you say the unit and then *zehn* (ten), much like English *thir-teen*. The twist comes at twenty-one: German says the **unit first and the ten second**, joined by *und*. Twenty-one is *einundzwanzig*, literally "one and twenty".

That is the opposite of English, and it is the single thing learners trip over. Everything else in this chapter is vocabulary.

## Step by step

<Step n="1" title="0 to 12">

| | | | |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 **null** | 1 **eins** | 2 **zwei** | 3 **drei** |
| 4 **vier** | 5 **fünf** | 6 **sechs** | 7 **sieben** |
| 8 **acht** | 9 **neun** | 10 **zehn** | 11 **elf** |
| 12 **zwölf** | | | |

<Example de="null, eins, zwei, drei, vier, fünf, sechs." />
<Example de="sieben, acht, neun, zehn, elf, zwölf." />

Remember the sound rules from chapter 1: the **v** in *vier* is an *f*, the **z** in *zwei* and *zehn* is *ts*, and the **ei** in *eins, zwei, drei* sounds like "eye".

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="13 to 19">

Unit + **zehn**. Two of them shorten the unit: 16 drops the **s** of *sechs*, and 17 drops the **en** of *sieben*.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| 13 dreizehn | 17 **sieb**zehn |
| 14 vierzehn | 18 achtzehn |
| 15 fünfzehn | 19 neunzehn |
| 16 **sech**zehn | |

<Example de="dreizehn, vierzehn, fünfzehn, sechzehn, siebzehn, achtzehn, neunzehn." />

</Step>

<Step n="3" title="The tens">

Unit + **zig**. The same two shorten again, and two more are irregular.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| 20 **zwan**zig | 60 **sech**zig |
| 30 drei**ßig** | 70 **sieb**zig |
| 40 vierzig | 80 achtzig |
| 50 fünfzig | 90 neunzig |

*Zwanzig* does not start with *zwei*, and *dreißig* is the only one written with **ß** instead of z.

<Example de="zwanzig, dreißig, vierzig, fünfzig, sechzig, siebzig, achtzig, neunzig." />

</Step>

<Step n="4" title="21 to 99: the unit comes first">

Say the unit, then **und**, then the ten, and write it all as one word.

| Number | German | Literally |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 21 | **ein**undzwanzig | one and twenty |
| 34 | vierunddreißig | four and thirty |
| 47 | siebenundvierzig | seven and forty |
| 68 | achtundsechzig | eight and sixty |
| 99 | neunundneunzig | nine and ninety |

*eins* loses its **s** inside a longer number: **ein**undzwanzig, not *einsundzwanzig*.

<Example de="einundzwanzig, vierunddreißig, siebenundvierzig." en="21, 34, 47" />
<Example de="achtundsechzig, neunundneunzig." en="68, 99" />

When you write down a number you hear, write the **second digit first**. You hear *sieben…* and write a 7 on the right, then *…undvierzig* and put the 4 in front of it.

</Step>

<Step n="5" title="Hundreds and thousands">

| | |
| --- | --- |
| 100 | (ein)hundert |
| 101 | hunderteins |
| 250 | zweihundertfünfzig |
| 365 | dreihundertfünfundsechzig |
| 1 000 | (ein)tausend |

The hundreds come first, as in English. Only the last two digits are swapped.

<Example de="hundert, zweihundertfünfzig, dreihundertfünfundsechzig, tausend." en="100, 250, 365, 1,000" />

</Step>

<Step n="6" title="Your age">

Age uses the verb **sein**, exactly as in English, plus **Jahre alt** (years old).

<Example de="Wie alt sind Sie?" en="How old are you? (formal)" />
<Example de="Wie alt bist du?" en="How old are you? (informal)" />
<Example de="Ich bin achtundzwanzig Jahre alt." en="I am twenty-eight years old." />
<Example de="Ich bin achtundzwanzig." en="I am twenty-eight." />

</Step>

<Step n="7" title="Phone numbers and addresses">

Phone numbers are usually read digit by digit. Many Germans say **zwo** instead of *zwei* on the phone, because *zwei* and *drei* are easy to confuse.

<Example de="Wie ist Ihre Telefonnummer?" en="What is your phone number?" />
<Example de="Meine Telefonnummer ist null, eins, sieben, sechs, vier, drei, zwei, neun." en="My phone number is 0176 4329." />

For an address you need the street, the house number, the postcode and the town. The house number comes **after** the street name.

<Example de="Wie ist Ihre Adresse?" en="What is your address?" />
<Example de="Ich wohne in der Goethestraße zwölf, in sechs null drei eins eins Frankfurt." en="I live at 12 Goethestraße, 60311 Frankfurt." />

</Step>

<Step n="8" title="Prices">

Germany, Austria and many of their neighbours pay in **Euro** and **Cent**. Prices are written with a comma, and the comma is spoken as the word *Euro*.

<Example de="Was kostet das?" en="What does that cost?" />
<Example de="Wie viel kostet der Kaffee?" en="How much is the coffee?" />
<Example de="Das kostet drei Euro fünfzig." en="That costs €3.50." />
<Example de="Der Kaffee kostet zwei Euro neunzig." en="The coffee costs €2.90." />

| Written | Spoken |
| --- | --- |
| 3,50 € | drei Euro fünfzig |
| 0,99 € | neunundneunzig Cent |
| 12,00 € | zwölf Euro |
| 24,95 € | vierundzwanzig Euro fünfundneunzig |

*Euro* and *Cent* have no plural after a number: *zwei Euro*, never *zwei Euros*.

</Step>

## Compared with English

<Compare en="I **am** thirty years old." de="Ich **bin** dreißig Jahre alt." note="Same verb in both languages: to be. Speakers of French, Spanish or Italian often want to say 'I have 30 years'; German does not." />

## Typical mistakes

<Mistake wrong="zwanzigundeins" right="einundzwanzig" why="The unit comes first, then und, then the ten." />

<Mistake wrong="Ich **habe** dreißig Jahre." right="Ich **bin** dreißig Jahre alt." why="Age is expressed with sein, and the sentence ends in alt." />

<Mistake wrong="einsundzwanzig" right="einundzwanzig" why="eins drops its s when another part of the number follows." />

<Mistake wrong="Das kostet fünf **Euros**." right="Das kostet fünf **Euro**." why="After a number, Euro and Cent stay in the singular." />

<Mistake wrong="dreizig" right="dreißig" why="30 is the one ten that is written with ß. All the others end in -zig." />

## Summary

<Summary>

| Range | Pattern | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 13–19 | unit + zehn | fünfzehn (but sechzehn, siebzehn) |
| 20–90 | unit + zig | fünfzig (but zwanzig, dreißig, sechzig, siebzig) |
| 21–99 | unit + und + ten | fünfundvierzig = 45 |
| 100+ | hundreds first | zweihundertfünfundvierzig = 245 |

| To say | German |
| --- | --- |
| How old are you? | Wie alt sind Sie? Ich bin … Jahre alt. |
| Your phone number | Meine Telefonnummer ist … |
| What does it cost? | Was kostet das? Das kostet … Euro. |

**When listening:** hear the unit, write it on the right; hear the ten, write it on the left.

</Summary>
