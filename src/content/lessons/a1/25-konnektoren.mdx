By now you can say a great deal, one short sentence at a time. *Ich bin müde. Ich gehe nach Hause. Ich schlafe.* That is correct, and it sounds like a list. The writing exam gives a point for how well your text hangs together, and a handful of small words is all it takes.

## The idea

German has two kinds of joining word, and the difference between them is purely a matter of **where the verb goes**.

**Group 1: und, oder, aber, denn, sondern.** These stand *between* two sentences and belong to neither. They occupy **position zero**. The sentence after them starts counting from one, so the word order stays exactly as it was. Nothing to learn but the words.

**Group 2: dann, danach, deshalb, zuerst.** These are adverbs. They stand *inside* the sentence, in **position one**. Since the verb must be second, it comes straight after them and the subject moves behind the verb. This is the inversion you have known since chapter 8.

Keep those two groups apart and you cannot go wrong.

## Step by step

<Step n="1" title="und: and">

<Example de="Ich komme aus Indien **und** ich wohne in Köln." en="I come from India and I live in Cologne." />
<Example de="Er lernt Deutsch **und** sie lernt Englisch." en="He is learning German and she is learning English." />

When both halves have the same subject, you can leave it out the second time, as in English:

<Example de="Ich komme aus Indien **und** wohne in Köln." en="I come from India and live in Cologne." />
<Example de="Sie kauft Brot **und** Käse." en="She buys bread and cheese." />

There is normally no comma before *und*.

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="oder: or">

<Example de="Möchtest du Kaffee **oder** Tee?" en="Would you like coffee or tea?" />
<Example de="Wir gehen ins Kino **oder** wir bleiben zu Hause." en="We go to the cinema or we stay at home." />
<Example de="Kommst du heute **oder** morgen?" en="Are you coming today or tomorrow?" />

No comma before *oder* either.

</Step>

<Step n="3" title="aber: but">

*aber* joins two things that contrast. It takes a comma.

<Example de="Ich bin müde, **aber** ich muss arbeiten." en="I am tired, but I have to work." />
<Example de="Die Wohnung ist klein, **aber** gemütlich." en="The flat is small but cosy." />
<Example de="Er spricht Deutsch, **aber** nicht sehr gut." en="He speaks German, but not very well." />

</Step>

<Step n="4" title="denn: because">

*denn* gives a reason, and it keeps the normal word order. It takes a comma and always stands between the two sentences, never at the start.

<Example de="Ich gehe nach Hause, **denn** ich bin müde." en="I am going home because I am tired." />
<Example de="Sie kann nicht kommen, **denn** sie ist krank." en="She cannot come because she is ill." />
<Example de="Wir bleiben zu Hause, **denn** es regnet." en="We are staying at home because it is raining." />

At A2 you will learn *weil*, which also means *because* but sends the verb to the end. Until then, *denn* is the safe way to give a reason.

</Step>

<Step n="5" title="sondern: but rather">

After a **negative** statement, a correction is introduced with **sondern**, not *aber*.

<Example de="Ich komme nicht aus Polen, **sondern** aus Ungarn." en="I am not from Poland but from Hungary." />
<Example de="Das ist kein Kaffee, **sondern** Tee." en="That is not coffee but tea." />
<Example de="Wir fahren nicht heute, **sondern** morgen." en="We are not going today but tomorrow." />

*aber* adds a contrast (*small **but** cosy*). *sondern* replaces something that was just denied (*not coffee **but** tea*).

</Step>

<Step n="6" title="Position zero: the word order does not change">

| First sentence | 0 | 1 | 2: verb | rest |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ich bin müde, | aber | ich | **muss** | arbeiten. |
| Ich gehe nach Hause, | denn | ich | **bin** | müde. |
| Er lernt Deutsch | und | sie | **lernt** | Englisch. |

After the connector, count again from one. The verb is second, as always.

</Step>

<Step n="7" title="dann, danach, zuerst, deshalb: position one">

These words tell a story in order, or give a consequence. They take the first position, so the **verb comes next**.

| Word | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **zuerst** | first |
| **dann** | then |
| **danach** | after that |
| **später** | later |
| **deshalb** | therefore, that is why |

<Example de="**Zuerst** frühstücke ich." en="First I have breakfast." />
<Example de="**Dann** fahre ich zur Arbeit." en="Then I go to work." />
<Example de="**Danach** kaufe ich ein." en="After that I go shopping." />
<Example de="Ich bin krank. **Deshalb** bleibe ich zu Hause." en="I am ill. That is why I am staying at home." />

Compare the two ways of giving a reason:

<Example de="Ich bleibe zu Hause, **denn** ich **bin** krank." en="I am staying at home because I am ill. (position zero: no change)" />
<Example de="Ich bin krank. **Deshalb bleibe** ich zu Hause." en="I am ill. So I am staying at home. (position one: verb next)" />

</Step>

<Step n="8" title="From a list to a text">

Separate sentences:

<Example de="Ich stehe um sieben Uhr auf. Ich frühstücke. Ich fahre zur Arbeit. Ich bin müde. Ich arbeite bis fünf." />

The same content, joined up:

<Example de="Ich stehe um sieben Uhr auf und frühstücke. Dann fahre ich zur Arbeit. Ich bin müde, aber ich arbeite bis fünf." en="I get up at seven and have breakfast. Then I go to work. I am tired, but I work until five." />

Three connectors turned five sentences into a small text. In the writing exam, aim for at least two or three of them in every message.

</Step>

## Compared with English

<Compare en="I am tired, **but** I have to work." de="Ich bin müde, **aber** ich muss arbeiten." note="und, oder, aber work exactly like English and, or, but: same position, same word order." />

<Compare en="**Then** I go to work." de="**Dann** fahre ich zur Arbeit." note="Here the languages part. After 'then' English keeps subject before verb. German puts the verb straight after dann." />

<Compare en="not coffee **but** tea / small **but** cosy" de="kein Kaffee, **sondern** Tee / klein, **aber** gemütlich" note="English has one 'but'. German uses sondern when correcting something negative." />

<Compare en="**Because** I am ill, I am staying home." de="Ich bleibe zu Hause, **denn** ich bin krank." note="English 'because' can start a sentence. German denn cannot: it always comes between the two parts." />

## Typical mistakes

<Mistake wrong="Dann **ich fahre** zur Arbeit." right="Dann **fahre ich** zur Arbeit." why="dann takes position one, so the verb must follow it directly." />

<Mistake wrong="Ich bin müde, aber **muss ich** arbeiten." right="Ich bin müde, aber **ich muss** arbeiten." why="aber is in position zero and does not cause inversion. The subject comes first as usual." />

<Mistake wrong="Ich komme nicht aus Polen, **aber** aus Ungarn." right="Ich komme nicht aus Polen, **sondern** aus Ungarn." why="A correction after a negative takes sondern." />

<Mistake wrong="**Denn** ich bin krank, ich bleibe zu Hause." right="Ich bleibe zu Hause, **denn** ich bin krank." why="denn cannot begin the sentence. It stands between the statement and its reason." />

<Mistake wrong="Deshalb **ich bleibe** zu Hause." right="Deshalb **bleibe ich** zu Hause." why="deshalb, like dann, is in position one. Verb next, then subject." />

## Summary

<Summary>

| Position zero: word order unchanged | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **und** | and |
| **oder** | or |
| **aber** | but |
| **denn** | because |
| **sondern** | but rather (after a negative) |

| Position one: verb comes next | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **zuerst** | first |
| **dann** | then |
| **danach** | after that |
| **deshalb** | that is why |

**Commas:** before aber, denn, sondern. Not before und, oder.

**Compare:** …, **denn** ich **bin** krank. · **Deshalb bleibe** ich zu Hause.

</Summary>
