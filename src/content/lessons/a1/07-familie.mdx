Family is one of the standard topics of the A1 exam. You may be asked *Sind Sie verheiratet?* or *Haben Sie Kinder?*, the form in the writing part often has a field *Familienstand*, and the cue card for introducing yourself may say *Familie*. This chapter gives you the words and the two pieces of grammar they need.

## The idea

Two tools make it possible to talk about family.

The verb **haben** (to have) lets you say what and whom you have: a sister, two children, a big family. Along with *sein* it is the most important verb in the language, and like *sein* it is slightly irregular.

The **possessives** (my, your, his, her …) say whose someone is: *my* mother, *her* brother. The good news is that they behave exactly like *ein* and *kein* from the last chapter. If you can say *eine Lampe*, you can say *meine Mutter*.

## Step by step

<Step n="1" title="The family">

| Male | Female | Together |
| --- | --- | --- |
| der Vater (father) | die Mutter (mother) | die Eltern (parents) |
| der Sohn (son) | die Tochter (daughter) | die Kinder (children) |
| der Bruder (brother) | die Schwester (sister) | die Geschwister (siblings) |
| der Großvater / der Opa | die Großmutter / die Oma | die Großeltern (grandparents) |
| der Onkel (uncle) | die Tante (aunt) | |
| der Mann (husband) | die Frau (wife) | |

*Eltern*, *Geschwister* and *Großeltern* exist only in the plural. *Mann* and *Frau* mean *man* and *woman*, and with a possessive they mean *husband* and *wife*: *mein Mann*, *meine Frau*.

<Example de="der Vater, die Mutter, die Eltern." en="the father, the mother, the parents" />
<Example de="der Bruder, die Schwester, die Geschwister." en="the brother, the sister, the siblings" />

Several of these have an umlaut plural: *die Väter, die Mütter, die Brüder, die Töchter, die Söhne*.

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="The verb haben">

| | haben | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ich | **habe** | I have |
| du | **hast** | you have |
| er / sie / es | **hat** | he / she / it has |
| wir | **haben** | we have |
| ihr | **habt** | you have |
| sie / Sie | **haben** | they / you have |

It is regular except for *du hast* and *er hat*, which lose the **b**.

<Example de="Ich habe eine Schwester." en="I have a sister." />
<Example de="Hast du Geschwister?" en="Do you have brothers or sisters?" />
<Example de="Sie hat zwei Kinder." en="She has two children." />
<Example de="Wir haben eine große Familie." en="We have a big family." />

</Step>

<Step n="3" title="The possessives">

Each person has its own word.

| Person | Possessive | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ich | **mein** | my |
| du | **dein** | your (informal) |
| er | **sein** | his |
| sie (she) | **ihr** | her |
| es | **sein** | its |
| wir | **unser** | our |
| ihr | **euer** | your (several people) |
| sie (they) | **ihr** | their |
| Sie | **Ihr** | your (formal) |

Yes, *ihr* appears again, now meaning *her* and *their*, and with a capital letter *your*. And yes, **sein** is also the verb *to be*. The sentence makes clear which is meant: *sein Vater* can only be "his father".

</Step>

<Step n="4" title="The ending follows the thing owned">

A possessive takes the same endings as *ein*: nothing for masculine and neuter, **-e** for feminine and for the plural.

| | masculine | feminine | neuter | plural |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| | der Vater | die Mutter | das Kind | die Eltern |
| a | ein Vater | ein**e** Mutter | ein Kind | |
| my | mein Vater | mein**e** Mutter | mein Kind | mein**e** Eltern |
| your | dein Vater | dein**e** Mutter | dein Kind | dein**e** Eltern |
| his | sein Vater | sein**e** Mutter | sein Kind | sein**e** Eltern |
| her | ihr Vater | ihr**e** Mutter | ihr Kind | ihr**e** Eltern |

The ending depends on the gender of the **person or thing owned**, not on the owner.

<Example de="Das ist **mein** Vater." en="That is my father." />
<Example de="Das ist **meine** Mutter." en="That is my mother." />
<Example de="Das sind **meine** Eltern." en="Those are my parents." />
<Example de="Wie heißt **dein** Bruder?" en="What is your brother called?" />
<Example de="Wo wohnt **Ihre** Tochter, Frau Weber?" en="Where does your daughter live, Mrs Weber?" />

</Step>

<Step n="5" title="sein or ihr: his or her">

Here you make two decisions, one after the other.

1. **Who is the owner?** A man: *sein-*. A woman: *ihr-*.
2. **What is owned?** Masculine or neuter: no ending. Feminine or plural: **-e**.

| | his | her |
| --- | --- | --- |
| brother (der) | sein Bruder | ihr Bruder |
| sister (die) | sein**e** Schwester | ihr**e** Schwester |

<Example de="Das ist Paul. **Seine** Schwester heißt Anna." en="That is Paul. His sister is called Anna." />
<Example de="Das ist Anna. **Ihr** Bruder heißt Paul." en="That is Anna. Her brother is called Paul." />

</Step>

<Step n="6" title="Talking about your family">

<Example de="Sind Sie verheiratet?" en="Are you married?" />
<Example de="Ja, ich bin verheiratet. Meine Frau heißt Elena." en="Yes, I am married. My wife is called Elena." />
<Example de="Nein, ich bin ledig." en="No, I am single." />
<Example de="Haben Sie Kinder?" en="Do you have children?" />
<Example de="Ja, ich habe zwei Kinder: einen Sohn und eine Tochter." en="Yes, I have two children: a son and a daughter." />
<Example de="Nein, ich habe keine Kinder." en="No, I have no children." />

| German | English |
| --- | --- |
| verheiratet | married |
| ledig | single |
| geschieden | divorced |
| der Familienstand | marital status |

One thing to notice: with *haben*, a masculine noun takes **einen** instead of *ein* (*einen Sohn, einen Bruder*). You saw *keinen* in the last chapter. Chapter 9 explains why; until then, learn *Ich habe einen Bruder* as a whole.

</Step>

## Compared with English

<Compare en="**my** father, **my** mother" de="**mein** Vater, **meine** Mutter" note="English 'my' never changes. German adds -e when the person or thing owned is feminine or plural." />

<Compare en="**his** sister / **her** sister" de="**seine** Schwester / **ihre** Schwester" note="Both languages look at the owner first: his or her. German then also looks at the thing owned and adds -e because Schwester is feminine." />

<Compare en="**your** name (one word for everyone)" de="**dein** Name, **euer** Name, **Ihr** Name" note="Three kinds of 'you' give three kinds of 'your'." />

<Compare en="I **have** a sister. / I **have got** a sister." de="Ich **habe** eine Schwester." note="German has no 'have got'. It is simply haben." />

## Typical mistakes

<Mistake wrong="Das ist **mein** Mutter." right="Das ist **meine** Mutter." why="Mutter is feminine, so the possessive needs -e, just like eine Mutter." />

<Mistake wrong="Er **habt** zwei Kinder." right="Er **hat** zwei Kinder." why="haben loses its b with du and er: du hast, er hat. 'habt' belongs to ihr." />

<Mistake wrong="Anna und **sein** Bruder" right="Anna und **ihr** Bruder" why="The owner is Anna, a woman, so it is ihr. sein is for a male owner." />

<Mistake wrong="Meine **Eltern ist** in Indien." right="Meine **Eltern sind** in Indien." why="Eltern is always plural, so the verb is plural too." />

<Mistake wrong="Ich **bin** zwei Kinder." right="Ich **habe** zwei Kinder." why="What you possess takes haben. sein describes what you are." />

## Summary

<Summary>

| | ich | du | er / sie / es | wir | ihr | sie / Sie |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **haben** | habe | hast | hat | haben | habt | haben |

| Owner | ich | du | er | sie | wir | ihr | sie | Sie |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Possessive** | mein | dein | sein | ihr | unser | euer | ihr | Ihr |

**Ending:** like *ein*. Masculine and neuter: no ending (*mein Vater, mein Kind*). Feminine and plural: **-e** (*meine Mutter, meine Eltern*).

**His or her:** first the owner (sein- or ihr-), then the ending for the thing owned.

</Summary>
