German has two words for *not*. Choosing between them is one of the most frequent decisions you make in the language, and beginners get it wrong constantly, usually by saying *nicht* everywhere. The rule is simple once you see it.

## The idea

First the indefinite article. Where English says *a* or *an*, German says **ein** or **eine**, depending on the gender you learned in the last chapter.

Then negation. German asks one question: *what* are you negating?

- If you are negating a **noun that has ein in front of it, or no article at all**, use **kein**. Think of it as *k + ein*: "not a", "no".
- For **everything else** (a verb, an adjective, a name, a noun with *der* or *mein*), use **nicht**.

## Step by step

<Step n="1" title="ein and eine">

| Gender | Definite | Indefinite | |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| masculine | der Tisch | **ein** Tisch | a table |
| feminine | die Lampe | **eine** Lampe | a lamp |
| neuter | das Buch | **ein** Buch | a book |
| plural | die Bücher | Bücher | books |

Masculine and neuter share **ein**; only the feminine adds **-e**. There is no plural of *ein*, just as English has no plural of *a*.

<Example de="Das ist **ein** Tisch." en="That is a table." />
<Example de="Das ist **eine** Lampe." en="That is a lamp." />
<Example de="Das ist **ein** Buch." en="That is a book." />
<Example de="Das sind Bücher." en="Those are books." />

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="First ein, then der">

As in English, something new is introduced with the indefinite article. Once it is known, you switch to the definite one.

<Example de="Das ist **ein** Buch. **Das** Buch ist neu." en="That is a book. The book is new." />
<Example de="Hier ist **eine** Tasche. **Die** Tasche ist schwer." en="Here is a bag. The bag is heavy." />

To ask what something is, use **Was ist das?**

<Example de="Was ist das? Das ist ein Stift." en="What is that? That is a pen." />

</Step>

<Step n="3" title="kein and keine">

**kein** is the negative of *ein* and takes exactly the same endings. Unlike *ein*, it does have a plural: **keine**.

| Gender | Positive | Negative | |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| masculine | ein Tisch | **kein** Tisch | no table, not a table |
| feminine | eine Lampe | **keine** Lampe | no lamp |
| neuter | ein Buch | **kein** Buch | no book |
| plural | Bücher | **keine** Bücher | no books |

<Example de="Das ist **kein** Tisch. Das ist ein Stuhl." en="That is not a table. That is a chair." />
<Example de="Ich habe **keine** Zeit." en="I have no time." />
<Example de="Wir haben **kein** Auto." en="We do not have a car." />
<Example de="Hier sind **keine** Kinder." en="There are no children here." />

</Step>

<Step n="4" title="kein with nouns that have no article">

Many German nouns are used without any article: things you cannot count (*Zeit, Geld, Hunger, Durst*), and plurals. Their negative is *kein* too.

| Positive | Negative |
| --- | --- |
| Ich habe Zeit. | Ich habe **keine** Zeit. |
| Ich habe Hunger. | Ich habe **keinen** Hunger. |
| Sie hat Geld. | Sie hat **kein** Geld. |
| Er trinkt Kaffee. | Er trinkt **keinen** Kaffee. |

You will notice *keinen* in two of these. That is the masculine form after verbs like *haben* and *trinken*; chapter 9 explains it. For now, recognise it.

<Example de="Hast du Durst? Nein, ich habe keinen Durst." en="Are you thirsty? No, I am not thirsty." />

</Step>

<Step n="5" title="nicht for everything else">

**nicht** negates a verb, an adjective, an adverb, a name, or a noun that has *der, die, das* or a possessive in front of it. It never changes its form.

<Example de="Ich komme **nicht**." en="I am not coming." />
<Example de="Das ist **nicht** gut." en="That is not good." />
<Example de="Das ist **nicht** Anna." en="That is not Anna." />
<Example de="Das ist **nicht** der Lehrer." en="That is not the teacher." />
<Example de="Ich wohne **nicht** in Berlin." en="I do not live in Berlin." />

</Step>

<Step n="6" title="Where nicht goes">

This is the part that needs care.

**At the end**, when you negate the whole sentence and there is only a simple verb:

<Example de="Ich arbeite **nicht**." en="I do not work." />
<Example de="Er kommt heute **nicht**." en="He is not coming today." />

**In front of** the word it negates, when that is an adjective, an adverb, or a phrase with a preposition:

<Example de="Das Buch ist **nicht** teuer." en="The book is not expensive." />
<Example de="Sie tanzt **nicht** gern." en="She does not like dancing." />
<Example de="Wir wohnen **nicht** in Köln." en="We do not live in Cologne." />

A useful check: after *sein*, *nicht* always comes before the adjective or noun.

</Step>

<Step n="7" title="The decision in one line">

Ask: is there a noun with **ein** or with **no article**? Then **kein**. Otherwise **nicht**.

| Sentence | Negative | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Das ist **ein** Problem. | Das ist **kein** Problem. | noun with ein |
| Ich habe Zeit. | Ich habe **keine** Zeit. | noun with no article |
| Das ist **das** Problem. | Das ist **nicht** das Problem. | noun with das |
| Das ist richtig. | Das ist **nicht** richtig. | adjective |
| Ich arbeite. | Ich arbeite **nicht**. | verb |

Jobs and nationalities have no article in German (*Ich bin Lehrer*), so they take *kein*: *Ich bin **kein** Lehrer.*

</Step>

## Compared with English

<Compare en="I do **not** have **a** car." de="Ich habe **kein** Auto." note="English negates the verb and keeps 'a'. German fuses 'not a' into one word and leaves the verb alone." />

<Compare en="I have **no** time. / I do **not** have time." de="Ich habe **keine** Zeit." note="English has two ways. German has one, and it is always kein with a noun like this." />

<Compare en="I do **not** work." de="Ich arbeite **nicht**." note="German needs no 'do'. The verb stays where it is and nicht goes to the end." />

<Compare en="**a** table, **an** apple" de="**ein** Tisch, **ein** Apfel" note="English chooses a or an by sound. German chooses ein or eine by gender." />

## Typical mistakes

<Mistake wrong="Ich habe **nicht** Zeit." right="Ich habe **keine** Zeit." why="Zeit is a noun with no article, so its negative is kein." />

<Mistake wrong="Das ist **nicht ein** Problem." right="Das ist **kein** Problem." why="'nicht ein' is almost never right. Use kein." />

<Mistake wrong="Das ist **kein** gut." right="Das ist **nicht** gut." why="gut is an adjective, not a noun. Adjectives take nicht." />

<Mistake wrong="Ich **nicht** komme." right="Ich komme **nicht**." why="The verb keeps its second position. nicht comes after it, here at the end." />

<Mistake wrong="Das ist **eine** Buch." right="Das ist **ein** Buch." why="Buch is neuter. Only feminine nouns take eine." />

## Summary

<Summary>

| | masculine | feminine | neuter | plural |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| a | ein | eine | ein | (none) |
| no, not a | kein | keine | kein | keine |

| Negating … | Use | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a noun with *ein* or no article | **kein** | Ich habe keine Zeit. |
| a verb | **nicht** (at the end) | Ich komme nicht. |
| an adjective or adverb | **nicht** (in front of it) | Das ist nicht gut. |
| a phrase with a preposition | **nicht** (in front of it) | Ich wohne nicht in Köln. |
| a noun with *der*, *mein*, or a name | **nicht** (in front of it) | Das ist nicht Anna. |

</Summary>
