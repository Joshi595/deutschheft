In chapter 4 you learned the regular endings. A group of very common verbs keeps those endings but also changes the vowel in the middle. You have already used several without comment: *Sie **spricht** Spanisch. Der Film **fängt** um acht an.* Here is the pattern behind them.

## The idea

The change is small and tightly limited.

**It only ever happens in two forms: du and er / sie / es.** The *ich* form and all the plural forms are perfectly regular. So for any of these verbs you have just two special forms to learn.

**The endings stay regular.** Only the vowel moves.

There are three patterns: **e becomes i**, **e becomes ie**, and **a becomes ä**. There is no rule that tells you which verbs do this; they are simply some of the oldest and most used verbs in the language. The word lists in this course mark them by showing the *er* form.

## Step by step

<Step n="1" title="Where the change happens">

Compare a regular verb with a vowel-changing one.

| | lernen (regular) | sprechen (e → i) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ich | lerne | spreche |
| **du** | lernst | spr**i**chst |
| **er / sie / es** | lernt | spr**i**cht |
| wir | lernen | sprechen |
| ihr | lernt | sprecht |
| sie / Sie | lernen | sprechen |

Two rows change. Four stay as you would expect.

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="e → i">

| Infinitive | du | er / sie / es | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| sprechen | spr**i**chst | spr**i**cht | to speak |
| essen | **i**sst | **i**sst | to eat |
| geben | g**i**bst | g**i**bt | to give |
| helfen | h**i**lfst | h**i**lft | to help |
| treffen | tr**i**ffst | tr**i**fft | to meet |
| nehmen | n**imm**st | n**imm**t | to take |

<Example de="Ich spreche Deutsch. Er **spricht** Englisch." en="I speak German. He speaks English." />
<Example de="Was **isst** du gern?" en="What do you like eating?" />
<Example de="Sie **gibt** mir das Buch." en="She gives me the book." />
<Example de="**Hilfst** du mir?" en="Will you help me?" />

Two details. **essen**: the stem ends in an s-sound, so *du* and *er* look the same: *du isst, er isst*. **nehmen**: the consonants change too: *du nimmst, er nimmt*.

<Example de="Ich nehme einen Kaffee. Er **nimmt** einen Tee." en="I will have a coffee. He will have a tea." />

You have met *geben* in the fixed expression **es gibt** (there is). Now you can see where it comes from: *es gibt* is simply the *er* form of *geben*.

</Step>

<Step n="3" title="e → ie">

A long *e* turns into a long *ie*.

| Infinitive | du | er / sie / es | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| lesen | l**ie**st | l**ie**st | to read |
| sehen | s**ie**hst | s**ie**ht | to see |
| fernsehen | s**ie**hst … fern | s**ie**ht … fern | to watch TV |

<Example de="Ich lese ein Buch. Sie **liest** die Zeitung." en="I am reading a book. She is reading the newspaper." />
<Example de="**Siehst** du den Mann dort?" en="Do you see the man over there?" />
<Example de="Er **sieht** am Abend **fern**." en="He watches TV in the evening." />

*lesen* behaves like *essen*: the stem ends in s, so *du liest* and *er liest* are identical.

</Step>

<Step n="4" title="a → ä">

| Infinitive | du | er / sie / es | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| fahren | f**ä**hrst | f**ä**hrt | to drive, to go (by vehicle) |
| schlafen | schl**ä**fst | schl**ä**ft | to sleep |
| tragen | tr**ä**gst | tr**ä**gt | to carry, to wear |
| waschen | w**ä**schst | w**ä**scht | to wash |
| anfangen | f**ä**ngst … an | f**ä**ngt … an | to begin |
| laufen | l**äu**fst | l**äu**ft | to run, to walk |

<Example de="Ich fahre nach Berlin. Er **fährt** nach Hamburg." en="I am going to Berlin. He is going to Hamburg." />
<Example de="Das Kind **schläft**." en="The child is sleeping." />
<Example de="Wann **fängt** der Kurs **an**?" en="When does the course begin?" />
<Example de="Sie **läuft** jeden Morgen." en="She goes running every morning." />

</Step>

<Step n="5" title="The plural never changes">

This is the most common slip. Learners who have just learned *er fährt* start saying *wir fähren*. The plural is always regular.

<Example de="Er **fährt** nach Köln. Wir **fahren** nach Köln." en="He is going to Cologne. We are going to Cologne." />
<Example de="Du **sprichst** gut Deutsch. Ihr **sprecht** gut Deutsch." en="You speak German well. You (all) speak German well." />

</Step>

<Step n="6" title="wissen: irregular in its own way">

**wissen** (to know a fact) is special. Its whole singular is irregular, and like *mögen* it has no ending for *ich* and *er*.

| | wissen |
| --- | --- |
| ich | **weiß** |
| du | **weißt** |
| er / sie / es | **weiß** |
| wir | wissen |
| ihr | wisst |
| sie / Sie | wissen |

<Example de="Ich **weiß** es nicht." en="I do not know." />
<Example de="**Weißt** du, wo der Bahnhof ist?" en="Do you know where the station is?" />

*Ich weiß nicht* is one of the most useful sentences you will ever learn.

</Step>

<Step n="7" title="How to learn them">

Do not try to remember abstract patterns. Learn each verb as a pair: **the infinitive and the er form**.

*sprechen, er spricht · essen, er isst · nehmen, er nimmt · lesen, er liest · sehen, er sieht · fahren, er fährt · schlafen, er schläft*

From the *er* form you get the *du* form by swapping **-t** for **-st**. Everything else is regular.

</Step>

## Compared with English

<Compare en="I speak, he speak**s**" de="ich spreche, er spr**i**cht" note="English marks 'he' with an -s. German marks it with -t, and in these verbs with a vowel change as well." />

<Compare en="I **do**, he **does**. I **say**, he **says**." de="ich sehe, er s**ie**ht" note="English has a few verbs whose sound changes in the he-form too (compare 'say' and 'says'). German simply has more of them, and writes the change down." />

<Compare en="I know (a fact) / I know (a person)" de="ich **weiß** / ich **kenne**" note="German uses wissen for facts and kennen for people and places you are familiar with." />

<Compare en="I am going to Berlin (by train)." de="Ich **fahre** nach Berlin." note="gehen means going on foot. Whenever a vehicle is involved, German says fahren." />

## Typical mistakes

<Mistake wrong="Er **sprecht** Deutsch." right="Er **spricht** Deutsch." why="sprechen changes e to i with du and er." />

<Mistake wrong="Wir **fähren** nach Berlin." right="Wir **fahren** nach Berlin." why="The vowel changes only with du and er. The plural stays regular." />

<Mistake wrong="Du **nehmst** den Bus." right="Du **nimmst** den Bus." why="nehmen is irregular in its consonants too: du nimmst, er nimmt." />

<Mistake wrong="Ich **spriche** Englisch." right="Ich **spreche** Englisch." why="The ich form never changes its vowel." />

<Mistake wrong="Ich **wisse** das nicht." right="Ich **weiß** das nicht." why="wissen is irregular in the whole singular: ich weiß, du weißt, er weiß." />

## Summary

<Summary>

| Pattern | Infinitive | du | er / sie / es |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| e → i | sprechen | sprichst | spricht |
| | essen | isst | isst |
| | geben | gibst | gibt |
| | nehmen | nimmst | nimmt |
| | helfen | hilfst | hilft |
| e → ie | lesen | liest | liest |
| | sehen | siehst | sieht |
| a → ä | fahren | fährst | fährt |
| | schlafen | schläfst | schläft |
| | anfangen | fängst an | fängt an |

**Only du and er / sie / es change.** ich and the plural are regular.

**wissen:** ich weiß, du weißt, er weiß, wir wissen.

</Summary>
