*I was ill, **so** I stayed at home. It was raining; **nevertheless** we went out. Hurry up, **otherwise** we will be late.* These little words turn two plain sentences into reasoning, and they make a text sound connected. That counts in the writing exam.

## The idea

You now know two kinds of joining word. **und, aber, denn** stand between sentences and change nothing. **weil, dass, wenn** open a subordinate clause and send the verb to the end.

This chapter adds a third kind. **deshalb, trotzdem, sonst** and their relatives are **adverbs**. They belong to the second sentence and normally stand at its **start**, in position 1. Since the verb must be second, **the verb follows immediately and the subject comes after it**.

*Ich bin krank. **Deshalb bleibe ich** zu Hause.*

You have known this mechanism since A1: it is the same thing that happens after *heute* or *dann*. The only new part is the meaning of the words.

## Step by step

<Step n="1" title="deshalb: consequence">

The first sentence gives the reason; the second, with *deshalb*, gives the result.

<Example de="Ich bin krank. **Deshalb bleibe ich** zu Hause." en="I am ill. That is why I am staying at home." />
<Example de="Es regnet. **Deshalb nehme ich** einen Schirm mit." en="It is raining. So I am taking an umbrella." />
<Example de="Der Bus hatte Verspätung, **deshalb bin ich** zu spät gekommen." en="The bus was late, so I arrived late." />

Three other words mean the same and behave the same: **deswegen, darum, daher**. In speech *deshalb* and *deswegen* are the most common.

<Example de="Ich habe kein Geld. **Deswegen kann ich** nicht mitkommen." en="I have no money. That is why I cannot come along." />

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="deshalb and weil say the same thing backwards">

| | Reason | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| weil | …, **weil** ich krank **bin**. | Ich bleibe zu Hause, … |
| deshalb | Ich bin krank. | **Deshalb bleibe** ich zu Hause. |

*weil* attaches to the **reason** and sends the verb to the end. *deshalb* attaches to the **result** and puts the verb right after itself.

<Example de="Ich bleibe zu Hause, **weil** ich krank **bin**." en="I am staying at home because I am ill." />
<Example de="Ich bin krank, **deshalb bleibe** ich zu Hause." en="I am ill, so I am staying at home." />

</Step>

<Step n="3" title="trotzdem: against expectation">

The first sentence leads you to expect one thing. *trotzdem* introduces the opposite.

<Example de="Es regnet. **Trotzdem gehen wir** spazieren." en="It is raining. Nevertheless we are going for a walk." />
<Example de="Ich bin müde. **Trotzdem muss ich** noch arbeiten." en="I am tired. All the same I still have to work." />
<Example de="Das Auto ist teuer, **trotzdem kaufe ich** es." en="The car is expensive; I am buying it anyway." />

*trotzdem* is stronger than *aber*: it says "in spite of that".

</Step>

<Step n="4" title="sonst: otherwise">

*sonst* says what will happen if the first sentence is not fulfilled. It often follows an instruction.

<Example de="Beeil dich, **sonst kommen wir** zu spät." en="Hurry up, otherwise we will be late." />
<Example de="Ich muss jetzt gehen, **sonst verpasse ich** den Bus." en="I have to go now, or else I will miss the bus." />
<Example de="Zieh eine Jacke an, **sonst erkältest du** dich." en="Put a jacket on, otherwise you will catch a cold." />

You know *sonst* from the shop too, with its other meaning: *Sonst noch etwas?* (Anything else?)

</Step>

<Step n="5" title="außerdem and also">

Two more that work the same way.

| Word | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **außerdem** | besides, in addition | Ich habe keine Zeit. **Außerdem bin ich** müde. |
| **also** | so, therefore | Es ist schon spät, **also gehen wir** jetzt. |

*also* is a false friend: it never means English *also*. That is **auch**.

</Step>

<Step n="6" title="In the middle of the sentence">

These adverbs do not have to come first. They can also stand **after the verb**, and then the subject keeps its usual place.

<Example de="**Deshalb** bleibe ich zu Hause." en="That is why I am staying at home." />
<Example de="Ich bleibe **deshalb** zu Hause." en="I am therefore staying at home." />
<Example de="Wir gehen **trotzdem** spazieren." en="We are going for a walk anyway." />

Both positions are correct. The one thing that is not possible is the adverb first with the subject straight after it.

</Step>

<Step n="7" title="Three kinds of connector side by side">

| Type | Words | Verb | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| position zero | und, aber, oder, denn | second, as normal | …, denn ich **bin** krank. |
| position one (adverbs) | deshalb, trotzdem, sonst, dann, außerdem, also | directly after the connector | Deshalb **bleibe** ich … |
| subordinating | weil, dass, wenn, ob | at the end | …, weil ich krank **bin**. |

Every connector you meet from now on belongs to one of these three groups. When you learn a new one, learn its group with it.

</Step>

## Compared with English

<Compare en="I am ill, **so I am staying** at home." de="Ich bin krank, **deshalb bleibe ich** zu Hause." note="After 'so' English keeps subject before verb. After deshalb German puts the verb first." />

<Compare en="**Although** it is raining, we go out. / It is raining, **but** we go out **anyway**." de="Es regnet. **Trotzdem** gehen wir raus." note="trotzdem is 'anyway, nevertheless'. The German for 'although' is obwohl, which you meet at B1." />

<Compare en="Hurry up, **or** we will be late." de="Beeil dich, **sonst** kommen wir zu spät." note="English often says 'or'. German needs sonst here, not oder." />

<Compare en="I **also** have a car." de="Ich habe **auch** ein Auto." note="German also means 'so, therefore'. The word for English 'also' is auch." />

## Typical mistakes

<Mistake wrong="Deshalb **ich bleibe** zu Hause." right="Deshalb **bleibe ich** zu Hause." why="deshalb takes position 1, so the verb must come directly after it." />

<Mistake wrong="Ich bin krank, deshalb ich zu Hause **bleibe**." right="Ich bin krank, deshalb **bleibe** ich zu Hause." why="deshalb is not like weil. It does not send the verb to the end." />

<Mistake wrong="Beeil dich, **oder** wir kommen zu spät." right="Beeil dich, **sonst** kommen wir zu spät." why="For a warning German uses sonst." />

<Mistake wrong="Es regnet, **trotzdem wir gehen** spazieren." right="Es regnet, **trotzdem gehen wir** spazieren." why="trotzdem behaves like deshalb: verb next, then subject." />

<Mistake wrong="Ich habe **also** ein Auto. (meaning: I too have a car)" right="Ich habe **auch** ein Auto." why="also means 'so'. For 'too, as well' use auch." />

## Summary

<Summary>

| Word | Meaning | Pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **deshalb** (deswegen, darum, daher) | therefore, that is why | reason. Deshalb + verb + subject |
| **trotzdem** | nevertheless, anyway | fact. Trotzdem + verb + subject |
| **sonst** | otherwise | instruction, sonst + verb + subject |
| **außerdem** | besides | Außerdem + verb + subject |
| **also** | so | also + verb + subject |

**Position 1 or after the verb:** *Deshalb bleibe ich zu Hause.* = *Ich bleibe deshalb zu Hause.*

**weil** goes with the reason, verb last. **deshalb** goes with the result, verb next.

</Summary>
