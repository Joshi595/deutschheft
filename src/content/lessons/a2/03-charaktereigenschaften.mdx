Talking about family, friends and colleagues, you soon need to say what they are *like*. In the A2 speaking exam you are asked to talk about yourself and the people around you, and a description of a friend or a family member is one of the easiest ways to keep talking.

## The idea

German asks two different questions about a person, and they get different kinds of answer.

- **Wie ist er?** What is he like? This asks for **character**: *Er ist freundlich und geduldig.*
- **Wie sieht er aus?** What does he look like? This asks for **appearance**: *Er ist groß und hat kurze Haare.*

For both, the basic grammar is the same as at A1: a form of **sein** plus an adjective, and **the adjective takes no ending**. That rule matters more now, because in unit 4 you will learn that adjectives *in front of* a noun do take endings. Keep the two situations apart from the start.

## Step by step

<Step n="1" title="After sein, the adjective never changes">

<Example de="Der Mann ist **freundlich**." en="The man is friendly." />
<Example de="Die Frau ist **freundlich**." en="The woman is friendly." />
<Example de="Das Kind ist **freundlich**." en="The child is friendly." />
<Example de="Die Kinder sind **freundlich**." en="The children are friendly." />

One form for every gender and for the plural. The same is true after **werden** (to become), **bleiben** (to stay), **wirken** (to seem) and **aussehen** (to look).

<Example de="Er wirkt sehr **selbstbewusst**." en="He seems very self-confident." />
<Example de="Sie bleibt immer **ruhig**." en="She always stays calm." />

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="Character">

| Adjective | Meaning | Opposite | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| freundlich | friendly | unfreundlich | unfriendly |
| höflich | polite | unhöflich | rude |
| ehrlich | honest | unehrlich | dishonest |
| geduldig | patient | ungeduldig | impatient |
| zuverlässig | reliable | unzuverlässig | unreliable |
| fleißig | hard-working | faul | lazy |
| mutig | brave | feige | cowardly |
| lustig | funny | ernst | serious |
| ruhig | calm, quiet | nervös | nervous |
| offen | open | schüchtern | shy |
| großzügig | generous | geizig | mean, stingy |
| optimistisch | optimistic | pessimistisch | pessimistic |

Many adjectives form their opposite with **un-**. The others have an opposite that is a different word. There is no rule for which is which, so learn each adjective together with its opposite.

<Example de="Meine Schwester ist fleißig und zuverlässig." en="My sister is hard-working and reliable." />
<Example de="Mein Chef ist oft ungeduldig." en="My boss is often impatient." />
<Example de="Er ist ein bisschen schüchtern, aber sehr ehrlich." en="He is a little shy, but very honest." />

</Step>

<Step n="3" title="Appearance">

Size and build use **sein**:

<Example de="Er ist groß und schlank." en="He is tall and slim." />
<Example de="Sie ist klein und sportlich." en="She is small and athletic." />

| German | English |
| --- | --- |
| groß / klein | tall / short |
| schlank / dick | slim / fat |
| jung / alt | young / old |
| hübsch | pretty |
| sportlich | athletic |

Hair, eyes and things a person wears use **haben** or **tragen**:

<Example de="Sie hat lange, blonde Haare." en="She has long blonde hair." />
<Example de="Er hat braune Augen." en="He has brown eyes." />
<Example de="Mein Vater hat einen Bart und trägt eine Brille." en="My father has a beard and wears glasses." />

*Haare* and *Augen* are plural in German, and the adjective in front of them ends in **-e**. Learn these as whole phrases for now: *kurze Haare, lange Haare, blonde Haare, dunkle Haare, blaue Augen, braune Augen, grüne Augen*. Unit 4 explains the ending.

</Step>

<Step n="4" title="aussehen">

**aussehen** is separable and changes its vowel: *er sieht … aus*.

<Example de="Wie **sieht** dein Bruder **aus**?" en="What does your brother look like?" />
<Example de="Er **sieht** sehr jung **aus**." en="He looks very young." />
<Example de="Du **siehst** müde **aus**." en="You look tired." />
<Example de="Sie **sieht aus wie** ihre Mutter." en="She looks like her mother." />

</Step>

<Step n="5" title="Stronger and weaker">

Put one of these in front of the adjective.

| German | English | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **sehr** | very | sehr freundlich |
| **wirklich** | really | wirklich nett |
| **ziemlich** | quite, rather | ziemlich ruhig |
| **besonders** | especially | besonders fleißig |
| **ein bisschen** | a little | ein bisschen schüchtern |
| **nicht sehr** | not very | nicht sehr geduldig |
| **gar nicht** | not at all | gar nicht faul |
| **zu** | too | zu ernst |
| **viel zu** | far too | viel zu nervös |

<Example de="Mein Bruder ist **ziemlich** faul." en="My brother is rather lazy." />
<Example de="Sie ist **gar nicht** schüchtern." en="She is not shy at all." />
<Example de="Er ist **viel zu** ernst." en="He is far too serious." />

</Step>

<Step n="6" title="Your opinion: finden and wirken">

**finden** takes the person in the accusative and then the adjective.

<Example de="Wie findest du den neuen Kollegen?" en="What do you think of the new colleague?" />
<Example de="Ich finde **ihn** sehr nett." en="I think he is very nice." />
<Example de="Ich finde **sie** ein bisschen arrogant." en="I find her a bit arrogant." />

**wirken** (to seem) is for a first impression:

<Example de="Er wirkt sympathisch." en="He seems likeable." />
<Example de="Auf mich wirkt sie sehr intelligent." en="She strikes me as very intelligent." />

</Step>

<Step n="7" title="A full description">

<Example de="Meine beste Freundin heißt Lena." en="My best friend is called Lena." />
<Example de="Sie ist groß und hat kurze, dunkle Haare." en="She is tall and has short dark hair." />
<Example de="Lena ist sehr lustig und immer optimistisch." en="Lena is very funny and always optimistic." />
<Example de="Manchmal ist sie ein bisschen ungeduldig." en="Sometimes she is a little impatient." />
<Example de="Ich mag sie, weil sie ehrlich und zuverlässig ist." en="I like her because she is honest and reliable." />

The last sentence uses *weil*, which sends the verb to the end. That is the subject of chapter 5.

</Step>

## Compared with English

<Compare en="What is he **like**? / What does he **look like**?" de="Wie **ist** er? / Wie **sieht** er **aus**?" note="English uses 'like' in both questions. German uses two different verbs, and no word for 'like' in the first." />

<Compare en="She has long hair. (singular)" de="Sie hat lange Haar**e**. (plural)" note="'Hair' is singular in English and normally plural in German." />

<Compare en="I think he is nice." de="Ich finde ihn nett." note="finden + accusative + adjective is shorter than the English, and very common." />

<Compare en="un-friendly, im-patient, dis-honest" de="**un**freundlich, **un**geduldig, **un**ehrlich" note="English has several negative prefixes. German mostly has one: un-." />

## Typical mistakes

<Mistake wrong="Die Frau ist **freundliche**." right="Die Frau ist **freundlich**." why="After sein the adjective takes no ending, whatever the gender." />

<Mistake wrong="Wie **ist** er **aus**?" right="Wie **sieht** er **aus**?" why="Appearance is asked with aussehen, a separable verb." />

<Mistake wrong="Ich finde **er** nett." right="Ich finde **ihn** nett." why="finden takes the accusative, so er becomes ihn." />

<Mistake wrong="Sie hat **ein langes Haar**." right="Sie hat **lange Haare**." why="For someone's hair in general, German uses the plural." />

<Mistake wrong="Er ist **sehr zu** ernst." right="Er ist **viel zu** ernst." why="To strengthen zu, use viel, not sehr." />

## Summary

<Summary>

| Question | Asks for | Answer with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wie ist er? | character | sein + adjective |
| Wie sieht er aus? | appearance | sein (size), haben (hair, eyes), tragen (glasses) |
| Wie findest du ihn? | your opinion | finden + accusative + adjective |

**After sein, werden, bleiben, wirken, aussehen:** no adjective ending.

**Intensifiers:** sehr · wirklich · ziemlich · besonders · ein bisschen · nicht sehr · gar nicht · zu · viel zu.

**Opposites:** un- (freundlich → unfreundlich) or a different word (fleißig ↔ faul).

</Summary>
