The **dative** is the case of the indirect object: the person *to whom* or *for whom* something is done. It answers the question **Wem?**

<Example de="Ich gebe **dem Mann** ein Buch." en="I give the man a book." />
<Example de="**Wem** gibst du das Buch?" en="Who are you giving the book to?" />

## The articles

| | Nominative | Dative | with ein / mein |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| masculine | der Mann | **dem** Mann | ein**em** Mann |
| feminine | die Frau | **der** Frau | ein**er** Frau |
| neuter | das Kind | **dem** Kind | ein**em** Kind |
| plural | die Kinder | **den** Kinder**n** | mein**en** Kinder**n** |

Two things are easy to miss:

- Feminine *die* becomes **der** in the dative. It looks like the masculine nominative, but it is not.
- In the dative plural the **noun itself adds -n** (unless it already ends in *-n* or *-s*): die Kinder → mit den Kinder**n**.

## Adjective endings

<GrammarBox title="After an article, dative adjectives end in -en">

After *dem, der, den, einem, einer, meinem, meiner* and so on, the ending is **-en** in every gender and in the plural. The article already shows the case, so the adjective has nothing left to signal.

<Example de="Ich helfe dem **alten** Mann." en="I am helping the old man." />
<Example de="Sie spricht mit einer **jungen** Frau." en="She is talking to a young woman." />
<Example de="Das Buch gehört dem **kleinen** Kind." en="The book belongs to the small child." />
<Example de="Wir spielen mit den **kleinen** Kindern." en="We are playing with the small children." />

</GrammarBox>

**Without an article**, the adjective has to show the case itself, so it takes the ending the article would have had: **-em**, **-er**, **-em**, and **-en** in the plural.

<Example de="Bei **schlechtem** Wetter bleiben wir zu Hause." en="In bad weather we stay at home." />
<Example de="Ich trinke Tee mit **kalter** Milch." en="I drink tea with cold milk." />
<Example de="Sie arbeitet gern mit **kleinen** Kindern." en="She likes working with small children." />

## Verbs that take the dative

Some verbs have a dative object where English would lead you to expect an ordinary one. They have to be learned as such.

| Verb | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| helfen | to help | Ich helfe **dir**. |
| danken | to thank | Wir danken **der** Lehrerin. |
| gehören | to belong to | Das Auto gehört **meinem** Bruder. |
| gefallen | to please (to like) | Das Kleid gefällt **mir**. |
| antworten | to answer | Er antwortet **dem** Chef nicht. |
| schmecken | to taste good to | Die Suppe schmeckt **den** Kindern. |
| gratulieren | to congratulate | Ich gratuliere **dir** zum Geburtstag. |

*gefallen* works the other way round from English *like*: the thing is the subject, the person is in the dative. *Das Kleid gefällt mir* is literally "the dress pleases me".

## Verbs with two objects

With *geben, schenken, zeigen, erklären, bringen, schicken* there is a person and a thing. The **person is dative**, the **thing is accusative**.

<Example de="Der junge Mann gibt **der alten Frau** **einen Brief**." en="The young man gives the old woman a letter." />

That one sentence contains three cases: *der junge Mann* (nominative, the subject), *der alten Frau* (dative, to whom), *einen Brief* (accusative, what).

## Prepositions that always take the dative

**aus, bei, mit, nach, seit, von, zu**

| Preposition | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| aus | out of, from | Sie kommt **aus der** Schweiz. |
| bei | at, near, at someone's place | Ich wohne **bei meinen** Eltern. |
| mit | with, by | Ich fahre **mit dem** Bus. |
| nach | after; to (cities, countries) | **Nach dem** Essen gehe ich spazieren. |
| seit | since, for (time) | Ich lerne **seit einem** Jahr Deutsch. |
| von | from, of | Das ist ein Geschenk **von meiner** Tante. |
| zu | to | Ich gehe **zum** Arzt. |

Short forms: *zu dem* = **zum**, *zu der* = **zur**, *von dem* = **vom**, *bei dem* = **beim**.

## Dative pronouns

| Nominative | Dative | | Nominative | Dative |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ich | **mir** | | wir | **uns** |
| du | **dir** | | ihr | **euch** |
| er / es | **ihm** | | sie (they) | **ihnen** |
| sie (she) | **ihr** | | Sie (formal) | **Ihnen** |

<Example de="Kannst du **mir** helfen? Ja, ich helfe **dir** gern." en="Can you help me? Yes, I am happy to help you." />

## A few masculine nouns add -n

A small group of masculine nouns add **-n** or **-en** in every case except the nominative: der Kollege → dem Kollege**n**, der Student → dem Student**en**, der Junge → dem Junge**n**, der Herr → dem Herr**n**.

<Example de="Ich arbeite mit einem neuen **Kollegen**." en="I work with a new colleague." />
