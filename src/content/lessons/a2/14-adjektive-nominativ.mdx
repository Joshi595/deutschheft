*The **new** flat is great.* In English *new* never changes. In German it does: *Die neu**e** Wohnung ist toll. Ein neu**er** Tisch. Neu**es** Glück.* Adjective endings are the part of German grammar that learners fear most, and the part examiners notice. The good news is that they follow one idea, and once you see it, most endings work themselves out. This chapter teaches that idea using only the nominative. The next two chapters add the accusative and the dative.

## The idea

**An adjective before a noun takes an ending. An adjective after the verb does not.**

*Der Tisch ist **neu**.* (no ending: after *sein*, *werden*, *bleiben*)
*Der **neue** Tisch.* (ending: before the noun)

Before the noun, German wants the **gender and case to be shown once** in the group *article + adjective + noun*. The ending of the adjective depends on how much the article has already shown:

- **The article shows it clearly** (*der, die, das*). The adjective can relax and takes the weak ending **-e** (or **-en**).
- **The article shows it only partly** (*ein* has no ending for masculine and neuter). The adjective takes over the job: **ein neu*er* Tisch, ein neu*es* Bett.**
- **There is no article.** The adjective does all the work and takes the ending the article would have had: **kalt*es* Wasser** (like *das*), **frisch*e* Milch** (like *die*).

Keep this "someone has to show it" idea in mind, and the tables below will make sense instead of being something to cram.

## Step by step

<Step n="1" title="After the verb: no ending">

After *sein, werden, bleiben* and similar verbs, the adjective is not part of the noun group. It never changes.

<Example de="Die Wohnung ist **groß**." en="The flat is big." />
<Example de="Die Kinder sind **müde**." en="The children are tired." />
<Example de="Das Wetter wird **schön**." en="The weather is going to be nice." />

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="After der, die, das: -e">

In the nominative singular, after the definite article, the ending is always **-e**. In the plural it is **-en**.

| | masculine | feminine | neuter | plural |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| nominative | der neu**e** Tisch | die neu**e** Lampe | das neu**e** Bett | die neu**en** Stühle |

<Example de="**Der neue** Kollege heißt Ben." en="The new colleague is called Ben." />
<Example de="**Die kleine** Küche ist sehr praktisch." en="The small kitchen is very practical." />
<Example de="**Die alten** Fenster sind kaputt." en="The old windows are broken." />

The same endings follow **dieser, jeder, welcher**: *Dieser rote Pullover gefällt mir.* *Welche neue Lehrerin?*

</Step>

<Step n="3" title="After ein, kein, mein: the adjective shows the gender">

*ein* has no ending in the masculine and the neuter, so you cannot tell from *ein* alone whether the noun is *der* or *das*. The adjective steps in with the ending of the definite article: **-er** for *der*, **-es** for *das*.

| | masculine | feminine | neuter | plural |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| nominative | ein neu**er** Tisch | eine neu**e** Lampe | ein neu**es** Bett | meine neu**en** Stühle |

<Example de="Das ist **ein guter** Film." en="That is a good film." />
<Example de="Hier ist **eine schöne** Wohnung." en="Here is a nice flat." />
<Example de="Das ist **ein großes** Problem." en="That is a big problem." />
<Example de="**Meine neuen** Nachbarn sind sehr nett." en="My new neighbours are very nice." />

*ein* has no plural. In the plural you use *kein, mein, dein …* and the adjective takes **-en**.

</Step>

<Step n="4" title="No article: the adjective does everything">

With no article (uncountable things, plurals, headlines, adverts), the adjective takes the ending of *der, die, das* itself.

| | masculine | feminine | neuter | plural |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| nominative | gut**er** Wein | frisch**e** Milch | kalt**es** Wasser | neu**e** Stühle |

<Example de="**Frischer** Kaffee ist der beste Kaffee." en="Fresh coffee is the best coffee." />
<Example de="**Kaltes** Wasser ist gesund." en="Cold water is healthy." />
<Example de="**Neue** Wohnungen sind teuer." en="New flats are expensive." />

You see this constantly in adverts and signs: *Schöne 3-Zimmer-Wohnung, frisches Obst, günstiger Preis*.

</Step>

<Step n="5" title="Spelling changes">

A few adjectives change slightly when they take an ending.

| Adjective | With ending |
| --- | --- |
| teuer | ein teu**r**es Auto |
| dunkel | ein dunk**l**er Raum |
| hoch | ein ho**h**es Haus |
| rosa, lila, orange | no ending: ein rosa Kleid |

Two adjectives in a row both take the same ending: *ein **schöner, großer** Garten*.

</Step>

<Step n="6" title="How to work it out quickly">

When you are not sure, ask two questions.

1. **Is there an article with an ending** (*der, die, das, dieser, eine, meine*)? Then the adjective takes **-e** in the singular and **-en** in the plural.
2. **Is the article** *ein* **without an ending, or is there no article?** Then the adjective shows the gender: **-er** for masculine, **-es** for neuter, **-e** for feminine and plural.

Check yourself with *der Wagen* and *das Auto*: *der neue Wagen*, but *ein neuer Wagen*; *das neue Auto*, but *ein neues Auto*.

</Step>

## Typical mistakes

<Mistake wrong="Der Tisch ist **neuer**." right="Der Tisch ist **neu**." why="After sein the adjective has no ending. (neuer here would mean 'newer'.)" />

<Mistake wrong="Das ist ein **gut** Film." right="Das ist ein **guter** Film." why="Before the noun, the adjective must have an ending. After ein, masculine is -er." />

<Mistake wrong="Das ist ein **große** Problem." right="Das ist ein **großes** Problem." why="das Problem is neuter, and ein does not show it, so the adjective takes -es." />

<Mistake wrong="Der **neuer** Kollege ist nett." right="Der **neue** Kollege ist nett." why="der already shows masculine, so the adjective takes only -e." />

<Mistake wrong="Die **neue** Nachbarn sind nett." right="Die **neuen** Nachbarn sind nett." why="After die in the plural, the ending is -en." />

## Summary

<Summary>

| Nominative | masculine | feminine | neuter | plural |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| der / dieser | der neu**e** | die neu**e** | das neu**e** | die neu**en** |
| ein / kein / mein | ein neu**er** | eine neu**e** | ein neu**es** | meine neu**en** |
| no article | neu**er** | neu**e** | neu**es** | neu**e** |

**After the verb:** no ending. *Der Tisch ist neu.*

**The rule of thumb:** the gender must show once. If the article shows it, the adjective takes -e or -en. If not, the adjective shows it: -er, -e, -es.

</Summary>
