The reading module of the Goethe-Zertifikat A2 lasts **30 minutes** and has **four parts** with **five questions each**, twenty in all. You do not need to understand every word. You need to find the sentence that answers the question, and recognise when a text says something *similar but different*. That is a skill you can train.

## What this part tests

| Part | Text | Task | Questions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Teil 1** | a short newspaper or magazine article | multiple choice a, b or c | 5 |
| **Teil 2** | an information board, e.g. a department store directory or a programme | which floor or place: a, b or c | 5 |
| **Teil 3** | an email or letter | multiple choice a, b or c | 5 |
| **Teil 4** | six short adverts and five people with a wish | which advert fits each person, or **X** if none does | 5 |

Each question has exactly one right answer. The wrong options are built from words that **do appear** in the text, but with a different meaning, time, person or number. The most common trap is an option that repeats words from the text but changes one detail.

## How to approach it

<Step n="1" title="Read the questions first">

Before you read a text, read its five questions. Underline the key word in each one: a time, a place, a person, a reason. Then read the text looking only for those things. The questions follow the order of the text in Teil 1 and Teil 3.

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="Teil 1 and 3: look for the changed detail">

When an option uses the same words as the text, check the details very carefully: who, when, how many, yes or no.

<Example de="Text: Das Café ist montags geschlossen, sonst täglich von 9 bis 18 Uhr geöffnet." />
<Example de="Option: Das Café ist jeden Tag geöffnet." en="Wrong – it is closed on Mondays." />

Words that often decide the answer: *nicht, kein, nur, erst, noch, schon, früher, jetzt, ab, bis*.

</Step>

<Step n="3" title="Teil 2: the directory">

A typical text is a list of departments on each floor (*Erdgeschoss, 1. Stock, 2. Stock, Untergeschoss*). The question describes what someone needs, not the name of the department. *Sie brauchen ein Geschenk für ein Baby* means the floor with *Babyartikel* or *Kinderkleidung*. Think of the **category**, then find it on the board.

| Floor | German |
| --- | --- |
| basement | das Untergeschoss (UG) |
| ground floor | das Erdgeschoss (EG) |
| first floor | der 1. Stock / das 1. Obergeschoss (OG) |

The third option is often *anderer Stock*: choose it only when the thing is really nowhere on the board.

</Step>

<Step n="4" title="Teil 4: the adverts, and X">

Each person has **two or three conditions**: what, when, how much. An advert fits only if **all** conditions fit. Exactly one person has **no** matching advert, and the answer is **X**. If you cannot find an advert after checking all six, do not force one.

</Step>

<Step n="5" title="Manage the time">

Thirty minutes for twenty questions is about **seven minutes per part**, with two minutes at the end to transfer your answers. If a question is hard, mark a guess and move on. A blank answer gives nothing; a guess gives a one-in-three chance.

</Step>
