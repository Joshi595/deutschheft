*Anna's car, my brother's car, the car of the neighbours.* English has two ways to say whose something is, and German has three. One is very easy (*Annas Auto*), one is what people say in conversation (*das Auto von meinem Bruder*), and one is what people write (*das Auto meines Bruders*). At A2 you should use the first two confidently and recognise the third. That is what this chapter teaches, together with the words for *mine* and *yours*.

## The idea

| Way | Example | Where you meet it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| name + **-s** | **Annas** Auto | everywhere, with names |
| **von** + dative | das Auto **von meinem** Bruder | speech, informal writing |
| genitive | das Auto **meines** Bruder**s** | writing, formal speech, fixed phrases |

The genitive is a fourth case, after the nominative, accusative and dative. In everyday speech it has mostly been replaced by *von* + dative, which you already know. But it survives in writing, in the news, in official letters and in many fixed expressions, so you need to recognise it. At B1 you will use it actively.

## Step by step

<Step n="1" title="Names: add -s, no apostrophe">

<Example de="Das ist **Annas** Auto." en="That is Anna's car." />
<Example de="**Peters** Schwester wohnt in Wien." en="Peter's sister lives in Vienna." />
<Example de="Wo ist **Mamas** Handy?" en="Where is Mum's phone?" />

German writes **no apostrophe** here. Only when a name already ends in an *s* sound does German use one: *Klaus' Auto*, *Max' Bruder*. In speech people usually avoid that and say *das Auto von Klaus*.

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="von + dative: the spoken way">

Put the thing first, then **von** and the owner in the dative.

<Example de="Das ist das Auto **von meinem** Bruder." en="That is my brother's car." />
<Example de="Die Wohnung **von meiner** Freundin ist sehr groß." en="My girlfriend's flat is very big." />
<Example de="Das ist das Zimmer **von den** Kindern." en="That is the children's room." />

This always works, it is never wrong in conversation, and you already know all the forms from chapter 10.

</Step>

<Step n="3" title="The genitive articles">

| | masculine | feminine | neuter | plural |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| definite | **des** Mann**es** | **der** Frau | **des** Kind**es** | **der** Kinder |
| indefinite | **eines** Mann**es** | **einer** Frau | **eines** Kind**es** | – |
| possessive | **meines** Bruder**s** | **meiner** Schwester | **meines** Kind**es** | **meiner** Eltern |

Masculine and neuter nouns take **-s** or **-es**: **-es** after one syllable (*des Mannes, des Kindes, des Jahres*), **-s** after longer nouns (*des Bruders, des Lehrers, des Autos*). Feminine and plural nouns do not change.

<Example de="Das ist das Auto **meines** Bruder**s**." en="That is my brother's car." />
<Example de="Das Büro **der** Chefin ist im dritten Stock." en="The boss's office is on the third floor." />

The genitive comes **after** the noun it belongs to: *das Auto des Lehrers* (the teacher's car).

</Step>

<Step n="4" title="Genitive phrases you will meet">

Some phrases always use the genitive. Learn them as fixed expressions.

| German | English |
| --- | --- |
| Ende **des** Monats | at the end of the month |
| Anfang **des** Jahres | at the beginning of the year |
| die Hälfte **der** Zeit | half the time |
| der Name **der** Straße | the name of the street |
| **wegen des** Wetters | because of the weather |
| **während der** Arbeit | during work |

<Example de="Die Miete zahle ich **Ende des Monats**." en="I pay the rent at the end of the month." />
<Example de="Das Konzert fällt **wegen des Wetters** aus." en="The concert is cancelled because of the weather." />

In speech, *wegen* is often heard with the dative: *wegen dem Wetter*. In the exam, write the genitive.

</Step>

<Step n="5" title="Asking: Wessen? and Wem gehört …?">

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| **Wessen** Tasche ist das? | Das ist **Annas** Tasche. |
| **Wem gehört** die Tasche? | Sie gehört **Anna**. |
| **Von wem** ist das Geschenk? | **Von meiner** Oma. |

*Wessen* is the genitive question word. It is correct but formal; in conversation people prefer *Wem gehört …?*

</Step>

<Step n="6" title="Mine, yours, his: meiner, meine, meins">

When the noun is left out, the possessive takes the ending of the article it replaces: *der → meiner*, *die → meine*, *das → meins*.

| Noun | Possessive | Without noun |
| --- | --- | --- |
| der Schlüssel | mein Schlüssel | Das ist **meiner**. |
| die Tasche | meine Tasche | Das ist **meine**. |
| das Handy | mein Handy | Das ist **meins**. |
| die Schuhe | meine Schuhe | Das sind **meine**. |

<Example de="Ist das dein Handy? – Nein, das ist nicht **meins**. Das ist **seins**." en="Is that your phone? – No, it is not mine. It is his." />
<Example de="Wem gehört der Schlüssel? – Das ist **meiner**." en="Whose key is this? – It is mine." />

The same works for *dein, sein, ihr, unser, euer*: *deiner, deine, deins; ihrer, ihre, ihrs*.

</Step>

## Compared with English

<Compare en="**Anna's** car" de="**Annas** Auto" note="Same idea, but no apostrophe in German." />

<Compare en="my **brother's** car" de="das Auto **von meinem** Bruder / das Auto **meines Bruders**" note="English puts the owner first. In German the owner normally comes after the thing (except for names)." />

<Compare en="That is **mine**." de="Das ist **meiner / meine / meins**." note="English has one form. German matches the gender of the thing." />

## Typical mistakes

<Mistake wrong="Das ist **Anna's** Auto." right="Das ist **Annas** Auto." why="German does not use an apostrophe before the possessive -s." />

<Mistake wrong="Das ist **mein Bruders** Auto." right="Das ist das Auto **von meinem Bruder**." why="Only names go in front with -s. With a noun, use von + dative or the genitive after the thing." />

<Mistake wrong="das Auto von **mein** Bruder" right="das Auto von **meinem** Bruder" why="von takes the dative." />

<Mistake wrong="Das Handy ist **mein**." right="Das Handy ist **meins**." why="Without a noun, the possessive needs an ending: meins for das." />

<Mistake wrong="das Büro **des** Chefin" right="das Büro **der** Chefin" why="Feminine genitive is der, and the noun does not change." />
