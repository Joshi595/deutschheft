**Warum?** (why?) is one of the questions you will be asked most, in life and in the exam: *Warum lernen Sie Deutsch? Warum können Sie nicht kommen?* At A1 you answered with *denn*. The normal answer in spoken German uses **weil**, and *weil* introduces the most important new piece of grammar at A2.

## The idea

Up to now every sentence you have built had the verb in **second place**. That is the rule for a **main clause**, a sentence that can stand alone.

German has a second type, the **subordinate clause**: a part of a sentence that cannot stand alone, because it depends on another part. *…because I am tired* is not a sentence on its own. In a subordinate clause the rule is different and absolute:

**the conjugated verb goes to the very end.**

*weil* is the first word you meet that opens such a clause. *dass*, *wenn*, *ob* and the others in this unit all work the same way. So what you learn here, you learn once for all of them.

## Step by step

<Step n="1" title="The verb goes to the end">

| Two sentences | Joined with weil |
| --- | --- |
| Ich gehe nach Hause. Ich **bin** müde. | Ich gehe nach Hause, weil ich müde **bin**. |
| Sie lernt Deutsch. Sie **arbeitet** in Berlin. | Sie lernt Deutsch, weil sie in Berlin **arbeitet**. |
| Er kommt nicht. Er **hat** keine Zeit. | Er kommt nicht, weil er keine Zeit **hat**. |

<Example de="Ich gehe nach Hause, **weil** ich müde **bin**." en="I am going home because I am tired." />
<Example de="Sie lernt Deutsch, **weil** sie in Berlin **arbeitet**." en="She is learning German because she works in Berlin." />
<Example de="Er kommt nicht, **weil** er keine Zeit **hat**." en="He is not coming because he has no time." />

Everything else in the clause keeps its order: subject first, then the rest, then the verb. And there is always a **comma** before *weil*.

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="With a modal verb">

There are two verbs now. The **conjugated** one is the modal, so the modal goes last. The infinitive stands just before it.

<Example de="Ich mache das, weil ich dir helfen **möchte**." en="I am doing this because I would like to help you." />
<Example de="Er lernt viel, weil er die Prüfung bestehen **will**." en="He studies a lot because he wants to pass the exam." />
<Example de="Sie bleibt zu Hause, weil sie arbeiten **muss**." en="She is staying at home because she has to work." />

Compare the order: main clause *ich **möchte** dir **helfen***, weil clause *weil ich dir **helfen möchte***. The two verbs swap places.

</Step>

<Step n="3" title="In the Perfekt">

The conjugated verb is *haben* or *sein*, so that goes last, after the participle.

<Example de="Ich rufe ihn nicht an, weil ich seine Nummer vergessen **habe**." en="I am not calling him because I have forgotten his number." />
<Example de="Sie ist müde, weil sie schlecht geschlafen **hat**." en="She is tired because she slept badly." />
<Example de="Wir sind zu spät, weil der Bus nicht gekommen **ist**." en="We are late because the bus did not come." />

</Step>

<Step n="4" title="With a separable verb">

At the end of a subordinate clause the two parts of a separable verb come back together and are written as **one word**.

<Example de="Wir müssen gehen, weil der Zug um acht Uhr **abfährt**." en="We have to go because the train leaves at eight." />
<Example de="Ich bin müde, weil ich jeden Tag um fünf **aufstehe**." en="I am tired because I get up at five every day." />

Main clause: *Der Zug **fährt** um acht **ab**.* Subordinate clause: *weil der Zug um acht **abfährt***.

</Step>

<Step n="5" title="The reason first">

You can begin with the *weil* clause. Then the whole clause counts as position 1, and the main clause must put its verb in position 2, **directly after the comma**.

<Example de="**Weil** ich krank **bin**, **gehe** ich zum Arzt." en="Because I am ill, I am going to the doctor." />
<Example de="**Weil** es **regnet**, **bleiben** wir zu Hause." en="Because it is raining, we are staying at home." />

Two verbs meet at the comma: *bin, gehe*. That looks strange and is correct. It is the surest sign of a sentence that starts with a subordinate clause.

</Step>

<Step n="6" title="Short answers">

In conversation the *weil* clause often stands alone, as the answer to *Warum?*

<Example de="Warum kommst du nicht mit? Weil ich keine Lust habe." en="Why aren't you coming along? Because I don't feel like it." />
<Example de="Warum lernst du Deutsch? Weil ich in Deutschland studieren möchte." en="Why are you learning German? Because I would like to study in Germany." />

</Step>

<Step n="7" title="weil or denn">

Both mean *because*. The difference is only grammatical.

| | weil | denn |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clause type | subordinate | main |
| Verb | at the end | second, as normal |
| Can start the sentence | yes | no |
| Style | everyday speech | slightly more written |

<Example de="Ich bleibe zu Hause, **weil** ich krank **bin**." en="I am staying at home because I am ill." />
<Example de="Ich bleibe zu Hause, **denn** ich **bin** krank." en="I am staying at home, for I am ill." />

If you are unsure of the word order, *denn* is the safe choice. But you need *weil* to understand others, and the examiners expect to hear it.

</Step>

<Step n="8" title="Useful reasons">

| German | English |
| --- | --- |
| weil ich müde bin | because I am tired |
| weil ich keine Zeit habe | because I have no time |
| weil ich arbeiten muss | because I have to work |
| weil es mir Spaß macht | because I enjoy it |
| weil ich kein Geld habe | because I have no money |
| weil das Wetter schlecht ist | because the weather is bad |
| weil ich mich nicht gut fühle | because I do not feel well |

</Step>

## Compared with English

<Compare en="…because I **am** tired." de="…, weil ich müde **bin**." note="English keeps the verb next to the subject. German sends it to the end of the clause." />

<Compare en="…because I **want to help** you." de="…, weil ich dir **helfen möchte**." note="With two verbs the German order is the mirror image of the English: infinitive first, then the conjugated verb." />

<Compare en="**Because** I am ill, **I am going** to the doctor." de="**Weil** ich krank bin, **gehe ich** zum Arzt." note="After an opening subordinate clause, English continues with the subject. German continues with the verb." />

<Compare en="because (one word)" de="**weil** and **denn**" note="Two words with the same meaning and different grammar." />

## Typical mistakes

<Mistake wrong="Ich gehe nach Hause, weil ich **bin** müde." right="Ich gehe nach Hause, weil ich müde **bin**." why="In a weil clause the conjugated verb stands at the end. This is the most common A2 mistake of all." />

<Mistake wrong="…, weil ich **möchte** dir helfen." right="…, weil ich dir helfen **möchte**." why="The conjugated modal goes last, after the infinitive." />

<Mistake wrong="Weil ich krank bin, **ich gehe** zum Arzt." right="Weil ich krank bin, **gehe ich** zum Arzt." why="The weil clause is position 1, so the main verb comes straight after the comma." />

<Mistake wrong="…, weil der Zug um acht **fährt ab**." right="…, weil der Zug um acht **abfährt**." why="At the end of a subordinate clause a separable verb is written as one word." />

<Mistake wrong="Ich bleibe zu Hause, **denn** ich krank **bin**." right="Ich bleibe zu Hause, **denn** ich **bin** krank." why="denn does not send the verb to the end. Only weil does." />

## Summary

<Summary>

**weil + subject + … + conjugated verb.** Always a comma before weil.

| Verb type | Main clause | weil clause |
| --- | --- | --- |
| simple | Ich **bin** müde. | weil ich müde **bin** |
| modal | Ich **muss** arbeiten. | weil ich arbeiten **muss** |
| Perfekt | Ich **habe** es vergessen. | weil ich es vergessen **habe** |
| separable | Der Zug **fährt** um acht **ab**. | weil der Zug um acht **abfährt** |

**weil clause first:** *Weil ich krank bin, **gehe** ich zum Arzt.* (verb, comma, verb)

**denn** = because, with normal word order, never at the start.

</Summary>
