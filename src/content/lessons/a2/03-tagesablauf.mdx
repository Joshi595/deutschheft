Describing an ordinary day needs a small set of verbs, a few time words, and one word-order rule: the verb is always the second element of the sentence.

## Present tense

Regular verbs take these endings on the stem. *machen* (to do, to make) has the stem *mach-*:

| Pronoun | machen | arbeiten |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ich | mach**e** | arbeit**e** |
| du | mach**st** | arbeit**est** |
| er / sie / es | mach**t** | arbeit**et** |
| wir | mach**en** | arbeit**en** |
| ihr | mach**t** | arbeit**et** |
| sie / Sie | mach**en** | arbeit**en** |

Stems ending in **-t** or **-d** (*arbeiten*, *finden*) add an extra **e** before *-st* and *-t* so that the ending can be pronounced.

<GrammarBox title="Some verbs change their vowel" label="Watch out">

A number of very common verbs change the stem vowel, but only in the **du** and **er / sie / es** forms.

| Infinitive | du | er / sie / es |
| --- | --- | --- |
| lesen (to read) | l**ie**st | l**ie**st |
| essen (to eat) | **i**sst | **i**sst |
| sehen (to see) | s**ie**hst | s**ie**ht |
| treffen (to meet) | tr**i**ffst | tr**i**fft |
| fahren (to drive, go) | f**ä**hrst | f**ä**hrt |
| waschen (to wash) | w**ä**schst | w**ä**scht |

</GrammarBox>

## Separable verbs

Many routine verbs have a prefix that splits off and goes to the end of the sentence: **auf**stehen, **ein**kaufen, **fern**sehen, **an**rufen, **an**ziehen.

<Example de="Ich **stehe** um sieben Uhr **auf**." en="I get up at seven o'clock." />
<Example de="Am Samstag **kaufe** ich im Supermarkt **ein**." en="On Saturday I do the shopping at the supermarket." />
<Example de="Abends **sieht** er **fern**." en="In the evening he watches TV." />

## Reflexive verbs

Things you do to yourself use a reflexive pronoun: *sich waschen* (to wash), *sich anziehen* (to get dressed), *sich treffen* (to meet up).

| Singular | Plural |
| --- | --- |
| ich wasche **mich** | wir waschen **uns** |
| du wäschst **dich** | ihr wascht **euch** |
| er / sie wäscht **sich** | sie / Sie waschen **sich** |

<Example de="Ich wasche **mich** und ziehe **mich** an." en="I wash and get dressed." />
<Example de="Wir treffen **uns** am Nachmittag." en="We are meeting up in the afternoon." />

When a body part is named, the pronoun is dative: *Ich putze **mir** die Zähne* (I brush my teeth).

## When and how often

| Time of day | | How often | |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| morgens | in the morning | immer | always |
| mittags | at midday | meistens | usually |
| nachmittags | in the afternoon | oft | often |
| abends | in the evening | manchmal | sometimes |
| nachts | at night | selten | rarely |
| jeden Tag | every day | nie | never |

Clock times take **um** (*um acht Uhr*), days take **am** (*am Montag*), and a span is **von … bis** (*von neun bis siebzehn Uhr*).

## The verb comes second

The conjugated verb is always the second element. If you start with a time expression, the subject has to move behind the verb.

<Example de="Ich **trinke** morgens Kaffee." en="I drink coffee in the morning." />
<Example de="Morgens **trinke** ich Kaffee." en="In the morning I drink coffee." />
<Example wrong de="Morgens ich trinke Kaffee." />

When a sentence says both *when* and *where*, time comes before place:

<Example de="Ich fahre **um acht Uhr** **zur Arbeit**." en="I go to work at eight o'clock." />
