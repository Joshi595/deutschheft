Talking about family, friends or colleagues, you soon need to say what they are *like*. German does this the same way English does: a form of **sein** (to be) plus an adjective.

## Asking what someone is like

The question uses **wie** (how), not *was* (what):

<Example de="**Wie** ist deine Mutter?" en="What is your mother like?" />
<Example de="Sie ist sehr freundlich und humorvoll." en="She is very friendly and funny." />
<Example de="**Wie** sind deine neuen Kollegen?" en="What are your new colleagues like?" />
<Example de="Sie sind nett, aber ein bisschen schüchtern." en="They are nice, but a little shy." />

## sein

You need all six forms, because you will describe yourself, the person you are talking to, and other people.

| Pronoun | sein | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ich | **bin** | I am |
| du | **bist** | you are (one person, informal) |
| er / sie / es | **ist** | he / she / it is |
| wir | **sind** | we are |
| ihr | **seid** | you are (several people, informal) |
| sie / Sie | **sind** | they are / you are (formal) |

<GrammarBox title="After sein, the adjective takes no ending">

An adjective that follows *sein* stays in its dictionary form, whatever the gender or number of the person described.

<Example de="Der Mann ist **freundlich**." en="The man is friendly." />
<Example de="Die Frau ist **freundlich**." en="The woman is friendly." />
<Example de="Das Kind ist **freundlich**." en="The child is friendly." />
<Example de="Die Kinder sind **freundlich**." en="The children are friendly." />

A common mistake is to add an ending here:

<Example wrong de="Die Frau ist **freundliche**." />

Endings appear only when the adjective stands directly in front of a noun (*eine freundliche Frau*). Lessons 5, 6 and 8 deal with those.

</GrammarBox>

The same holds after **wirken** (to seem) and **bleiben** (to stay):

<Example de="Er wirkt sehr selbstbewusst." en="He seems very self-confident." />
<Example de="Sie bleibt immer optimistisch." en="She always stays optimistic." />

## Stronger and weaker

Put one of these in front of the adjective to say *how* friendly, lazy or shy someone is.

| German | English | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **sehr** | very | sehr freundlich |
| **ziemlich** | quite, rather | ziemlich intelligent |
| **besonders** | especially | besonders fleißig |
| **ein bisschen** | a little | ein bisschen schüchtern |
| **nicht sehr** | not very | nicht sehr geduldig |
| **gar nicht** | not at all | gar nicht faul |
| **zu** | too | zu pessimistisch |
| **viel zu** | far too | viel zu faul |

## Opposites

Many adjectives form their opposite with the prefix **un-**:

- freundlich → **un**freundlich
- ehrlich → **un**ehrlich
- geduldig → **un**geduldig
- zuverlässig → **un**zuverlässig

Others have an opposite that is a different word altogether:

- fleißig (hard-working) ↔ faul (lazy)
- mutig (brave) ↔ feige (cowardly)
- optimistisch ↔ pessimistisch

You cannot guess which adjectives take *un-*, so learn the opposite together with the word.
