*Tomorrow I am going to the cinema. Next year I will learn Spanish. It is going to rain.* English has several ways to talk about the future, and learners naturally look for the German "will". It exists: **werden**. But Germans use it much less than English speakers use *will*, because German usually says the future with the plain **present tense**. This chapter shows when each one is right.

## The idea

German has two ways to talk about the future.

1. **Present tense + a time expression.** This is the normal way for plans and arrangements. The time expression makes it clear that it is the future.
   *Morgen **fahre** ich nach Berlin.* (Tomorrow I am going to Berlin.)

2. **werden + infinitive.** This is the "real" future tense. It adds a meaning: a **promise**, a **prediction**, a **resolution**, or a future that is **not obvious** from the context.
   *Ich **werde** dich anrufen.* (I will call you – I promise.)

If in doubt, use the present tense. It is never wrong when the time is clear, and it sounds natural.

## Step by step

<Step n="1" title="The present tense for the future">

<Example de="**Morgen** arbeite ich bis sechs." en="Tomorrow I am working until six." />
<Example de="**Nächste Woche** fliegen wir nach Spanien." en="Next week we are flying to Spain." />
<Example de="Was machst du **am Wochenende**?" en="What are you doing at the weekend?" />

English often uses *going to* or the *-ing* form here. German simply uses the present.

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="werden: the forms">

*werden* is irregular in the *du* and *er* forms.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| ich **werde** | wir **werden** |
| du **wirst** | ihr **werdet** |
| er/sie/es **wird** | sie/Sie **werden** |

Like a modal verb, *werden* stands in position 2 and the **infinitive goes to the end**.

<Example de="Ich **werde** morgen früher **kommen**." en="I will come earlier tomorrow." />
<Example de="**Wirst** du mir **helfen**?" en="Will you help me?" />

</Step>

<Step n="3" title="Promises and resolutions">

When you promise something or make a resolution, *werden* gives it weight.

<Example de="Ich **werde** dich nie **vergessen**." en="I will never forget you." />
<Example de="Keine Sorge, ich **werde** pünktlich **sein**." en="Don't worry, I will be on time." />
<Example de="Nächstes Jahr **werde** ich mehr Sport **machen**." en="Next year I will do more sport." />

</Step>

<Step n="4" title="Predictions">

Weather forecasts, expectations and guesses about the future often use *werden*.

<Example de="Morgen **wird** es **regnen**." en="It will rain tomorrow." />
<Example de="Das **wird** bestimmt teuer **werden**." en="That is sure to get expensive." />
<Example de="In zwanzig Jahren **werden** wir alle elektrisch **fahren**." en="In twenty years we will all drive electric cars." />

</Step>

<Step n="5" title="werden in a subordinate clause">

In a clause with *dass, weil, wenn* …, the conjugated verb goes to the end, so *werden* comes **after** the infinitive.

<Example de="Ich glaube, dass es morgen **regnen wird**." en="I think that it will rain tomorrow." />
<Example de="Er sagt, dass er das Geld **zurückzahlen wird**." en="He says that he will pay the money back." />

</Step>

<Step n="6" title="werden on its own: to become">

Without a second verb, *werden* means **to become** or **to get**. This is actually its most frequent use.

<Example de="Ich möchte Ärztin **werden**." en="I would like to become a doctor." />
<Example de="Es **wird** dunkel." en="It is getting dark." />
<Example de="Er **wird** im Mai dreißig." en="He turns thirty in May." />

The Perfekt is *ist geworden*: *Sie **ist** Lehrerin **geworden**.* (She became a teacher.) *Es ist kalt geworden.* (It has got cold.)

</Step>

## Compared with English

<Compare en="I **am going** to Berlin tomorrow." de="Ich **fahre** morgen nach Berlin." note="The German present tense covers 'I go', 'I am going' and 'I am going to go'." />

<Compare en="I **will** call you." de="Ich **werde** dich anrufen. / Ich rufe dich an." note="Both are correct. werden makes it sound like a promise." />

<Compare en="I **want** to become a doctor." de="Ich **will** Arzt **werden**." note="German will means 'want'. The German for 'will' is werde. A classic false friend." />

<Compare en="It is **getting** cold." de="Es **wird** kalt." note="'get' + adjective is werden." />

## Typical mistakes

<Mistake wrong="Ich **will** morgen kommen. (meaning: I will come)" right="Ich **werde** morgen kommen. / Ich komme morgen." why="ich will means 'I want'." />

<Mistake wrong="Ich werde **kommen** morgen." right="Ich werde morgen **kommen**." why="The infinitive goes to the end." />

<Mistake wrong="Du **wirdst** das schaffen." right="Du **wirst** das schaffen." why="du wirst, er wird: irregular." />

<Mistake wrong="Er sagt, dass er morgen **wird kommen**." right="Er sagt, dass er morgen **kommen wird**." why="In a dass clause the conjugated verb goes last." />

## Summary

<Summary>

| Use | Form | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| plans, arrangements | present + time | Morgen fahre ich nach Berlin. |
| promise, resolution | werden + infinitive | Ich werde dich anrufen. |
| prediction | werden + infinitive | Morgen wird es regnen. |
| to become, to get | werden alone | Es wird dunkel. Sie ist Ärztin geworden. |

**werden:** ich werde, du wirst, er wird, wir werden, ihr werdet, sie werden

**Word order:** Ich werde morgen **kommen**. · …, dass ich morgen **kommen werde**.

**False friend:** ich will = I want.

</Summary>
