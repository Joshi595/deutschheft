The **genitive** shows that one thing belongs to another. It answers the question **Wessen?** (whose?). English does the same job with *'s* or *of*.

<Example de="das Auto **des Mannes**" en="the man's car" />
<Example de="die Farbe **des Hauses**" en="the colour of the house" />

The owner comes second in German, the other way round from English *the man's car*.

## The articles

| | Definite | Indefinite | Possessive |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| masculine | **des** Mann**es** | **eines** Mann**es** | **meines** Mann**es** |
| feminine | **der** Frau | **einer** Frau | **meiner** Frau |
| neuter | **des** Kind**es** | **eines** Kind**es** | **meines** Kind**es** |
| plural | **der** Kinder | (none) | **meiner** Kinder |

<GrammarBox title="Masculine and neuter nouns take an ending too">

In the genitive it is not only the article that changes. Masculine and neuter **nouns add -s or -es**.

- **-es** for nouns of one syllable, and for nouns ending in *-s, -ß, -x, -z*: des Mann**es**, des Kind**es**, des Haus**es**, des Platz**es**.
- **-s** for longer nouns: des Lehrer**s**, des Auto**s**, des Mädchen**s**, des Computer**s**.

Feminine and plural nouns add nothing: der Frau, der Kinder.

<Example de="Das Buch **des Lehrers** liegt auf dem Tisch." en="The teacher's book is on the table." />
<Example de="Die Tasche **der Lehrerin** ist schwarz." en="The (female) teacher's bag is black." />
<Example wrong de="das Buch **des Lehrer**" />

</GrammarBox>

## All four cases

| | masculine | feminine | neuter | plural |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nominative | der Mann | die Frau | das Kind | die Kinder |
| Accusative | den Mann | die Frau | das Kind | die Kinder |
| Dative | dem Mann | der Frau | dem Kind | den Kindern |
| Genitive | des Mannes | der Frau | des Kindes | der Kinder |

Feminine has *der* in both the dative and the genitive. The plural does not: dative **den** Kinder**n**, genitive **der** Kinder.

## Adjectives

After an article, genitive adjectives end in **-en**, like in the dative.

<Example de="das Auto des **alten** Mannes" en="the old man's car" />
<Example de="der Name der **neuen** Kollegin" en="the new colleague's name" />

## Names

Names simply add **-s**, with no apostrophe, and stand in front as in English.

<Example de="**Annas** Auto ist neu." en="Anna's car is new." />
<Example de="Das ist **Peters** Bruder." en="That is Peter's brother." />

## von + dative in everyday speech

In spoken German the genitive is often replaced by **von** with the dative. Both are correct; the genitive sounds more formal and is the norm in writing.

<Example de="das Auto **meines Vaters**" en="my father's car (written, formal)" />
<Example de="das Auto **von meinem Vater**" en="my father's car (spoken)" />

## Prepositions with the genitive

| Preposition | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **wegen** | because of | wegen des Wetters |
| **trotz** | despite | trotz des Regens |
| **während** | during | während des Unterrichts |
| **statt** | instead of | statt eines Autos |
| **innerhalb** | within, inside | innerhalb der Stadt |
| **außerhalb** | outside | außerhalb der Stadt |

<Example de="**Wegen des Regens** bleibe ich zu Hause." en="Because of the rain I am staying at home." />
<Example de="**Trotz der Kälte** gehen wir spazieren." en="Despite the cold we are going for a walk." />
<Example de="**Während der Pause** trinken wir einen Kaffee." en="During the break we have a coffee." />

*wegen* does the same job as *weil* from lesson 2, but it is followed by a noun rather than a whole clause:

<Example de="Ich bleibe zu Hause, **weil es regnet**." en="I am staying at home because it is raining." />
<Example de="Ich bleibe **wegen des Regens** zu Hause." en="I am staying at home because of the rain." />
