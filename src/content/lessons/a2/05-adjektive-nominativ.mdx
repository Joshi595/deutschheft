In lesson 1 the adjective came after *sein* and never changed: *Der Mann ist nett.* As soon as an adjective stands **directly in front of a noun**, it takes an ending.

<Example de="Der Mann ist **nett**." en="The man is nice. (after sein: no ending)" />
<Example de="Der **nette** Mann wohnt hier." en="The nice man lives here. (before a noun: ending)" />

This lesson covers the **nominative**, the case of the subject. It answers *Wer?* (who?) or *Was?* (what?), and it is also the case used after *sein*:

<Example de="**Der neue Lehrer** kommt aus Wien." en="The new teacher comes from Vienna." />
<Example de="Das ist **ein neuer Lehrer**." en="That is a new teacher." />

## One idea behind all the endings

Somewhere in front of the noun, its gender has to be visible, and it only needs to be visible once.

- *der*, *die*, *das* already show the gender, so the adjective can relax and take a plain **-e**.
- *ein* does not tell you whether the noun is masculine or neuter, so the adjective has to do it: **-er** (like d*er*) or **-es** (like da*s*).

## The endings

| | der / die / das | ein / kein / mein … | no article |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| masculine | der nett**e** Mann | ein nett**er** Mann | nett**er** Mann |
| feminine | die nett**e** Frau | eine nett**e** Frau | nett**e** Frau |
| neuter | das nett**e** Kind | ein nett**es** Kind | nett**es** Kind |
| plural | die nett**en** Kinder | keine nett**en** Kinder | nett**e** Kinder |

A few things to notice:

- After **der / die / das** in the singular the ending is always **-e**.
- **kein** and the possessives (**mein, dein, sein, ihr, unser, euer**) behave exactly like **ein**.
- *ein* has no plural. In the plural, after *die*, *keine* or *meine*, the ending is **-en**.
- With **no article** at all, the adjective carries the full signal itself: **-er, -e, -es**, and **-e** in the plural.

<GrammarBox title="The two mistakes to avoid" label="Watch out">

Using the *der* pattern after *ein*:

<Example wrong de="ein **gute** Mann" />
<Example de="ein **guter** Mann" en="a good man" />

Using the plural ending in the singular:

<Example wrong de="die **schönen** Stadt" />
<Example de="die **schöne** Stadt" en="the beautiful city" />

</GrammarBox>

## Examples

<Example de="Der **alte** Mann sitzt im Park." en="The old man is sitting in the park." />
<Example de="Ein **kleines** Kind spielt im Garten." en="A small child is playing in the garden." />
<Example de="Meine **neue** Kollegin ist sehr freundlich." en="My new colleague is very friendly." />
<Example de="Das ist kein **guter** Film." en="That is not a good film." />
<Example de="Die **neuen** Nachbarn sind nett." en="The new neighbours are nice." />
<Example de="**Frisches** Brot schmeckt am besten." en="Fresh bread tastes best." />

## Small spelling changes

- Adjectives ending in **-el** and **-er** usually drop that *e* when an ending is added: *dunkel* → ein dunk**les** Zimmer, *teuer* → ein teu**res** Auto.
- **hoch** loses its *c*: ein **hoher** Berg.

The accusative follows in the next lesson. It differs from the nominative in exactly one place.
