**wenn** means *if* and also *whenever*. It links a condition to its result.

<Example de="**Wenn** ich Zeit **habe**, **gehe** ich ins Kino." en="If I have time, I go to the cinema." />

It works exactly like *weil* in lesson 2: *wenn* opens a subordinate clause, so the conjugated verb goes to the end of that clause.

## Two clauses, two verb positions

| Clause | Verb position | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| main clause | second | Ich **gehe** ins Kino. |
| wenn clause | last | …, wenn ich Zeit **habe**. |

You can put the two clauses in either order.

<Example de="Ich **gehe** ins Kino, wenn ich Zeit **habe**." en="I go to the cinema if I have time." />
<Example de="Wenn ich Zeit **habe**, **gehe** ich ins Kino." en="If I have time, I go to the cinema." />

<GrammarBox title="wenn clause first: verb, comma, verb">

When the sentence starts with the *wenn* clause, that whole clause counts as the first element. The main verb must still be second, so it comes straight after the comma, in front of the subject.

<Example de="Wenn es **regnet**, **bleibe** ich zu Hause." en="If it rains, I stay at home." />
<Example wrong de="Wenn es regnet, ich **bleibe** zu Hause." />

</GrammarBox>

You may add **dann** (then) to the main clause. It changes nothing about the word order:

<Example de="Wenn es regnet, **dann** bleibe ich zu Hause." en="If it rains, then I stay at home." />

## More than one verb

As with *weil*, the conjugated verb is last and everything else stands before it.

<Example de="Wenn du Fragen **hast**, kannst du mich anrufen." en="If you have questions, you can call me." />
<Example de="Wenn sie **anruft**, sage ich es ihr." en="If she calls, I will tell her." />
<Example de="Wenn du mitkommen **willst**, musst du dich beeilen." en="If you want to come along, you have to hurry." />

## Present tense for the future

German uses the present tense in both clauses even when English says *will*:

<Example de="Wenn du viel lernst, **bestehst** du die Prüfung." en="If you study a lot, you will pass the exam." />

## wenn, wann or als?

All three can be *when* in English.

| Word | Use | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **wenn** | if; whenever; when (present and future) | Wenn ich Zeit habe, komme ich. |
| **wann** | in questions about time | Wann kommst du? |
| **als** | when, for one occasion or period in the past | Als ich klein war, wohnten wir in Köln. |

**falls** means *in case* and is used when the condition is less likely:

<Example de="**Falls** es schneit, bleiben wir hier." en="In case it snows, we will stay here." />

<GrammarBox title="If I had …, I would …" label="A look ahead">

For conditions that are not real, German uses special verb forms: **hätte** (had), **wäre** (were) and **würde** + infinitive (would). The word order is the same as above.

<Example de="Wenn ich Geld **hätte**, **würde** ich viel reisen." en="If I had money, I would travel a lot." />
<Example de="Wenn ich reich **wäre**, **würde** ich ein Haus kaufen." en="If I were rich, I would buy a house." />

This is treated fully at level B1. For now it is enough to recognise it.

</GrammarBox>
