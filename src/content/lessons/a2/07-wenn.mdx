*If it rains, we stay at home. When I have time, I will call you.* Conditions and plans are everywhere in the A2 exam, above all in the speaking task where two people plan something together: *Wenn du am Samstag Zeit hast, können wir …*

## The idea

**wenn** is the third member of the family you met with *weil* and *dass*. It opens a subordinate clause, so the conjugated verb goes to the end.

It has two meanings that English keeps apart: **if** (a condition) and **whenever / when** (something that happens each time, or that will happen in the future). German does not distinguish them, and usually you do not need to.

A *wenn* sentence has two halves: the **condition** and the **result**. Very often the condition comes first, and that brings back the pattern from the *weil* chapter: **verb, comma, verb**.

*Wenn es **regnet**, **bleibe** ich zu Hause.*

## Step by step

<Step n="1" title="Two clauses, two verb positions">

| Clause | Verb position | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| main clause | second | Ich **gehe** ins Kino. |
| wenn clause | last | …, wenn ich Zeit **habe**. |

<Example de="Ich **gehe** ins Kino, wenn ich Zeit **habe**." en="I go to the cinema if I have time." />
<Example de="Ruf mich an, wenn du Hilfe **brauchst**." en="Call me if you need help." />

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="The condition first">

When the *wenn* clause opens the sentence, it fills position 1. The main verb must follow at once, before its subject.

<Example de="Wenn ich Zeit **habe**, **gehe** ich ins Kino." en="If I have time, I go to the cinema." />
<Example de="Wenn es **regnet**, **bleiben** wir zu Hause." en="If it rains, we stay at home." />
<Example de="Wenn du Hunger **hast**, **können** wir etwas essen." en="If you are hungry, we can eat something." />

You may add **dann** to the main clause. It takes position 1 there, and the verb still comes next:

<Example de="Wenn es regnet, **dann bleiben** wir zu Hause." en="If it rains, then we stay at home." />

</Step>

<Step n="3" title="With two verbs">

As with *weil* and *dass*: conjugated verb last, the other verb before it.

<Example de="Wenn du mitkommen **willst**, musst du dich beeilen." en="If you want to come along, you have to hurry." />
<Example de="Wenn sie **anruft**, sage ich es ihr." en="If she calls, I will tell her." />
<Example de="Wenn du die Hausaufgaben gemacht **hast**, darfst du fernsehen." en="When you have done your homework, you may watch TV." />

</Step>

<Step n="4" title="Present tense for the future">

German uses the **present tense** in both halves, even when English says *will*.

<Example de="Wenn du viel lernst, **bestehst** du die Prüfung." en="If you study a lot, you will pass the exam." />
<Example de="Wenn ich in Berlin bin, **besuche** ich dich." en="When I am in Berlin, I will visit you." />
<Example de="Wenn das Wetter morgen schön ist, **machen** wir einen Ausflug." en="If the weather is nice tomorrow, we will go on a trip." />

</Step>

<Step n="5" title="Whenever: habits">

With **immer** (always) the *wenn* clause describes something that happens every time.

<Example de="**Immer wenn** ich müde bin, trinke ich Kaffee." en="Whenever I am tired, I drink coffee." />
<Example de="Wenn ich Urlaub habe, fahre ich ans Meer." en="When I am on holiday, I go to the seaside." />
<Example de="Wenn ich nicht schlafen kann, lese ich." en="When I cannot sleep, I read." />

</Step>

<Step n="6" title="Planning together">

This is the language of the speaking exam, where you and a partner arrange something.

<Example de="Wenn du am Samstag Zeit hast, können wir ins Kino gehen." en="If you are free on Saturday, we can go to the cinema." />
<Example de="Wenn du willst, können wir auch essen gehen." en="If you like, we can also go out to eat." />
<Example de="Was machen wir, wenn es regnet?" en="What do we do if it rains?" />
<Example de="Wenn es regnet, gehen wir ins Museum." en="If it rains, we will go to the museum." />

</Step>

<Step n="7" title="wenn, wann or als">

All three can be *when* in English. German keeps them strictly apart.

| Word | Use | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **wenn** | if; whenever; when in the present or future | Wenn ich Zeit habe, komme ich. |
| **wann** | in questions about time, direct or indirect | Wann kommst du? Ich weiß nicht, wann er kommt. |
| **als** | when, for **one** event or period in the **past** | Als ich klein war, wohnten wir in Köln. |

<Example de="**Wann** beginnt der Film?" en="When does the film start?" />
<Example de="**Wenn** der Film beginnt, mache ich das Handy aus." en="When the film starts, I switch off my phone." />
<Example de="**Als** ich ein Kind war, hatte ich einen Hund." en="When I was a child, I had a dog." />

The test for the past: did it happen **once** (or over one period)? Then *als*. Did it happen **repeatedly**? Then *wenn*: *Immer wenn ich meine Oma besucht habe, hat sie Kuchen gebacken.*

**falls** means *in case* and is used when the condition is less likely:

<Example de="**Falls** es schneit, bleiben wir hier." en="In case it snows, we will stay here." />

</Step>

<Step n="8" title="A look ahead: unreal conditions">

For things that are not real, German uses special forms: **hätte** (had), **wäre** (were) and **würde** + infinitive (would). The word order stays exactly the same.

<Example de="Wenn ich Geld **hätte**, **würde** ich viel reisen." en="If I had money, I would travel a lot." />
<Example de="Wenn ich reich **wäre**, **würde** ich ein Haus kaufen." en="If I were rich, I would buy a house." />

Chapter 19 uses these forms for polite requests, and level B1 treats them in full. For now it is enough to recognise them.

</Step>

## Compared with English

<Compare en="**If** I have time … / **When** I have time …" de="**Wenn** ich Zeit habe …" note="English separates a condition from a point in time. German uses wenn for both." />

<Compare en="If it rains, **we stay** at home." de="Wenn es regnet, **bleiben wir** zu Hause." note="After an opening wenn clause the German main clause starts with its verb." />

<Compare en="If you study, you **will pass**." de="Wenn du lernst, **bestehst** du." note="No future tense is needed in German. The present does the job." />

<Compare en="**When** I was a child …" de="**Als** ich ein Kind war …" note="One period in the past takes als, not wenn. This is the most common mix-up." />

## Typical mistakes

<Mistake wrong="Wenn es regnet, **ich bleibe** zu Hause." right="Wenn es regnet, **bleibe ich** zu Hause." why="The wenn clause is position 1. The main verb follows straight after the comma." />

<Mistake wrong="Ich komme, wenn ich **habe** Zeit." right="Ich komme, wenn ich Zeit **habe**." why="In the wenn clause the conjugated verb stands at the end." />

<Mistake wrong="**Wenn** ich ein Kind war, wohnte ich in Polen." right="**Als** ich ein Kind war, wohnte ich in Polen." why="A single period in the past takes als." />

<Mistake wrong="Ich weiß nicht, **wenn** der Zug kommt." right="Ich weiß nicht, **wann** der Zug kommt." why="A question about time, even an indirect one, takes wann." />

<Mistake wrong="Wenn du lernst, **wirst** du die Prüfung **bestehen**." right="Wenn du lernst, **bestehst** du die Prüfung." why="The future form is not wrong, but German normally uses the plain present here." />

## Summary

<Summary>

**wenn + … + verb,** then **verb + subject …** *Wenn ich Zeit **habe**, **gehe** ich ins Kino.*

Or the other way round: *Ich **gehe** ins Kino, wenn ich Zeit **habe**.*

| Word | Meaning | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| wenn | if, whenever, when | present, future, repeated past |
| wann | when? | questions |
| als | when | one event or period in the past |
| falls | in case | an unlikely condition |

**Tense:** present in both halves, also for the future.

**Planning:** *Wenn du Zeit hast, können wir … · Was machen wir, wenn …?*

</Summary>
