The listening module of the Goethe-Zertifikat A2 takes about **30 minutes** and has **four parts**, twenty questions in all. You hear people on the phone, in announcements, in everyday conversations and on the radio. They speak at a normal pace, but clearly. The trick is to know **what kind of information** you are waiting for before the recording starts.

## What this part tests

| Part | You hear | Task | How often |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Teil 1** | five short texts: phone messages, announcements, the radio | multiple choice a, b or c | **twice** |
| **Teil 2** | one longer conversation between two people | match each person or day to an activity or a thing | **once** |
| **Teil 3** | five short dialogues | multiple choice a, b or c (in the exam with pictures) | **twice** |
| **Teil 4** | a radio interview | true or false (in the exam: *Ja* or *Nein*) | **twice** |

Teil 2 is the only part you hear **once**, so it needs the most concentration. Before every part you get time to read the questions. Use every second of it.

## How to approach it

<Step n="1" title="Predict the kind of answer">

Read the question and decide what you are listening for: a **time**, a **day**, a **price**, a **place**, a **person**, a **reason**. If the question is *Wann beginnt der Kurs?*, your ears switch to numbers and days, and you can ignore the rest.

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="The speaker often corrects himself">

Recordings like to mention two or three possibilities and then choose one. The first number you hear is often **not** the answer.

<Example de="Wir treffen uns um sieben … nein, warte, um halb acht, ich muss noch arbeiten." en="We will meet at seven … no, wait, at half past seven, I still have to work." />

Signals that a correction is coming: *nein, doch, lieber, aber, eigentlich, leider, ich meine*.

</Step>

<Step n="3" title="Numbers, times and dates">

These are the most common answers in Teil 1 and 3. Practise until you can hear them without thinking.

| You hear | It means |
| --- | --- |
| Viertel nach drei | 3:15 |
| halb vier | 3:30 |
| Viertel vor vier | 3:45 |
| vierzehn Uhr dreißig | 14:30 |
| am dritten Mai | on 3 May |
| fünfundvierzig Euro | 45 euros |

Remember that German says the units first: *fünfundvierzig* is *five-and-forty*.

</Step>

<Step n="4" title="Teil 2: make a grid">

In Teil 2 you hear one conversation once, and match five people or days to things from a list. The conversation follows the order of the questions. Write the first letters of the possible answers next to each name before you listen, and tick them as they come. If you miss one, leave it and keep up with the next. Guess it at the end.

<Example de="Am Montag gehe ich schwimmen, am Dienstag habe ich Gitarre, und am Mittwoch? Da muss ich leider zum Zahnarzt." en="On Monday I go swimming, on Tuesday I have guitar, and on Wednesday? Unfortunately I have to go to the dentist." />

</Step>

<Step n="5" title="Teil 4: the opinion and the facts">

The radio interview asks about what the person **does**, **did** or **thinks**. Statements often change one small thing: *always* becomes *sometimes*, *he* becomes *his wife*, *before* becomes *after*. Listen for the exact detail the statement is about.

<Example de="Früher bin ich jeden Tag mit dem Auto gefahren. Heute fahre ich nur noch mit dem Rad." en="I used to drive every day. Today I only cycle." />

The statement *Er fährt jeden Tag mit dem Auto* is therefore **false**.

</Step>

## Summary

<Summary>

| Part | Text | Heard | Listen for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | messages, announcements | twice | times, days, places, prices |
| 2 | one conversation | **once** | who / which day → what |
| 3 | short dialogues | twice | the final decision |
| 4 | radio interview | twice | the exact detail, then and now |

**Before each part:** read the questions and predict the kind of answer.

**Correction signals:** nein, doch, lieber, eigentlich, leider.

</Summary>
