**Warum?** (why?) is one of the questions you will hear most. The standard way to answer it is with **weil** (because).

<Example de="Warum gehst du nach Hause?" en="Why are you going home?" />
<Example de="Ich gehe nach Hause, **weil** ich müde **bin**." en="I am going home because I am tired." />

Look at where *bin* ended up. That is the whole lesson.

## The verb goes to the end

*weil* opens a subordinate clause, and in a subordinate clause the conjugated verb moves to the very end.

| Two sentences | Joined with weil |
| --- | --- |
| Ich gehe nach Hause. Ich **bin** müde. | Ich gehe nach Hause, weil ich müde **bin**. |
| Sie mag Pizza. Sie **ist** lecker. | Sie mag Pizza, weil sie lecker **ist**. |
| Er kommt nicht. Er **hat** keine Zeit. | Er kommt nicht, weil er keine Zeit **hat**. |

<GrammarBox title="weil + … + conjugated verb">

English keeps the verb next to the subject (*because I **am** tired*). Copying that into German is the most common mistake with *weil*:

<Example wrong de="Ich gehe nach Hause, weil ich **bin** müde." />
<Example de="Ich gehe nach Hause, weil ich müde **bin**." en="I am going home because I am tired." />

There is always a comma before *weil*.

</GrammarBox>

## When there are two verbs

The rule stays the same: the **conjugated** verb goes last. The other verb (infinitive or participle) stands just before it.

With a modal verb:

<Example de="Ich mache das, weil ich dir helfen **möchte**." en="I am doing this because I would like to help you." />
<Example de="Er lernt viel, weil er die Prüfung bestehen **will**." en="He studies a lot because he wants to pass the exam." />

In the perfect tense:

<Example de="Ich rufe ihn nicht an, weil ich seine Nummer vergessen **habe**." en="I am not calling him because I have forgotten his number." />

A separable verb is written as one word again at the end:

<Example de="Wir müssen los, weil der Zug um acht Uhr **abfährt**." en="We have to go because the train leaves at eight." />

## Starting with the reason

You can put the *weil* clause first. The main clause then begins with its verb, directly after the comma.

<Example de="Weil ich krank **bin**, **gehe** ich zum Arzt." en="Because I am ill, I am going to the doctor." />
<Example de="Weil es **regnet**, **bleiben** wir zu Hause." en="Because it is raining, we are staying at home." />

Two verbs meet at the comma: *bin, gehe*. That is correct, not a mistake.

## Short answers

In conversation the *weil* clause often stands alone:

<Example de="Warum kommst du nicht mit? Weil ich keine Lust habe." en="Why aren't you coming along? Because I don't feel like it." />

## weil and denn

**denn** also means *because*, but it does not change the word order. It is a little more formal and cannot start the sentence.

<Example de="Ich bleibe zu Hause, **weil** ich krank **bin**." en="I am staying at home because I am ill." />
<Example de="Ich bleibe zu Hause, **denn** ich **bin** krank." en="I am staying at home, for I am ill." />
