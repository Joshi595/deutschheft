German adjectives have three steps, just like English *fast, faster, fastest*.

| Basic form | Comparative | Superlative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| schnell | schnell**er** | am schnell**sten** |
| klein | klein**er** | am klein**sten** |
| interessant | interessant**er** | am interessant**esten** |

German never uses *mehr* for this. However long the adjective, it takes **-er**:

<Example de="Das Buch ist **interessanter** als der Film." en="The book is more interesting than the film." />
<Example wrong de="Das Buch ist **mehr interessant** als der Film." />

## Comparing two things

**Unequal: comparative + als** (than)

<Example de="Mein Auto ist schneller **als** dein Auto." en="My car is faster than your car." />
<Example de="Sie ist älter **als** ich." en="She is older than me." />

**Equal: so + basic form + wie** (as … as)

<Example de="Ich bin **so** groß **wie** mein Vater." en="I am as tall as my father." />
<Example de="Der Film ist **nicht so** gut **wie** das Buch." en="The film is not as good as the book." />

<GrammarBox title="als after a comparative, wie after so" label="Watch out">

English speakers tend to mix these up, and so do many Germans in casual speech. In standard German:

<Example de="größer **als**" en="bigger than" />
<Example de="so groß **wie**" en="as big as" />
<Example wrong de="größer **wie**" />

</GrammarBox>

## Umlaut

Most short adjectives with **a, o** or **u** take an umlaut in the comparative and superlative.

| Basic form | Comparative | Superlative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| alt | **ä**lter | am **ä**ltesten |
| jung | j**ü**nger | am j**ü**ngsten |
| groß | gr**ö**ßer | am gr**ö**ßten |
| lang | l**ä**nger | am l**ä**ngsten |
| kurz | k**ü**rzer | am k**ü**rzesten |
| warm | w**ä**rmer | am w**ä**rmsten |
| kalt | k**ä**lter | am k**ä**ltesten |
| stark | st**ä**rker | am st**ä**rksten |

## Irregular forms

These few are very common and have to be learned by heart.

| Basic form | Comparative | Superlative | |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| gut | **besser** | am **besten** | good, better, best |
| viel | **mehr** | am **meisten** | much, more, most |
| gern | **lieber** | am **liebsten** | gladly, rather, best of all |
| hoch | **höher** | am **höchsten** | high, higher, highest |
| nah | **näher** | am **nächsten** | near, nearer, nearest |

*gern, lieber, am liebsten* is how you say what you like, prefer and like best:

<Example de="Ich trinke **gern** Kaffee, aber ich trinke **lieber** Tee." en="I like drinking coffee, but I prefer tea." />
<Example de="**Am liebsten** trinke ich heiße Schokolade." en="Best of all I like hot chocolate." />

## Two small spelling rules

- Adjectives ending in **-er** or **-el** drop the *e* in the comparative: *teuer* → **teurer**, *dunkel* → **dunkler**.
- After **-d, -t, -s, -ß, -z** the superlative adds an extra *e* so it can be pronounced: am ält**e**sten, am kürz**e**sten, am heiß**e**sten. The exception is *groß*: **am größten**.

## Two forms of the superlative

<GrammarBox title="am …sten or der / die / das …ste">

**After a verb**, with no noun following, use **am …sten**. It never changes.

<Example de="Dieser Zug fährt **am schnellsten**." en="This train goes fastest." />
<Example de="Im Januar ist es **am kältesten**." en="It is coldest in January." />

**In front of a noun**, use **der / die / das …ste**, with the normal adjective endings from lessons 5, 6 and 8.

<Example de="Das ist **der schnellste** Zug." en="That is the fastest train." />
<Example de="Sie ist **die jüngste** Schülerin in der Klasse." en="She is the youngest pupil in the class." />
<Example de="Ich nehme **den billigsten** Flug." en="I am taking the cheapest flight." />

</GrammarBox>

Comparatives in front of a noun take those same endings, added after the *-er*:

<Example de="Ich brauche ein **schnelleres** Auto." en="I need a faster car." />
<Example de="Mein **älterer** Bruder wohnt in Hamburg." en="My older brother lives in Hamburg." />
