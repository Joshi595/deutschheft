The writing module of the Goethe-Zertifikat A2 lasts **30 minutes** and has **two tasks**. Both are short. Neither is a test of beautiful style. The examiners check whether you did **what the task asked**, whether a reader would **understand** you, and whether your German is **good enough for A2**. Every content point you miss costs more than any grammar mistake, so the method in this chapter starts with the points.

## What this part tests

| | Teil 1 | Teil 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Text | a short message (SMS, chat) | an email |
| Reader | a friend or family member: **du** | a teacher, colleague, neighbour, landlord: usually **Sie** |
| Length | 20–30 words | 30–40 words |
| Content | three points to cover | three points to cover |

For each task the examiners look at:

- **Content.** Is every point there, and is it clear? A missing point loses a large share of the marks.
- **Communication.** Does the text have the right form: greeting, closing, *du* or *Sie*, a tone that fits?
- **Language.** Is the vocabulary and grammar varied enough and correct enough for A2? Connecting sentences with *weil, dass, aber, deshalb* shows that you are at A2, not A1.

## How to approach it

<Step n="1" title="Number the points">

Read the task and number the three points. Write one or two sentences for each, **in the same order**. Do not add a fourth topic; there are no marks for it, only new chances to make mistakes.

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="Teil 1: the text message">

A message to a friend can be informal and short. You still need a greeting and your name.

*Task: You are on the train and will be late for dinner with your friend Leon. Write a message: apologise, say why, suggest a new time.*

<Example de="Hallo Leon, es tut mir leid, ich komme zu spät. Mein Zug hat eine Stunde Verspätung. Können wir uns um halb neun treffen statt um halb acht? Bis später! Sara" en="Hi Leon, I am sorry, I will be late. My train is an hour late. Can we meet at half past eight instead of half past seven? See you later! Sara" />

That is 33 words with all three points: sorry, reason, new time.

</Step>

<Step n="3" title="Teil 2: the email with Sie">

The second email usually goes to someone you know a little, but formally: your course teacher, your boss, the landlord. Use *Sie* and *Ihnen*, with a capital letter.

*Task: You cannot come to your German course next week. Write to your teacher, Frau Roth: say why, ask about the homework, ask whether you can come to another course.*

<Example de="Liebe Frau Roth, leider kann ich nächste Woche nicht zum Kurs kommen, weil ich eine Dienstreise nach Hamburg habe. Könnten Sie mir bitte die Hausaufgaben schicken? Kann ich vielleicht am Donnerstag in den Abendkurs kommen? Viele Grüße, Mehmet Arslan" en="Dear Ms Roth, unfortunately I cannot come to the course next week because I have a business trip to Hamburg. Could you please send me the homework? Could I perhaps come to the evening course on Thursday? Best regards, Mehmet Arslan" />

That is 40 words, three points, a *weil* clause and a polite *könnten*.

</Step>

<Step n="4" title="Openings and closings">

| Reader | Opening | Closing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a friend | Hallo Leon, / Liebe Sara, / Lieber Tom, | Bis bald! / Viele Grüße / Liebe Grüße |
| someone you call Sie, by name | Liebe Frau Roth, / Lieber Herr Wolf, | Viele Grüße / Freundliche Grüße |
| an office, a stranger | Sehr geehrte Damen und Herren, | Mit freundlichen Grüßen |

After the opening comma, the next word starts with a small letter (unless it is a noun or *Sie*).

</Step>

<Step n="5" title="Sentences for the typical points">

| Purpose | Sentence |
| --- | --- |
| apologise | Es tut mir leid, dass … / Entschuldigung, … |
| give a reason | …, weil … / …, denn … |
| suggest | Können wir uns am … treffen? / Wie wäre es mit …? |
| ask for something | Könnten Sie mir bitte … ? |
| thank | Vielen Dank für … |
| accept / decline | Ich komme gern. / Leider kann ich nicht kommen. |
| invite | Hast du Lust, … zu …? / Ich möchte Sie zu … einladen. |

</Step>

<Step n="6" title="Check before you finish">

1. Are **all three points** there, in order?
2. **du** or **Sie**, and is it the same from start to end?
3. Greeting at the top, closing and **name** at the bottom?
4. The **verb**: second in main clauses, last after *weil* and *dass*?
5. About the right **length**? Too short risks missing content; much too long risks mistakes.

</Step>

## Summary

<Summary>

| | Teil 1 | Teil 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reader | friend (du) | teacher, boss, neighbour (Sie) |
| Length | 20–30 words | 30–40 words |
| Points | 3 | 3 |

**Method:** number the points → one or two sentences each → greeting, closing, name → check.

**Show A2:** one *weil* or *dass*, one *könnten* or *würde*, one Perfekt.

**Time:** about 10 minutes for Teil 1, 15 for Teil 2, 5 to check.

</Summary>
