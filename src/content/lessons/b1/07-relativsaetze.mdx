*The man **who** lives next door. The book **that** I am reading. The friend **to whom** I gave the key.* Relative clauses let you describe something with a whole sentence instead of a single adjective. They are everywhere in written German – in the B1 reading texts nearly every paragraph has one – and they make your own writing sound much more advanced. The system is logical: two questions decide the relative pronoun, and the verb goes to the end as in every subordinate clause.

## The idea

A relative clause gives information about a noun. It starts with a **relative pronoun** and ends with the **conjugated verb**.

*Der Mann, **der** neben mir **wohnt**, ist Arzt.* The man who lives next to me is a doctor.

Two questions decide the relative pronoun:

1. **Which noun does it refer to?** That noun gives the **gender and number**: *der Mann* → masculine singular.
2. **What job does the pronoun do in its own clause?** That gives the **case**: in *der neben mir wohnt*, the man is the one who lives → subject → **nominative**.

The relative pronouns look almost exactly like the definite article. Only the dative plural and the genitive are different.

| | m | f | n | pl |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nom. | der | die | das | die |
| Akk. | den | die | das | die |
| Dat. | dem | der | dem | **denen** |
| Gen. | **dessen** | **deren** | **dessen** | **deren** |

## Step by step

<Step n="1" title="Nominative: the noun does the action">

<Example de="Der Mann, **der** dort **steht**, ist mein Chef." en="The man who is standing there is my boss." />
<Example de="Ich habe eine Kollegin, **die** fünf Sprachen **spricht**." en="I have a colleague who speaks five languages." />
<Example de="Das ist das Kind, **das** so gut **singt**." en="That is the child who sings so well." />

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="Accusative: the noun is the object">

Only the masculine changes: **den**.

<Example de="Der Film, **den** ich gestern **gesehen habe**, war super." en="The film that I saw yesterday was great." />
<Example de="Die Frau, **die** du **kennst**, ist meine Tante." en="The woman you know is my aunt." />
<Example de="Das Buch, **das** sie mir **geschenkt hat**, ist spannend." en="The book she gave me is exciting." />

Ask: inside the relative clause, is the noun the one doing it (nominative) or the one it is done to (accusative)? *ich habe **den Film** gesehen* → *den*.

</Step>

<Step n="3" title="Dative: after a dative verb or a dative idea">

<Example de="Der Freund, **dem** ich **geholfen habe**, hat mich zum Essen eingeladen." en="The friend I helped has invited me for a meal." />
<Example de="Die Kollegin, **der** das Auto **gehört**, ist im Urlaub." en="The colleague who owns the car is on holiday." />
<Example de="Das sind die Nachbarn, **denen** ich die Schlüssel **gegeben habe**." en="Those are the neighbours I gave the keys to." />

Watch the plural: **denen**, not *den*.

</Step>

<Step n="4" title="dessen and deren: whose">

*dessen* (masculine and neuter) and *deren* (feminine and plural) mean **whose**. The noun after them takes its own article ending, as if there were no article.

<Example de="Das ist der Mann, **dessen** Auto vor meiner Tür steht." en="That is the man whose car is parked in front of my door." />
<Example de="Die Frau, **deren** Sohn in meiner Klasse ist, ist Ärztin." en="The woman whose son is in my class is a doctor." />
<Example de="Die Familie, **deren** Haus wir gekauft haben, wohnt jetzt in Spanien." en="The family whose house we bought now lives in Spain." />

</Step>

<Step n="5" title="Position and commas">

A relative clause comes **directly after** its noun, and has a **comma** on both sides. If the noun is in the middle of the main clause, the main clause continues after the relative clause.

<Example de="Die Wohnung, **die wir gestern besichtigt haben**, ist zu teuer." en="The flat we viewed yesterday is too expensive." />

If the main clause would only have one word left after it (usually the second verb part), the relative clause can move to the end: *Ich habe den Mann **gesehen**, der hier wohnt.* That is easier to read than *Ich habe den Mann, der hier wohnt, gesehen.*

</Step>

<Step n="6" title="Building a relative clause step by step">

Start with two sentences that share a noun.

1. *Ich habe einen Freund. Der Freund wohnt in Rom.*
2. Find the shared noun: *der Freund*.
3. Gender and number from the first sentence: masculine singular.
4. Case from the second sentence: *der Freund wohnt* → subject → nominative → **der**.
5. Verb to the end: *Ich habe einen Freund, **der** in Rom **wohnt**.*

<Example de="Ich kaufe das Kleid. Ich habe **das Kleid** im Schaufenster gesehen. → Ich kaufe das Kleid, **das** ich im Schaufenster gesehen habe." en="I am buying the dress that I saw in the shop window." />

</Step>

## Compared with English

<Compare en="the man **who** / the book **that** / the dog **which**" de="der Mann, **der** / das Buch, **das** / der Hund, **der**" note="English chooses by person or thing. German chooses by gender and case." />

<Compare en="The film (that) I saw" de="Der Film, **den** ich gesehen habe" note="English can leave out the relative pronoun. German never can." />

<Compare en="the friend I gave the key **to**" de="der Freund, **dem** ich den Schlüssel gegeben habe" note="English can end with a preposition. German puts the case into the pronoun itself (more on prepositions in the next chapter)." />

<Compare en="The man, **who** lives here, …" de="Der Mann, **der** hier wohnt, …" note="German always puts commas around a relative clause, whether it is essential or not." />

## Typical mistakes

<Mistake wrong="Der Film, **der** ich gesehen habe, war gut." right="Der Film, **den** ich gesehen habe, war gut." why="In the relative clause the film is the object: accusative, den." />

<Mistake wrong="Die Frau, **die** ich geholfen habe, …" right="Die Frau, **der** ich geholfen habe, …" why="helfen takes the dative: der." />

<Mistake wrong="Die Leute, **den** ich geholfen habe, …" right="Die Leute, **denen** ich geholfen habe, …" why="Dative plural relative pronoun is denen." />

<Mistake wrong="Der Mann, der **wohnt** neben mir, …" right="Der Mann, der neben mir **wohnt**, …" why="A relative clause is a subordinate clause: verb at the end." />

<Mistake wrong="Das ist der Mann, **wessen** Auto hier steht." right="Das ist der Mann, **dessen** Auto hier steht." why="'whose' in a relative clause is dessen or deren." />
