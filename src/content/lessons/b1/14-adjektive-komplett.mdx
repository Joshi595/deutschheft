You learned adjective endings at A2 one case at a time. At B1 you need them in every case, in longer noun groups and in written texts, where examiners look closely. This chapter puts the whole system on one page, built on the single idea you already know. Then it shows two things that make written German compact: **adjectives used as nouns** (*die Deutschen, etwas Gutes*) and **participles used as adjectives** (*die steigenden Preise, das reparierte Auto*).

## The idea

The rule behind every ending: **the gender and case must be shown clearly once**, either by the article or by the adjective.

There are three tables, depending on what comes before the adjective:

1. **After der, dieser, jeder, welcher** (the article shows everything): the adjective takes **-e** or **-en**. Only five places have -e: nominative singular (all three genders) and accusative feminine and neuter. **Everything else is -en.**
2. **After ein, kein, mein …** (the article shows almost everything): the same as table 1, **except** in the three places where *ein* has no ending (nom. m., nom. n., acc. n.). There the adjective shows the gender: **-er, -es, -es**.
3. **No article**: the adjective takes the ending of *der, die, das* itself, except in the masculine and neuter genitive (-en).

## Step by step

<Step n="1" title="Table 1: after der">

| | m | f | n | pl |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nom. | der alt**e** Mann | die alt**e** Frau | das alt**e** Haus | die alt**en** Leute |
| Akk. | den alt**en** Mann | die alt**e** Frau | das alt**e** Haus | die alt**en** Leute |
| Dat. | dem alt**en** Mann | der alt**en** Frau | dem alt**en** Haus | den alt**en** Leuten |
| Gen. | des alt**en** Mannes | der alt**en** Frau | des alt**en** Hauses | der alt**en** Leute |

<Example de="Wir haben **den alten** Schrank verkauft." en="We sold the old wardrobe." />
<Example de="Die Mieter **des neuen** Hauses sind sehr zufrieden." en="The tenants of the new building are very satisfied." />

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="Table 2: after ein / mein / kein">

| | m | f | n | pl |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nom. | ein alt**er** Mann | eine alt**e** Frau | ein alt**es** Haus | meine alt**en** Freunde |
| Akk. | einen alt**en** Mann | eine alt**e** Frau | ein alt**es** Haus | meine alt**en** Freunde |
| Dat. | einem alt**en** Mann | einer alt**en** Frau | einem alt**en** Haus | meinen alt**en** Freunden |
| Gen. | eines alt**en** Mannes | einer alt**en** Frau | eines alt**en** Hauses | meiner alt**en** Freunde |

<Example de="Ich wohne in **einem alten** Haus mit **einem großen** Garten." en="I live in an old house with a big garden." />
<Example de="Das ist **ein wichtiger** Punkt." en="That is an important point." />

</Step>

<Step n="3" title="Table 3: no article">

| | m | f | n | pl |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nom. | gut**er** Wein | frisch**e** Milch | kalt**es** Wasser | alt**e** Leute |
| Akk. | gut**en** Wein | frisch**e** Milch | kalt**es** Wasser | alt**e** Leute |
| Dat. | gut**em** Wein | frisch**er** Milch | kalt**em** Wasser | alt**en** Leuten |
| Gen. | gut**en** Weins | frisch**er** Milch | kalt**en** Wassers | alt**er** Leute |

<Example de="**Viele junge** Leute ziehen in die Stadt." en="Many young people move to the city." />
<Example de="Wir suchen Mitarbeiter mit **guten** Deutschkenntnissen." en="We are looking for staff with good German." />

After **viele, einige, wenige, mehrere** and numbers, the adjective follows table 3. After **alle** and **keine**, it follows table 1: *alle jungen Leute*.

</Step>

<Step n="4" title="Adjectives as nouns">

An adjective can become a noun: it gets a capital letter but **keeps its adjective ending**.

| | definite | indefinite |
| --- | --- | --- |
| man | der Deutsch**e** | ein Deutsch**er** |
| woman | die Deutsch**e** | eine Deutsch**e** |
| people | die Deutsch**en** | Deutsch**e** |

<Example de="Mein Nachbar ist **ein Deutscher**, seine Frau ist **eine Österreicherin**." en="My neighbour is a German, his wife is Austrian." />
<Example de="Ich habe mit **dem Angestellten** gesprochen." en="I spoke to the employee." />

Common ones: *der/die Bekannte* (acquaintance), *Verwandte* (relative), *Erwachsene* (adult), *Jugendliche* (young person), *Angestellte* (employee), *Kranke* (sick person), *Arbeitslose* (unemployed person).

</Step>

<Step n="5" title="etwas Neues, nichts Besonderes, das Gute">

After **etwas, nichts, viel, wenig**, an adjective becomes a neuter noun with **-es**. With **das**, it takes **-e**.

<Example de="Gibt es **etwas Neues**?" en="Is there anything new?" />
<Example de="Heute ist **nichts Besonderes** passiert." en="Nothing special happened today." />
<Example de="**Das Beste** am Urlaub war das Essen." en="The best thing about the holiday was the food." />

</Step>

<Step n="6" title="Participles as adjectives">

Both participles can stand before a noun and take normal adjective endings.

| Participle | Formation | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| present | infinitive + **d** | doing, active, ongoing | das lach**end**e Kind (the laughing child) |
| past | Partizip II | done, finished, passive | das gekocht**e** Ei (the boiled egg) |

<Example de="Wegen der **steigenden** Preise kaufen viele Leute weniger." en="Because of the rising prices many people buy less." />
<Example de="Das **reparierte** Auto steht vor der Tür." en="The repaired car is in front of the door." />
<Example de="Bitte schicken Sie uns das **ausgefüllte** Formular zurück." en="Please send us back the completed form." />

These are typical of written German, especially official texts.

</Step>

## Compared with English

<Compare en="**the** old man / **an** old man / old men" de="**der** alte Mann / **ein** alter Mann / alte Männer" note="The adjective ending changes with the article. English adjectives never change." />

<Compare en="a German (man) / the Germans" de="ein Deutscher / die Deutschen" note="German nationality nouns are often adjectives, and change their endings like adjectives." />

<Compare en="**something new**" de="**etwas Neues**" note="German makes the adjective a noun with a capital letter and -es." />

<Compare en="the **rising** prices / the **filled-in** form" de="die **steigenden** Preise / das **ausgefüllte** Formular" note="English uses -ing and -ed forms the same way, but German adds adjective endings." />

## Typical mistakes

<Mistake wrong="mit einem **großer** Garten" right="mit einem **großen** Garten" why="After einem the adjective is -en." />

<Mistake wrong="**Ein** Deutsche hat mir geholfen. (a man)" right="**Ein Deutscher** hat mir geholfen." why="After ein, the nominalised adjective takes -er for a man." />

<Mistake wrong="Gibt es etwas **neues**?" right="Gibt es etwas **Neues**?" why="After etwas the adjective is a noun: capital letter and -es." />

<Mistake wrong="die **steigende** Preise" right="die **steigenden** Preise" why="Participles take adjective endings: after die in the plural, -en." />

<Mistake wrong="viele **jungen** Leute" right="viele **junge** Leute" why="After viele the adjective follows the no-article table: -e." />

## Summary

<Summary>

**After der:** -e in nom. sing. and acc. f/n; -en everywhere else.
**After ein:** like der, but **-er** (nom. m.), **-es** (nom./acc. n.).
**No article / after viele, einige, numbers:** endings of der, die, das (gen. m/n: -en).

**Adjectives as nouns:** der Deutsche / ein Deutscher · die Angestellte · die Erwachsenen
**After etwas, nichts, viel, wenig:** etwas Neues, nichts Besonderes · **das Beste**

**Participles:** present (-end) = ongoing · past (Partizip II) = done · both with adjective endings.

</Summary>
