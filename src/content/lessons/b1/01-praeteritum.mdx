At A1 and A2 you talked about the past with the **Perfekt**: *Ich habe gegessen, ich bin gefahren.* You also used a few Präteritum forms without thinking much about them: *war, hatte, konnte, musste*. At B1 you meet the past in newspapers, books, stories and reports, and those are written almost entirely in the **Präteritum**. In the B1 reading exam you will see it in every text. This chapter gives you the whole system: two endings, one list of vowel changes, and a clear rule for when each tense is used.

## The idea

German has two everyday past tenses with almost the same meaning. The difference is mainly one of **style**:

| | Perfekt | Präteritum |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where | speech, emails, chat, letters to friends | written narrative: books, newspapers, reports, CVs |
| Example | Gestern **bin** ich nach Hause **gegangen**. | Gestern **ging** ich nach Hause. |
| Feeling | conversational, close | narrative, distant, more formal |

A few very common verbs are used in the Präteritum **even in speech**, because their Perfekt sounds clumsy: **sein** (war), **haben** (hatte), the **modal verbs** (konnte, musste, wollte …), **es gibt** (es gab) and **wissen** (wusste). You already do this.

In the north of Germany people use the Präteritum in speech a little more than in the south, but the rule above holds everywhere.

## Step by step

<Step n="1" title="Regular verbs: -te">

Take the stem and add **-te** plus the person ending. The *ich* and *er* forms are the same.

| | machen | arbeiten |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ich | mach**te** | arbeit**ete** |
| du | mach**test** | arbeit**etest** |
| er/sie/es | mach**te** | arbeit**ete** |
| wir | mach**ten** | arbeit**eten** |
| ihr | mach**tet** | arbeit**etet** |
| sie/Sie | mach**ten** | arbeit**eten** |

Verbs whose stem ends in **-t** or **-d** (and some with *-m, -n* after a consonant) add an extra **-e-**: *arbeitete, wartete, redete, öffnete*.

<Example de="Früher **wohnte** sie in Hamburg und **arbeitete** in einem Hafenbüro." en="She used to live in Hamburg and worked in a port office." />
<Example de="Wir **warteten** zwei Stunden auf den Zug." en="We waited two hours for the train." />

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="Irregular verbs: a new vowel, no -te">

Irregular verbs change their stem vowel and take **no -te**. The *ich* and *er* forms have **no ending at all**.

| | gehen | kommen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ich | **ging** | **kam** |
| du | ging**st** | kam**st** |
| er/sie/es | **ging** | **kam** |
| wir | ging**en** | kam**en** |
| ihr | ging**t** | kam**t** |
| sie/Sie | ging**en** | kam**en** |

The vowel cannot be predicted from the infinitive, so the forms must be learned. Here are the ones you meet most often:

| Infinitive | Präteritum | Infinitive | Präteritum |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| sein | war | fahren | fuhr |
| haben | hatte | laufen | lief |
| werden | wurde | rufen | rief |
| gehen | ging | schlafen | schlief |
| kommen | kam | stehen | stand |
| sehen | sah | sitzen | saß |
| geben | gab | liegen | lag |
| nehmen | nahm | essen | aß |
| finden | fand | trinken | trank |
| sprechen | sprach | beginnen | begann |
| schreiben | schrieb | helfen | half |
| bleiben | blieb | treffen | traf |
| lesen | las | verlieren | verlor |
| tun | tat | ziehen | zog |

<Example de="Er **kam** um acht, **aß** schnell und **ging** wieder." en="He came at eight, ate quickly and left again." />
<Example de="Plötzlich **stand** ein Mann vor der Tür." en="Suddenly a man was standing at the door." />

Many of these vowel groups repeat: *schreiben – schrieb, bleiben – blieb*; *finden – fand, trinken – trank, beginnen – begann*. Learning them in groups is faster than one by one.

</Step>

<Step n="3" title="Mixed verbs: -te and a new vowel">

A small group takes **both** a vowel change and the regular **-te**.

| Infinitive | Präteritum | Perfekt |
| --- | --- | --- |
| denken | da**chte** | hat gedacht |
| bringen | bra**chte** | hat gebracht |
| kennen | kann**te** | hat gekannt |
| wissen | wuss**te** | hat gewusst |
| nennen | nann**te** | hat genannt |
| rennen | rann**te** | ist gerannt |

<Example de="Ich **dachte**, du **wusstest** das schon." en="I thought you already knew that." />
<Example de="Niemand **kannte** den Mann." en="Nobody knew the man." />

</Step>

<Step n="4" title="Separable verbs">

The prefix goes to the end, just as in the present.

<Example de="Der Zug **kam** pünktlich in Berlin **an**." en="The train arrived in Berlin on time." />
<Example de="Sie **stand** um sechs **auf** und **rief** sofort ihre Schwester **an**." en="She got up at six and called her sister straight away." />

In a subordinate clause the verb joins together again at the end: *…, als der Zug **ankam**.*

</Step>

<Step n="5" title="Which tense? A practical rule">

| Situation | Use |
| --- | --- |
| You tell a friend about your weekend | Perfekt, plus war, hatte, konnte … |
| You write a story, a report, a CV, a newspaper text | Präteritum |
| You talk about a state or ability in the past | war, hatte, konnte, musste (in any style) |

<Example de="Am Wochenende **war** ich in Köln. Ich **habe** meine Tante **besucht**, und wir **sind** ins Museum **gegangen**." en="At the weekend I was in Cologne. I visited my aunt and we went to the museum. (spoken)" />
<Example de="Im Jahr 1989 **fiel** die Berliner Mauer. Tausende Menschen **feierten** auf den Straßen." en="In 1989 the Berlin Wall fell. Thousands of people celebrated in the streets. (written)" />

Mixing the two in one text is normal: Präteritum for *sein, haben* and modals, Perfekt for the other verbs.

</Step>

<Step n="6" title="Reading a story">

Fairy tales, novels and news reports use the Präteritum throughout. Learn to recognise the forms even if you would not use them yourself.

<Example de="Es **war** einmal ein König, der **hatte** drei Töchter. Die jüngste **war** so schön, dass die Sonne selbst sich **wunderte**." en="Once upon a time there was a king who had three daughters. The youngest was so beautiful that the sun itself was amazed." />

<Example de="Die Polizei **fand** das Auto am nächsten Morgen. Der Fahrer **hielt** sich in einem Hotel **auf**." en="The police found the car the next morning. The driver was staying in a hotel." />

When you meet an unfamiliar form, look for the infinitive: remove the ending, guess the vowel. *fing an* → *anfangen*; *verließ* → *verlassen*; *hielt* → *halten*.

</Step>

## Compared with English

<Compare en="I **went**. / I **have gone**." de="Ich **ging**. / Ich **bin gegangen**." note="In English these two tenses mean different things. In German they mean the same; the choice is about style, written or spoken." />

<Compare en="I **was** tired, so I **went** to bed." de="Ich **war** müde, deshalb **bin** ich ins Bett **gegangen**." note="Mixing is normal in German speech: war in the Präteritum, the action in the Perfekt." />

<Compare en="sing – sang – sung, drink – drank – drunk" de="singen – sang – gesungen, trinken – trank – getrunken" note="English and German share many of the same irregular patterns. Use them to guess." />

<Compare en="I **was working** when he **called**." de="Ich **arbeitete**, als er **anrief**." note="German has no -ing form. The Präteritum covers both 'worked' and 'was working'." />

## Typical mistakes

<Mistake wrong="Er **gingte** nach Hause." right="Er **ging** nach Hause." why="Irregular verbs take no -te." />

<Mistake wrong="Ich **kamte** zu spät." right="Ich **kam** zu spät." why="kommen is irregular: kam, with no ending in the ich form." />

<Mistake wrong="Er **dankte** an seine Mutter. (meaning: he thought of)" right="Er **dachte** an seine Mutter." why="The Präteritum of denken is dachte. dankte comes from danken, to thank." />

<Mistake wrong="Wir **arbeitten** bis acht." right="Wir **arbeiteten** bis acht." why="After -t, add -e- before the past ending." />

<Mistake wrong="Gestern **aß** ich Pizza und **sah** einen Film. (in a chat with a friend)" right="Gestern **habe** ich Pizza **gegessen** und einen Film **gesehen**." why="Not wrong, but in speech and chats the Perfekt is much more natural." />

## Summary

<Summary>

| Type | ich / er | wir / sie | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| regular | stem + **te** | stem + **ten** | machte, arbeitete |
| irregular | new vowel, **no ending** | new vowel + **en** | ging, kam, sah |
| mixed | new vowel + **te** | new vowel + **ten** | dachte, brachte, wusste |

**Use the Präteritum** in written narrative, and for **war, hatte, konnte, musste, wollte, es gab, wusste** in any style.

**Use the Perfekt** for other verbs in speech, chats and personal emails.

</Summary>
