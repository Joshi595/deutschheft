The reading module of the Goethe-Zertifikat B1 lasts **65 minutes** and has **five parts** with **30 questions**. It is a separate module: you can pass or repeat it on its own. The texts are longer than at A2 and more varied, but the questions are still about finding specific information. You do not need every word. You need to know what each part asks for and where to look.

## What this part tests

| Part | Text | Task | Items | Suggested time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Teil 1** | a blog or personal email about an experience | richtig / falsch | 6 | 10 min |
| **Teil 2** | two newspaper articles | multiple choice a, b, c | 2 × 3 | 20 min |
| **Teil 3** | ten short adverts and seven people looking for something | which advert fits? (one person: 0 = none) | 7 | 10 min |
| **Teil 4** | seven readers' comments on a topic | is the person **for** the idea? ja / nein | 7 | 15 min |
| **Teil 5** | rules, instructions or terms (e.g. house rules, a course) | multiple choice a, b, c | 4 | 10 min |

That leaves a few minutes at the end to transfer your answers to the answer sheet, which you must do within the 65 minutes.

## How to approach it

<Step n="1" title="Teil 1: the blog">

The statements follow the order of the text. Read one statement, find the place, decide, move on. Watch for **time** (*früher – heute*), **people** (*sie – ihre Schwester*) and **negations** (*nicht mehr, kein, noch nie*).

<Example de="Text: Früher bin ich jeden Tag mit dem Auto gefahren, aber seit ich in Leipzig wohne, nehme ich das Rad." />
<Example de="Aussage: Sie fährt jeden Tag mit dem Auto. → falsch" />

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="Teil 2: the articles">

Read the questions first, then the article. The three questions usually follow the text in order: the first is near the beginning, the last near the end. The wrong options use words from the text with a different meaning, so check the **whole** sentence, not single words.

</Step>

<Step n="3" title="Teil 3: the adverts">

Each person has **two or three conditions** (what, when, where, how much). Underline them. An advert is only right if **all** conditions fit. Exactly one person has **no** matching advert: the answer is **0**. Each advert can only be used once.

</Step>

<Step n="4" title="Teil 4: for or against?">

The question is always the same: *Ist die Person für …?* Read each comment and decide on the **overall** position. Many comments start with one side and then turn: the part after **aber**, **trotzdem**, **allerdings** or **doch** usually shows the real opinion.

<Example de="Zwar ist ein Tempolimit gut für die Umwelt, aber ich finde, jeder sollte selbst entscheiden. → nein" />

</Step>

<Step n="5" title="Teil 5: rules and instructions">

These texts are formal: passive, modal verbs and long nouns (see chapter 28). Translate the important sentence into simple German in your head. Questions ask what is **allowed**, **required** or **possible**: *darf, muss, kann, ist (nicht) erlaubt*.

</Step>

<Step n="6" title="Time management">

Do the parts in order, but keep to the time plan. If a part takes too long, mark your best guess and go on. There are no minus points, so never leave a blank.

</Step>

## Summary

<Summary>

| Part | Task | Key strategy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | blog: richtig / falsch | order of the text; time, person, negation |
| 2 | articles: a, b, c | questions first; check the whole sentence |
| 3 | adverts: match, one is 0 | all conditions must fit; each advert once |
| 4 | comments: for or against | the part after aber / trotzdem counts |
| 5 | rules: a, b, c | darf, muss, kann; simplify formal sentences |

**Time:** 10 · 20 · 10 · 15 · 10 minutes. Never leave a blank.

</Summary>
