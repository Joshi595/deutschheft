*After she **had finished** her studies, she moved to Berlin. **When** I was a child, we lived in the country. **While** he was cooking, the phone rang.* Stories need more than one point in the past, and they need words that put events in order. This chapter gives you the past perfect, the tense for "one step further back", and the time conjunctions that link clauses: *nachdem, bevor, während, als, wenn, seit, bis, sobald*. All of them are subordinating, so the verb goes to the end, as you know from *weil* and *dass*.

## The idea

**1. The Plusquamperfekt** is the past of the Perfekt. It describes something that was **already finished** at a point in the past.

*Als ich ankam, **war** der Zug schon **abgefahren**.* When I arrived, the train **had** already **left**.

It is formed exactly like the Perfekt, but with the **past of haben or sein**: *hatte* or *war* + participle.

**2. Time conjunctions** open a subordinate clause that says *when* the main event happens. The main question is the time relation:

| Relation | Conjunction |
| --- | --- |
| before the main event | **bevor** |
| after the main event | **nachdem** |
| at the same time | **während, als, wenn** |
| from a starting point | **seit / seitdem** |
| up to an end point | **bis** |
| immediately after | **sobald** |

## Step by step

<Step n="1" title="Forming the Plusquamperfekt">

Take the Perfekt and put *haben* or *sein* into the Präteritum. The participle and the choice between *haben* and *sein* stay the same.

| Perfekt | Plusquamperfekt |
| --- | --- |
| ich **habe** gegessen | ich **hatte** gegessen |
| du **bist** gegangen | du **warst** gegangen |
| sie **hat** angerufen | sie **hatte** angerufen |
| wir **sind** angekommen | wir **waren** angekommen |

<Example de="Ich **hatte** den Film schon **gesehen**, deshalb bin ich nicht mitgegangen." en="I had already seen the film, so I did not go along." />
<Example de="Als wir ankamen, **waren** die anderen schon **gegangen**." en="When we arrived, the others had already left." />

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="nachdem: after">

*nachdem* says that the subordinate clause happened **first**. Its classic tense rule:

| Main clause | nachdem clause |
| --- | --- |
| Präteritum or Perfekt | **Plusquamperfekt** |
| Präsens | **Perfekt** |

<Example de="**Nachdem** sie das Studium **beendet hatte**, zog sie nach Berlin." en="After she had finished her studies, she moved to Berlin." />
<Example de="**Nachdem** ich **gegessen habe**, gehe ich immer spazieren." en="After I have eaten, I always go for a walk." />

The nachdem clause is always one step further back in time than the main clause.

</Step>

<Step n="3" title="bevor: before">

*bevor* says that the subordinate clause happens **later**. The tenses are normally the same in both clauses.

<Example de="**Bevor** ich zur Arbeit **gehe**, trinke ich einen Kaffee." en="Before I go to work, I drink a coffee." />
<Example de="Er rief mich an, **bevor** er **losfuhr**." en="He called me before he set off." />

</Step>

<Step n="4" title="als or wenn?">

Both translate English *when*. In the past, the choice is strict:

| | Past | Present / future |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **one** event or period | **als** | wenn |
| **repeated** events (whenever) | **wenn** (often with *immer*) | wenn |

<Example de="**Als** ich ein Kind **war**, wohnten wir auf dem Land." en="When I was a child, we lived in the country. (one period)" />
<Example de="**Als** er mich **sah**, lachte er." en="When he saw me, he laughed. (one moment)" />
<Example de="**Wenn** wir Oma **besuchten**, gab es immer Kuchen." en="Whenever we visited Grandma, there was always cake. (repeated)" />

Test: can you say *whenever*? Then *wenn*. Is it one time in the past? Then *als*.

</Step>

<Step n="5" title="während: while">

*während* means two things happen at the **same time**.

<Example de="**Während** er **kochte**, klingelte das Telefon." en="While he was cooking, the phone rang." />
<Example de="Ich höre Musik, **während** ich **arbeite**." en="I listen to music while I work." />

You know *während* as a preposition with the genitive (*während der Arbeit*). As a conjunction it is followed by a clause.

</Step>

<Step n="6" title="seit, bis, sobald">

| Conjunction | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **seit / seitdem** | since | **Seit** ich in Berlin **wohne**, fahre ich nur Fahrrad. |
| **bis** | until | Warte hier, **bis** ich **zurückkomme**. |
| **sobald** | as soon as | **Sobald** ich Zeit **habe**, rufe ich dich an. |

<Example de="**Seitdem** sie den neuen Job **hat**, ist sie viel zufriedener." en="Since she has had the new job, she has been much happier." />
<Example de="**Sobald** der Regen **aufhört**, gehen wir los." en="As soon as the rain stops, we will set off." />

As with *seit* the preposition, a situation that continues uses the **present** tense.

</Step>

<Step n="7" title="Word order: the clause first">

When the time clause comes first, it takes position 1, and the main clause starts with the verb: **verb, comma, verb**.

<Example de="Nachdem er angekommen **war**, **rief** er seine Mutter an." en="After he had arrived, he called his mother." />

</Step>

## Compared with English

<Compare en="**When** I was young … / **When(ever)** I visited her …" de="**Als** ich jung war … / **Wenn** ich sie besuchte …" note="English has one 'when' for the past. German separates one-time (als) and repeated (wenn)." />

<Compare en="After he **had eaten**, he left." de="Nachdem er **gegessen hatte**, ging er." note="Both languages use a past perfect here, and German is stricter about it after nachdem." />

<Compare en="**While** cooking, he listened to the radio." de="**Während** er kochte, hörte er Radio." note="German needs a full clause with a subject and a conjugated verb. There is no -ing shortcut." />

<Compare en="**Since** I have been living here …" de="**Seit** ich hier **wohne** …" note="German uses the present for a situation that is still going on." />

## Typical mistakes

<Mistake wrong="**Wenn** ich ein Kind war, spielte ich viel Fußball." right="**Als** ich ein Kind war, spielte ich viel Fußball." why="Childhood is one period in the past, so als." />

<Mistake wrong="Nachdem er **aß**, ging er spazieren." right="Nachdem er **gegessen hatte**, ging er spazieren." why="nachdem with a past main clause needs the Plusquamperfekt." />

<Mistake wrong="Nachdem er gegessen hatte, **er ging** spazieren." right="Nachdem er gegessen hatte, **ging er** spazieren." why="The clause takes position 1, so the verb of the main clause comes next." />

<Mistake wrong="Ich **hatte** schon nach Hause **gegangen**." right="Ich **war** schon nach Hause **gegangen**." why="gehen forms the Perfekt with sein, so the Plusquamperfekt uses war." />

<Mistake wrong="Bevor ich gehe, ich trinke einen Kaffee." right="Bevor ich gehe, **trinke ich** einen Kaffee." why="After a clause in first position: verb, then subject." />

## Summary

<Summary>

**Plusquamperfekt:** hatte / war + participle — what was already finished before another past event.

| Conjunction | Meaning | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| nachdem | after | one tense further back (Plusquamperfekt / Perfekt) |
| bevor | before | same tense |
| als | when (one time, past) | |
| wenn | when (present, future) / whenever (past) | |
| während | while | same time |
| seit(dem) | since | present for a continuing situation |
| bis | until | |
| sobald | as soon as | |

All send the verb to the end. Clause first → **verb, comma, verb**.

</Summary>
