The listening module of the Goethe-Zertifikat B1 lasts about **40 minutes** and has **four parts** with **30 questions**. Two parts are heard **twice** and two only **once**. The recordings are authentic in style: people speak at normal speed, interrupt each other and correct themselves. The good news: the questions follow the order of the recording, and you always get time to read them first.

## What this part tests

| Part | You hear | Task | Items | Heard |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Teil 1** | five short texts: announcements, voicemails, radio | for each text: one richtig / falsch + one a, b, c | 10 | twice |
| **Teil 2** | a monologue, e.g. a guided tour or an information talk | multiple choice a, b, c | 5 | **once** |
| **Teil 3** | an everyday conversation between two people | richtig / falsch | 7 | **once** |
| **Teil 4** | a radio discussion with a host and two guests | who says what? (host / guest 1 / guest 2) | 8 | twice |

## How to approach it

<Step n="1" title="Use the reading time">

Before each part you get time to read the questions. Underline the key word in each: a time, a place, a number, a reason, a name. Decide what kind of information you are listening for. In Teil 1, notice that each text has **two** questions: often one about a detail and one about the main point.

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="Teil 1: twice, so relax the first time">

Use the first listening to get the situation and the second to check the details. Typical traps are **changes of plan** and **corrections**:

<Example de="Der Zug nach Hamburg fährt heute nicht von Gleis 4, sondern von Gleis 7." />
<Example de="Wir treffen uns um acht – nein, warte, lieber um halb neun." />

</Step>

<Step n="3" title="Teil 2: once, so follow the structure">

A guided tour or information talk has a clear structure: introduction, sections, practical information at the end. The five questions follow the talk. If you miss one, **let it go** and listen for the next. Signal words help you know where you are: *zuerst, danach, jetzt kommen wir zu, zum Schluss, noch ein wichtiger Hinweis*.

</Step>

<Step n="4" title="Teil 3: once, a conversation">

Seven true/false statements about a conversation between friends, colleagues or family. Listen for **agreements and decisions**: often the speakers discuss two or three options and then choose one. The final decision is what the statement is usually about.

<Example de="– Fahren wir am Samstag? – Samstag kann ich nicht. Lieber Sonntag. – Okay, Sonntag." />

</Step>

<Step n="5" title="Teil 4: who says it?">

A host (*Moderator/in*) and two guests discuss a topic. For each statement you decide **who** said it. The statements paraphrase what is said, so listen for meaning, not for the same words. It helps to know each guest's general position after the first round: one is often more positive, the other more critical.

| Who | Typical role |
| --- | --- |
| Moderator/in | asks questions, sums up, introduces the topic |
| Gast 1 / Gast 2 | give opinions, examples, experiences |

</Step>

<Step n="6" title="General tips">

- Never leave an answer blank; guess at the end.
- Do not try to understand every word, especially in parts heard once.
- Transfer answers to the answer sheet at the end, in the time given.

</Step>

## Summary

<Summary>

| Part | Text | Heard | Listen for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 5 short texts, 2 items each | twice | changes, corrections, the main point |
| 2 | monologue | **once** | structure signals, details in order |
| 3 | conversation | **once** | decisions, agreement |
| 4 | discussion, 3 speakers | twice | meaning of each position: who says it |

**Always:** read the questions first, predict, never leave a blank.

</Summary>
