*Komm **doch** mal vorbei! Das ist **ja** toll! Was machst du **denn** hier? Das ist **halt** so.* Spoken German is full of tiny words that have no direct translation. They do not change the facts of a sentence; they change its **tone**: friendly or impatient, surprised or resigned. Native speakers use them constantly, and without them your German can sound abrupt or a bit like a textbook. In the B1 listening exam, they are often the clue to how a speaker *feels*. This chapter explains the eight most common ones.

## The idea

**Modal particles** (*Modalpartikeln*) express the speaker's **attitude**. They have four features:

1. They are **unstressed**: you say them quickly and quietly.
2. They stand in the **middle field**, usually right after the verb and pronouns: *Komm **doch** mal vorbei.*
3. They can never stand in **position 1**.
4. They can be **left out** without changing the information, only the tone.

Many of them also exist as normal words with a different meaning: *denn* (because), *ja* (yes), *schon* (already), *eben* (just now). The particle use is different.

## Step by step

<Step n="1" title="mal: softens requests">

*mal* (from *einmal*) makes a request or suggestion casual and less demanding: *just*, *for a moment*.

<Example de="Kannst du **mal** kommen?" en="Can you come here a moment?" />
<Example de="Schau **mal**!" en="Have a look!" />
<Example de="Ich muss **mal** telefonieren." en="I just need to make a phone call." />

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="doch: encouragement, contradiction, reminder">

*doch* is the most versatile particle.

| Use | Example | Feeling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| encouraging a request | Komm **doch** mit! | Why don't you come along! |
| reminding of something known | Das weißt du **doch**! | You know that! (surely) |
| mild reproach | Das habe ich dir **doch** gesagt! | I told you! |

<Example de="Setz dich **doch**!" en="Do sit down!" />
<Example de="Du kennst **doch** Anna, oder?" en="You know Anna, don't you?" />

*doch* + *mal* together is very friendly: *Ruf mich **doch mal** an!*

</Step>

<Step n="3" title="ja: surprise, or something both know">

| Use | Example | Feeling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| surprise | Das ist **ja** toll! | Wow, that's great! |
| shared knowledge | Du weißt **ja**, dass ich kein Fleisch esse. | As you know, … |

<Example de="Du bist **ja** schon da!" en="Oh, you're already here!" />
<Example de="Er wohnt **ja** jetzt in Berlin." en="He lives in Berlin now, as you know." />

</Step>

<Step n="4" title="denn: in questions">

In questions, *denn* makes the question sound **interested** and natural. Without it, many questions sound like an interrogation.

<Example de="Was machst du **denn** hier?" en="What are you doing here, then?" />
<Example de="Wie geht es dir **denn**?" en="So, how are you?" />
<Example de="Wo warst du **denn**? Ich habe dich gesucht!" en="Where were you? I was looking for you!" />

With a strong stress, it can show impatience or surprise: *Was ist **DENN** los?*

</Step>

<Step n="5" title="eben and halt: that's just how it is">

Both say that something **cannot be changed** and must be accepted. *halt* is more southern and informal; *eben* is more northern and general.

<Example de="Das ist **eben** so." en="That's just how it is." />
<Example de="Dann nehmen wir **halt** den nächsten Zug." en="Then we'll just take the next train." />

</Step>

<Step n="6" title="schon: reassurance, concession">

| Use | Example | Feeling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| reassurance | Das schaffst du **schon**! | Don't worry, you'll manage. |
| partial agreement | Das ist **schon** richtig, aber … | That's true enough, but … |

<Example de="Keine Sorge, das wird **schon**!" en="Don't worry, it'll be fine!" />

</Step>

<Step n="7" title="eigentlich: by the way, actually">

*eigentlich* in a question changes the subject gently or shows that you are curious. In a statement it means *actually, in fact*.

<Example de="Wie alt bist du **eigentlich**?" en="How old are you, by the way?" />
<Example de="**Eigentlich** wollte ich heute nicht ausgehen, aber …" en="Actually I didn't want to go out today, but …" />

</Step>

## Compared with English

<Compare en="**Why don't you** come along!" de="Komm **doch** mit!" note="English uses a whole phrase for what German does with one small word." />

<Compare en="Can you **just** help me **for a sec**?" de="Kannst du mir **mal** helfen?" note="mal often matches English 'just' or 'for a second'." />

<Compare en="**So**, what are you doing here?" de="Was machst du **denn** hier?" note="English adds 'so' or 'then', or uses intonation. German uses denn." />

<Compare en="That's **just** the way it is." de="Das ist **eben** / **halt** so." note="eben and halt express resignation." />

## Typical mistakes

<Mistake wrong="**Doch** komm mit!" right="Komm **doch** mit!" why="Particles never stand in position 1." />

<Mistake wrong="**Gib** mir das Salz! (to a friend at dinner)" right="Gib mir **mal** das Salz, bitte." why="Not wrong, but without mal and bitte it can sound abrupt." />

<Mistake wrong="Was machst du hier? (in a friendly greeting)" right="Was machst du **denn** hier?" why="Without denn the question can sound suspicious rather than surprised." />

<Mistake wrong="Du **DOCH** kennst Anna." right="Du kennst **doch** Anna." why="The particle comes after the verb and is unstressed." />

## Summary

<Summary>

| Particle | Where | Effect | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| mal | requests | casual, softer | Schau mal! |
| doch | requests, statements | encouraging; reminder; reproach | Komm doch mit! |
| ja | statements | surprise; known fact | Das ist ja toll! |
| denn | questions | interested, natural | Was ist denn los? |
| eben / halt | statements | resignation | Das ist halt so. |
| schon | statements | reassurance; partial yes | Das wird schon! |
| eigentlich | questions / statements | by the way; actually | Wie alt bist du eigentlich? |

**Position:** middle field, never position 1, unstressed.

</Summary>
