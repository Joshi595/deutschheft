Chapter 7: Conditional Wenn (Bedingungssätze)

Master conditional sentences using "wenn" (if/when) in German

📖 Introduction

Conditional sentences with "wenn" are used to express conditions and their consequences. In German, the structure differs from English because the verb position changes based on whether the "wenn" clause is at the beginning or end of the sentence.

  • Type 1: Wenn + present tense = present consequence (general rules)
  • Type 2: Wenn + subjunctive = hypothetical consequences (unlikely situations)
  • Main clause: Verb moves to position 2 after main clause subject
  • Subordinate clause: Verb moves to end after "wenn"

🎯 Why Learn This?

Conditional sentences are essential for everyday communication. They help express cause-and-effect relationships, make predictions, and discuss hypothetical situations. You'll use this structure constantly when speaking about "if...then" scenarios.

📝 Conditional Sentence Structure

German Pattern English Translation Verb Position
Wenn ich Zeit habe, gehe ich ins Kino. If I have time, I go to the movies. habe (end), gehe (position 2)
Ich gehe ins Kino, wenn ich Zeit habe. I go to the movies if I have time. gehe (position 2), habe (end)
Wenn ich Geld hätte, würde ich reisen. If I had money, I would travel. hätte (end), würde reisen (position 2)

🎯 Key Grammar Points

  • V2 Rule: In German main clauses, the conjugated verb is always in position 2. The subject can follow.
  • Subordinate Verbs: In subordinate clauses (after "wenn"), the verb moves to the end.
  • Comma Usage: Always place a comma between the "wenn" clause and the main clause.
  • Conjunctions: Other conditionals: "falls" (in case), "so lange" (as long as), "außer wenn" (except if)
