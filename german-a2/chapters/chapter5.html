Chapter 5: Adjective Endings (Nominative Case)

Master German adjective agreement in the nominative case

📖 Introduction

In German, adjectives must agree with the noun they describe. This agreement includes the adjective's ending, which changes based on:

  • Gender of the noun (Masculine, Feminine, Neuter)
  • Article type before the noun (Definite, Indefinite, None, Negative/Possessive)
  • Number (Singular or Plural)
  • Case of the noun (today: Nominative only)

🎯 Why Learn Adjective Endings?

✓ Essential for accuracy: Native speakers immediately notice wrong adjective endings

✓ Foundation for other cases: Nominative is the easiest - accusative, dative, and genitive build on this

✓ Improves listening comprehension: Recognizing adjective endings helps you understand spoken German

✓ Builds confidence: Correct usage sounds natural and professional

Key Rules for Nominative Endings:

✓ Definite Article (der, die, das): Ending is always -E (singular) or -EN (plural)

✓ Indefinite Article (ein, eine, ein): Masculine -ER, Feminine -E, Neuter -ES, Plural -EN

✓ Negative/Possessive (kein, mein, dein, sein, ihr, unser, euer): Follow same pattern as indefinite article

✓ Without Article (Bare): Adjective takes the definite article ending itself (Masculine -ER, Feminine -E, Neuter -ES, Plural -E)

💡 Memory Tip: The Pattern

Notice the pattern: The adjective ending "echoes" or matches what the article would be if it were there!

• Der gute Mann → Ein gutER Mann (echoes masculine -ER from definite "der")

• Die schöne Frau → EinE schöne Frau (echoes feminine -E from definite "die")

• Das kleine Kind → Ein kleinES Kind (echoes neuter -ES from definite "das")

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ Wrong: ein gute Mann (mixing indefinite and definite patterns)

✓ Correct: ein guter Mann (indefinite uses -ER for masculine)

❌ Wrong: Die schönen Frau (using plural ending for singular)

✓ Correct: Die schöne Frau (definite article always uses -E for singular)
