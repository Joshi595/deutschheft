Chapter 6: Adjective Endings (Accusative Case)

Master German adjective agreement in the accusative case - the direct object

📖 Introduction - Accusative Case and Adjectives

The accusative case marks the direct object of a sentence - the person or thing that is directly affected by the action. When you use adjectives with accusative nouns, the adjective endings change in important ways, especially for masculine nouns!

🔑 What is Direct Object (Accusative)?

Subject (Nominative): The person doing the action

Direct Object (Accusative): The person or thing receiving the action

  • Example: "Ich sehe den großen Mann"
  • Subject = Ich (nominative - I am seeing)
  • Direct Object = den großen Mann (accusative - the big man is being seen)

Key Question: Ask "Wen?" (Whom?) or "Was?" (What?) to find the accusative direct object.

⚠️ Important: Accusative Only Changes for Masculine!

This is crucial to remember: Only masculine adjective endings change significantly in accusative! Feminine and neuter endings stay almost the same.

Gender Nominative (Subject) Accusative (Object) Change?
Masculine Der große Mann Den großen Mann ✅ BIG CHANGE
Feminine Die große Frau Die große Frau ❌ No change
Neuter Das große Kind Das große Kind ❌ No change

🎯 Why Only Masculine Changes?

In German, the article is what really signals the case change. Notice:

  • Masculine: der (nom) → den (acc) - article CHANGES
  • Feminine: die (nom) → die (acc) - article stays the SAME
  • Neuter: das (nom) → das (acc) - article stays the SAME

When the article changes, the adjective ending changes to match. This is why masculine is different!

💡 Accusative vs Nominative Comparison

Nominative: "Der große Mann sitzt." (The big man sits. - subject)

Accusative: "Ich sehe den großen Mann." (I see the big man. - object)

Feminine Example (no change):

  • Nominative: "Die schöne Frau kommt." (The beautiful woman comes.)
  • Accusative: "Ich sehe die schöne Frau." (I see the beautiful woman.) - adjective identical!

📝 Understanding Accusative Adjective Endings

German (Nom → Acc) English Example
groß → großen (m.) big/tall Ich kaufe einen großen Laptop
schön → schöne (f.) beautiful Ich habe eine schöne Katze
klein → kleine (n.) small Ich sehe das kleine Kind
alt → alten (m.) old Wir fahren einen alten Auto
neu → neuen (m.) new Ich kaufe einen neuen Hut
schnell → schnellen (m.) fast Er fährt einen schnellen Wagen
intelligent → intelligenten (m.) intelligent Ich sehe einen intelligenten Jungen
freundlich → freundlichen (m.) friendly Ich kenne einen freundlichen Mann

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ Wrong: Ich sehe den große Mann (missing -EN ending)

✓ Correct: Ich sehe den großen Mann (accusative masculine requires -EN)

❌ Wrong: Ich kaufe ein neuens Auto (adding extra -s to neuter)

✓ Correct: Ich kaufe ein neues Auto (neuter accusative stays -ES)
