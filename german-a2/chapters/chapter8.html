Chapter 8: Dative Case - Adjective Endings

Master dative case adjective agreement - the indirect object case

📖 Introduction

The dative case (Dativ) is the indirect object case in German. It answers the question "to whom?" or "for whom?" and is used with certain verbs and prepositions. Like nominative and accusative, dative adjective endings follow specific patterns based on the article, gender, and whether the adjective is preceded by a definite or indefinite article.

  • Definite Articles (der, die, das): Dative always uses -EN ending for all genders
  • Indefinite Articles (ein, eine, ein): Dative uses -EN for masculine and neuter, -EN for feminine
  • Plural (die): Dative uses -EN for all plural nouns
  • Dative Prepositions: mit, zu, von, seit, nach, aus, bei, außer (always trigger dative)

🎯 Why Learn This?

The dative case is essential for expressing indirect objects, describing who receives something, and using many common German prepositions. Mastering dative adjective endings completes your understanding of all three main German cases and will significantly improve your ability to construct correct, natural-sounding sentences.

📝 Dative Adjective Endings

Article Type Masculine Feminine Neuter Example
Definite (dem/der) -EN -EN -EN dem großen Mann
Indefinite (einem/einer) -EN -EN -EN einem großen Mann
Possessive (meinem/meiner) -EN -EN -EN meinem großen Hund
No Article (Plural) -EN (for all genders) -EN großen Häusern

🎯 Key Pattern

In the dative case, ALL adjectives end in -EN regardless of gender or article type! This is different from nominative and accusative. This simplification makes dative endings actually easier to remember than the other cases.
